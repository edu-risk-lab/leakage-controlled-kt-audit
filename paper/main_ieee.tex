\begin{abstract}
Knowledge tracing (KT) models increasingly use concept graphs, but
constructing such graphs from learner interaction logs can introduce
leakage when edges are inferred before train/test splitting. We present
a leakage-controlled protocol for \KC{} graph construction and auditing
in KT. The protocol enforces learner-based temporal splits, train-only
edge inference, DAG validation with cycle pruning, cold-start KC
stratification, and a DAG Disruption Rate (\DDR{}) diagnostic for graph
augmentations. We instantiate the protocol on Junyi Academy,
ASSISTments~2012, and XES3G5M, plus two synthetic sanity logs
(Synthetic~C2/C5) shipped with the repository preprocessing scripts. The audit produces acyclic train-only
prerequisite graphs on all benchmarks; the \DDR{} sweep separates four
label-agnostic graph augmentation families into four distinct
structural regimes: attribute masking leaves prerequisite structure
unchanged, edge dropping disrupts it approximately linearly,
random-walk subgraph sampling preserves connectivity and disrupts it
sub-linearly, and node dropping disrupts it the most through
incident-edge cascades. Ground-truth cross-validation on Junyi (the
only benchmark in our study that ships with an expert prerequisite
annotation) shows that train-only behavioural inference and expert
curation are highly correlated at the \emph{node} level (Jaccard
${\approx}0.63$) but only weakly correlated at the \emph{edge} level
(precision/recall F1 ${\approx}0.18$ at $K\!=\!|\text{expert}|$), with
low direction agreement (${\approx}0.26$); we interpret this as
evidence that the two sources capture related but distinct aspects of
prerequisite structure. Under leakage-controlled evaluation with pyKT checkpoints, GKT does not
consistently outperform \textit{simpleKT}/AKT on ASSISTments or XES3G5M
(GKT trails by ${\approx}0.008$ and ${\approx}0.039$ AUC respectively in
the released fold~0 run); this observation, surfaced by the protocol
rather than by model design alone, motivates broader adoption of
leakage-controlled evaluation when comparing graph-augmented and
sequence-only KT. Direct leakage metrics (\textsc{ECR}\textsubscript{flag}${=}0$ on
every corpus) and a train-only versus full-log ablation show headline
metric shifts below three decimals on public benchmarks. The
cold-start diagnostic exposes per-stratum behaviour that aggregate AUC
can hide (e.g.\ Junyi hot vs.\ warm strata for \textit{simpleKT}). The paper is positioned as a reproducible protocol/resource
for graph-enhanced KT, not as a new KT model.

\keywords{Knowledge tracing \and Concept graphs \and Leakage control \and
DAG audit \and Ground-truth cross-validation \and Cold-start diagnostics \and
Reproducibility}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Knowledge tracing (KT) estimates a learner's latent mastery of
\KCs{} from interaction history, supporting adaptive practice,
intelligent tutoring, and curriculum recommendation. The field has
progressed from Bayesian Knowledge Tracing~\cite{corbett1995bkt} to
deep sequential models~\cite{piech2015dkt,zhang2017dkvmn,ghosh2020akt,
liu2023simplekt,pandey2019rkt,shen2021saint} and graph-enhanced models
that propagate information over relations between
\KCs{}~\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt,yang2021gikt,dygkt2024,
dgekt2024,wang2022grapkt,chen2023hgkt}. These graph-based models rely on
auxiliary concept graphs, usually encoding prerequisite or similarity
relations.

\paragraph{Problem setting.}
Let $\mathcal{D}=\{(u,t,q,c,y)\}$ be a KT interaction log with learner
$u$, timestamp $t$, item $q$, tagged \KC{} $c$, and correctness label
$y\in\{0,1\}$. A learner-based temporal split
$\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}},\mathcal{F}_{\text{val}},
\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}})$ assigns each learner to exactly one fold.
For evaluation fold $f$, the protocol must construct a fold-specific
multi-relational concept graph
$G_f=(\mathcal{C},\Epre^f\cup\Esim^f)$ using \emph{only}
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$, then train a KT model on
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$ and score predictions on
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^f$. The central question is whether reported
graph-augmented KT gains remain valid when $G_f$ is audited for
leakage, acyclicity, and cold-start coverage.

workflow~(a), prerequisite or similarity edges may be supported by
validation/test interactions; the KT module then appears to generalise
while part of the structural input already encodes future behaviour.
Workflow~(b) closes this channel by making graph construction a
first-class, fold-conditioned step.

\paragraph{Real-world significance.}
Leakage-controlled concept graphs matter wherever KT systems drive
high-stakes decisions. Intelligent tutoring platforms use mastery
estimates to schedule practice and gate advanced modules; incorrect
graphs can push learners into topics for which prerequisites were never
mastered~\cite{heffernan2006assist,junyi_dataset}. Curriculum analytics
teams compare cohorts across terms; graphs built from pooled logs can
confound term effects with structural shortcuts. MOOC and corporate
training pipelines increasingly deploy graph-augmented KT
\cite{choi2020ednet,xes3g5m2023}; without fold-specific audits,
reported AUC improvements are difficult to attribute to modelling
versus preprocessing. Our protocol targets these deployment settings by
making graph provenance, DAG validity, and cold-start strata explicit
reporting artefacts rather than hidden preprocessing choices.

A key risk is that concept graphs are often inferred from the same
interaction logs used to train and evaluate KT models. If graph edges
are computed from the full log before train/test splitting, held-out
information can leak back into the model through the graph. This leakage
may occur even when the KT model itself is trained only on the training
fold. Benchmarking tools such as \texttt{pyKT}~\cite{liu2022pykt} have
highlighted the importance of preprocessing and split discipline, but a
fold-specific audit protocol for KC graph construction remains
under-specified.

A second risk arises when generic graph augmentations are imported into
KT. Node dropping, edge dropping, attribute masking, and subgraph
sampling were originally designed for general-purpose graphs
\cite{you2020graphcl,zhu2021gca,xia2022simgrace,thakoor2021bgrl,
velickovic2018dgi,hassani2020mvgrl}. However, prerequisite graphs are
directional and educationally meaningful: removing a prerequisite edge
may remove a pedagogical constraint. The degree to which label-agnostic
graph augmentations disturb educational prerequisite DAGs has not been
systematically measured in KT.

A third issue is the informal use of ``cold-start'' in KT. In this
paper, we define cold-start at the KC level, based on the number of
train-fold interactions associated with each KC, in contrast to user- or
item-level cold-start in recommender systems~\cite{schein2002coldstart}.
This separates cold-start KC behaviour from other cold-start settings.

We address these issues at the level of protocol rather than model. We
do not propose a new KT architecture or claim state-of-the-art
performance. Instead, we release a leakage-controlled graph construction
and audit protocol, a structural augmentation diagnostic, a ground-truth
cross-validation procedure (instantiated on Junyi), and a cold-start KC
report. The protocol is instantiated on Junyi
Academy~\cite{junyi_dataset,chang2015junyi}, ASSISTments~2012, and
XES3G5M~\cite{xes3g5m2023}.

\paragraph{Novelty.}
Relative to prior KT surveys and benchmarks
\cite{abdelrahman2023kt_survey,liu2022pykt}, our contribution is not a
new predictive backbone but a \emph{reproducible audit layer} around
concept-graph construction. Compared with graph-KT papers that treat
$G$ as a fixed input~\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt,yang2021gikt,
dygkt2024,dgekt2024}, we (i)~formalise fold-specific graph construction
with explicit leakage diagnostics, (ii)~introduce \DDR{} to quantify how
generic augmentations disrupt prerequisite DAGs, (iii)~operationalise
KC-level cold-start reporting, and (iv)~ground behavioural prerequisite
inference against expert curation where available. Compared with
prerequisite-learning surveys~\cite{bai2025survey,lin2016moocprereq,
pan2020concept}, we do not propose a new inference rule; instead we
audit a fixed train-only rule and quantify disagreement with expert
graphs. Compared with graph contrastive KT
\cite{lee2022cl4kt,song2022biclkt,wan2021contrastive}, we separate
\emph{structural} disruption of educational DAGs from downstream AUC,
which can mask graph-quality issues.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1.} A train-only protocol for constructing and auditing
  multi-relational KC graphs
  $G=(\mathcal{C}, \Epre \cup \Esim)$ for KT, including split checks,
  edge provenance, and DAG validation with cycle pruning.
  \item \textbf{C2.} The \DDR{} metric, which quantifies how generic
  graph augmentations disrupt prerequisite assumptions on educational
  DAGs, instantiated with four formally defined augmentation operators.
  \item \textbf{C3.} A cold-start KC diagnostic with explicit
  frequency-based strata and reproducible scripts on three KT
  benchmarks.
  \item \textbf{C4.} A ground-truth cross-validation procedure that
  quantifies, on Junyi Academy, how train-only behavioural inference of
  $\Epre$ agrees with the expert prerequisite annotation of
  Chang~et~al.~\cite{junyi_dataset}, using edge-level, direction-level,
  and reachability-level metrics across a sweep of top-$K$ cutoffs.
  \item \textbf{C5.} An empirical observation, surfaced by the protocol
  on ASSISTments and XES3G5M pyKT checkpoints, that GKT does not
  consistently outperform \textit{simpleKT}/AKT once train-only graph
  construction is enforced, together with a train-only versus full-log
  ablation and direct leakage metrics. We discuss three
  non-mutually-exclusive hypotheses for this pattern and frame it as a
  motivation for leakage-controlled evaluation as a benchmarking
  precondition rather than as a refutation of graph-augmented KT.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Scope.}
Tables~\ref{tab:baseline-junyi}--\ref{tab:baseline-synthetic-c5} report diagnostic
pyKT checkpoints on fold~0 with train-only exported graphs (valid+test

We do not claim that the protocol eliminates
every possible form of leakage, nor do we make claims about real-world
learning outcomes.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Knowledge Tracing and Benchmark Discipline}
\label{sec:related-kt}
Early KT relied on probabilistic latent-state models such as Bayesian
Knowledge Tracing (BKT)~\cite{corbett1995bkt} and extensions with
individualised parameters~\cite{yudelson2013pfa,khajah2016das3h}.
Deep KT introduced recurrent sequence models (DKT)~\cite{piech2015dkt},
memory-augmented variants (DKVMN)~\cite{zhang2017dkvmn}, and
attention-based architectures (AKT, simpleKT)~\cite{ghosh2020akt,
liu2023simplekt,chen2022dimkt,wang2023corekt}. Self-attentive and
convolutional designs (RKT, SAINT)~\cite{pandey2019rkt,shen2021saint}
further improved sequence modelling. Large-scale surveys
\cite{abdelrahman2023kt_survey} document rapid architectural progress
but also heterogeneous preprocessing. The \texttt{pyKT} library
\cite{liu2022pykt} standardises training loops and metrics across
datasets including EdNet~\cite{choi2020ednet} and
XES3G5M~\cite{xes3g5m2023}.

\textbf{Limitations of this line for our setting.}
Most KT benchmarks treat the interaction log as the sole input and
optimise predictive metrics (AUC, ACC, NLL) without auditing auxiliary
structure. Preprocessing choices---sequence length, Q-matrix
construction, split type---can shift rankings by several points
\cite{liu2022pykt,abdelrahman2023kt_survey}, yet graph provenance is
rarely logged. Aggregate metrics also hide per-\KC{} behaviour: a model
can appear strong overall while failing on rare skills. Our protocol
complements benchmark libraries by standardising fold-specific concept
graphs and KC-level cold-start reporting rather than proposing another
sequence backbone.

\subsection{Graph-based Knowledge Tracing and Prerequisite Structure}
\label{sec:related-graphkt}
GKT~\cite{nakagawa2019gkt} first propagated mastery signals over a
fixed KC graph with graph neural networks. SKT~\cite{tong2020skt}
separates prerequisite and similarity relations; GIKT~\cite{yang2021gikt}
adds question--KC structure. Follow-ups include hierarchical and
interaction-rich designs (HGKT, GrapKT, IEKT)~\cite{chen2023hgkt,
wang2022grapkt,liu2021iekt}, dynamic graphs (DyGKT)~\cite{dygkt2024},
dual-graph ensembles (DGEKT)~\cite{dgekt2024}, and context-aware graph
extensions~\cite{xiong2023gracekt,huang2022hawkt}. In parallel,
prerequisite-learning research infers directed relations from MOOC logs,
content, or expert crowdsourcing~\cite{lin2016moocprereq,pan2020concept,
chen2020dlprereq,laguna2018prereq,bai2025survey,chang2015junyi}.

\textbf{Limitations of this line for our setting.}
Graph-KT papers typically \emph{assume} a concept graph is given and
optimise predictive loss, leaving graph construction outside the
experimental factorisation. When graphs are inferred from the same log
used for evaluation, held-out transitions can influence edges---a
channel distinct from label leakage in the KT loss. Prerequisite-learning
work optimises edge quality against expert or content signals but seldom
couples inference to KT splits or reports DAG audit trails. Expert
annotations (e.g.\ Junyi~\cite{chang2015junyi,junyi_dataset}) and
behavioural inference capture related but non-identical structure; treating
one as a proxy for the other is empirically unsupported without
cross-validation. Our protocol keeps inference fixed and auditable, then
quantifies disagreement with expert graphs where available.

\subsection{Graph Augmentation, Contrastive KT, and Reproducible Resources}
\label{sec:related-aug}
General graph self-supervised learning popularised label-agnostic
augmentations: GraphCL~\cite{you2020graphcl}, GCA~\cite{zhu2021gca},
SimGRACE~\cite{xia2022simgrace}, BGRL~\cite{thakoor2021bgrl}, DGI
\cite{velickovic2018dgi}, and multi-view contrastive methods
\cite{hassani2020mvgrl,zhu2020graphtsa,feng2022graphmvp}. GNN encoders
(GCN, GraphSAGE)~\cite{kipf2017gcn,hamilton2017graphsage} underpin many
KT graph modules. These augmentations have been imported into contrastive
KT (CL4KT, Bi-CLKT, DECKT)~\cite{lee2022cl4kt,song2022biclkt,deckt2025,
wan2021contrastive}, usually optimising representation quality on
interaction sequences or bipartite graphs.

\textbf{Limitations of this line for our setting.}
Augmentation papers optimise invariance objectives on general graphs;
educational prerequisite DAGs impose directionality and pedagogical
interpretability that random edge or node drops can violate. Empirical
studies on general graphs report task-dependent augmentation rankings
\cite{zhu2020graphtsa}, but \DDR{} effects on prerequisite DAGs are not
part of standard KT reporting. Contrastive KT work rarely reports whether
augmentations preserve acyclicity or alter reachability between \KCs{}.
Resource-oriented reproducibility in KT has focused on code and splits
\cite{liu2022pykt} rather than on graph audit logs. We release provenance
artefacts, \DDR{} sweeps, and cold-start tables so graph-enhanced KT
studies can separate structural disruption from predictive gains.

\textbf{Positioning.}
Our work is orthogonal to inventing new KT architectures or prerequisite
inference rules. It supplies an audit layer---leakage diagnostics, DAG
pruning logs, \DDR{}, ground-truth cross-validation, and cold-start
strata---that existing graph-KT and contrastive-KT papers typically omit.

\section{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
Let $\mathcal{D}$ be a KT interaction log, $\mathcal{C}$ the set of
\KCs{}, and $\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}},
\mathcal{F}_{\text{val}},\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}})$ a learner-based
temporal split. The protocol constructs a fold-specific graph
$G_f=(\mathcal{C},\Epre^f\cup\Esim^f)$ using only
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$.

Figure~\ref{fig:framework} summarises module dependencies. Split
discipline is a hard precondition: without learner-disjoint folds,
downstream diagnostics are not interpretable. Graph construction and DAG
audit are coupled: train-only inference can introduce cycles, so pruning
must be logged before any graph-augmented KT run. Leakage metrics,
\DDR{}, cold-start strata, and ground-truth cross-validation are
\emph{parallel} audits---they do not retrain models---which keeps the
protocol lightweight but means it cannot certify every possible leakage
path (e.g.\ hidden features in proprietary pipelines).

\subsection{Formal Problem Statement and Leakage Audit}
\label{sec:formal-problem}
For each evaluation fold $f$, the protocol seeks a graph
$G_f=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}_f)$ with $\mathcal{E}_f=\Epre^f\cup\Esim^f$
that is \emph{feasible} with respect to train-only evidence and
structural constraints, then verifies low direct leakage on scalar
diagnostics. Let $\mathcal{I}_{\text{tr}}^f$ denote train-fold
interactions and $\mathcal{H}_f=\mathcal{F}_{\text{val}}^f\cup
\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^f$ held-out interactions.

\paragraph{Decision variables.}
Edge sets $\Epre^f\subseteq\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}$ and
$\Esim^f\subseteq\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}$, edge weights
$w_{ij}\ge 0$, and pruning decisions on cyclic subsets of $\Epre^f$.

\paragraph{Objective.}
The construction stage maximises retained pedagogical structure subject
to sparsity and acyclicity:
\begin{equation}
\max_{\Epre^f,\Esim^f}\;
\sum_{(i,j)\in\Epre^f} w_{ij}
\;+\;
\lambda \sum_{(i,j)\in\Esim^f} \mathrm{sim}(c_i,c_j)
\label{eq:graph-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{sim}$ is Jaccard or PMI (Section~\ref{sec:graph-sim-theory})
and $\lambda\ge 0$ trades prerequisite mass against similarity mass (in
practice $\lambda{=}0$ because the two edge types are inferred in
separate pipelines).

\paragraph{Constraints.}
\begin{align}
\text{(C1)} \;& \forall e\in\mathcal{E}_f,\;
  S_{\mathrm{hi}}(e)=\emptyset;
  \label{eq:c1}\\
\text{(C2)} \;& \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f
  = \emptyset,\notag\\
&\; \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f
  = \emptyset,\notag\\
&\; \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f
  = \emptyset;
  \label{eq:c2}\\
\text{(C3)} \;& \Epre^f \text{ is acyclic after pruning};
  \label{eq:c3}\\
\text{(C4)} \;& |\Epre^f|\le K,\;
  |\mathrm{Out}_{\Epre^f}(i)|\le k;\notag\\
&\; \mathrm{sim}(c_i,c_j)\ge\tau \ \forall (i,j)\in\Esim^f;
  \label{eq:c4}\\
\text{(C5)} \;& \mathrm{TBVR}_f = 0;\notag\\
&\;\text{(no post-cutoff train transitions in edge evidence).}
  \label{eq:c5}
\end{align}
C1--C2 block cross-fold and cross-learner contamination; C3--C4 encode
the DAG audit and top-$K$/top-$k$ filters of Section~\ref{sec:graph};
C5 is checked by Eq.~\eqref{eq:tbvr}.

\paragraph{Audit functionals (diagnostics, not training losses).}
Because C1 cannot always be verified from AUC alone, the protocol
reports four scalar functionals on $G_f$:
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ecr-flag}),
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ecr-overlap}),
$\mathrm{EOC}_f$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:eoc}), and
$\mathrm{TBVR}_f$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:tbvr}).
Ideal hygiene: $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$,
$\mathrm{TBVR}_f{=}0$, modest $\mathrm{EOC}_f$; large
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$ may still occur under honest splits
when curricula repeat across disjoint learners.

\subsection{Leakage Control}
\label{sec:leakage}
We audit five leakage classes---\emph{structural}, \emph{edge}, \emph{target},
\emph{temporal}, and \emph{cold-start neighbourhood}---summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy}. Structural leakage occurs when the same
learner contributes interactions to more than one of train, validation, or test under a fold, coupling splits at the identifier level. Edge leakage occurs when similarity or
prerequisite edges receive statistical support from interactions
outside the train fold of that evaluation split. Target leakage occurs
when correctness labels, outcome-conditioned statistics, or other
supervision-derived artefacts influence graph construction or features
after pooling across splits (including fitting item--KC mappings on
full data instead of expert tags or train-only evidence). Temporal
leakage occurs when interactions at or after the learner-specific split
boundary contribute to edge inference or to graph-derived inputs at
evaluation time. Cold-start neighbourhood leakage is the special case
where edges touching rare \KCs{} are justified primarily by held-out or
future-split interactions, so tail-\KC{} structure mirrors aggregate
performance rather than train-plausible evidence.

Each inferred edge is logged with its fold of origin, timestamp range,
relation type, and train-only flag; severe violations halt the pipeline.
In addition, we report four scalar \emph{direct} leakage diagnostics that do
not reduce to predictive accuracy. Let $\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f$,
$\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f$, and $\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f$ denote the
learner-id sets in train, validation, and test under fold $f$. The learner overlap flag is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f := \mathbb{1}\big[
(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f) \cup
(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f) \cup
(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f)
\neq \emptyset
\big]\,.
\label{eq:ecr-flag}
\end{equation}
When folds are learner-disjoint, $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f=0$ for every fold---the regime enforced below.

Let $\mathcal{E}_f$ denote the graph edge set (prerequisite and similarity) consumed when evaluating fold
$f$, and let $\mathcal{H}_f = \mathcal{F}_{\text{val}}^{f} \cup
\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^{f}$ denote held-out interactions under that
split. For an edge $e=(i,j)$, let $S_{\text{tr}}(e)$ be the multiset of
train-fold interaction tokens counted toward $e$'s evidence, and let
$S_{\text{hi}}(e)$ be the analogous multiset restricted to
$\mathcal{H}_f$. Define the overlap indicator
$\chi_{\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}}(e)=\mathbb{1}[|S_{\text{hi}}(e)|>0]$.
The held-out pattern overlap rate mirrors an edge-level importance scan (cf.\ feature importance plots): it asks whether each retained edge
aligns with \emph{some} mass observable among held-out interactions---possibly contributed by disjoint learners---rather than encoding learner pooling across folds,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}_f|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_f}
\chi_{\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}}(e)\,.
\label{eq:ecr-overlap}
\end{equation}
For Edge--Outcome Correlation, stack normalized edge weights into
$\mathbf{w}_f\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{E}_f|}$ (for ties, use the order
exported by the audit log). Let $\mathbf{y}_f\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{E}_f|}$
carry test-fold outcome summaries aligned to each edge---for example,
endpoint averages of correctness on $\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^{f}$ or
their logits---computed \emph{after} graph construction (labels are used
only for this diagnostic, not for inferring $\mathcal{E}_f$).
Let $R_f\in[-1,1]^{2\times 2}$ be the Pearson correlation matrix between
the two scalar sequences $(w,y)$ pooled over edges with defined variance.
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{EOC}_f \;=\; \|R_f\|_F\,,
\label{eq:eoc}
\end{equation}
For binary correctness summaries this matches the spirit of a $2\times 2$
correlation-matrix summary relating structural edge mass to outcome mass;
if $\mathrm{Corr}(\mathbf{w}_f,\mathbf{y}_f)=\rho$, then $R_f$ has unit
diagonal and off-diagonals $\rho$, hence
$\mathrm{EOC}_f=\sqrt{2+2\rho^2}$.

Let $t_{u,i}$ be the timestamp of interaction $i$ for learner $u$ and
let $\tau_u$ be $u$'s temporal split cutoff for fold $f$. Write
$\mathcal{I}_{u,f}^{>}=\{\, i : t_{u,i}>\tau_u \,\}$ for post-boundary
interactions and $\mathcal{V}_{u,f}$ for the subset of those tokens that
incorrectly appear in any multiset counted toward $\mathcal{E}_f$ or in
graph-derived features at inference for fold $f$. The Temporal
Boundary Violation Rate is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TBVR}_f \;=\;
\frac{\sum_u |\mathcal{V}_{u,f}|}{\sum_u |\mathcal{I}_{u,f}^{>}|}\,.
\label{eq:tbvr}
\end{equation}
Under learner-disjoint splits, no learner spans train and held-out folds;
we therefore instantiate $\tau_u$ \emph{within}
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$ by taking $\tau_u$ at the index that
separates the earliest $\lfloor r n_u\rfloor$ chronologically ordered
train interactions from the tail of learner $u$, where $r$ matches the
protocol train ratio (here $r{=}0.7$) and $n_u$ is $u$'s train-fold length.
The multiset checked is the transition evidence underlying the retained
prerequisite edges $\Epre^f$.
Ideal structural hygiene yields $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$, temporal hygiene yields $\mathrm{TBVR}_f{=}0$, and we additionally desire a modest $\mathrm{EOC}_f$ relative to permuted-reference baselines.
The overlap statistic $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$ \emph{need not} vanish under honest splits because ubiquitous curricula reproduce frequent prerequisite transitions across disjoint learners; large $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$ therefore must not be read as $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}1$ or as a train-only provenance violation without consulting the audit trail.

\paragraph{Why AUC/ACC are insufficient.}
High test AUC or accuracy does \emph{not} certify absence of leakage:
held-out information can be \emph{baked into} the graph (or static
artefacts) before optimisation, so the model merely exploits a
consistent shortcut rather than measuring generalisation under honest
provenance. Conversely, scrubbing leakage can lower headline metrics
without making the model worse in substance---it removes illegitimate
signals. Direct diagnostics such as $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f$, $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$, $\mathrm{EOC}_f$,
and $\mathrm{TBVR}_f$ therefore complement discrimination scores by
testing whether the structural inputs themselves remain statistically
independent of the wrong folds or outcomes.

\subsection{Graph Representation for Knowledge Tracing}
\label{sec:graph-repr}
We represent each fold's knowledge structure as a multi-relational
directed graph $G_f=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{E}_f)$ where
nodes $c\in\mathcal{C}$ are \KCs{} (hashed identifiers in the released
pipeline), and edges carry relation type $r\in\mathcal{R}=\{\text{pre},
\text{sim}\}$. Prerequisite edges $(i,j)\in\Epre^f$ encode directed
precedence inferred from temporal transitions; similarity edges
$(i,j)\in\Esim^f$ encode co-assessment structure from the train-fold
Q-matrix. Each edge stores provenance fields (fold id, train-only flag,
source tag, support count, weight $w_{ij}$) exported as CSV for audit.

For graph-augmented KT baselines, $G_f$ is materialised as a row-normalised
adjacency matrix $A_f\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{C}|\times|\mathcal{C}|}$
(\path{pykt_graph_matrix.py}): prerequisite and similarity relations can be
merged or consumed separately depending on the backbone (GKT uses a single
KC graph; GIKT additionally uses question--KC bipartite structure from the
train fold). Node features are not learned at graph-construction time;
the protocol treats $G_f$ as a \emph{structural prior} consumed by downstream
KT optimisers. This separation mirrors GNN practice on citation or
knowledge graphs~\cite{kipf2017gcn,hamilton2017graphsage} but with
education-specific relation semantics and fold conditioning.

Technology stack: graph construction is NumPy/pandas/NetworkX; cycle
detection uses Johnson's algorithm~\cite{johnson1975finding}; KT training
optionally delegates to \texttt{pyKT}~\cite{liu2022pykt} with exported
NPZ adjacency. All artefacts are versioned per dataset and fold under
\path{data/graphs/<dataset>/fold_<f>/}.

\subsection{Train-only Graph Construction}
\label{sec:graph}
\paragraph{Prerequisite edges $\Epre$.}
For uniformity across heterogeneous benchmarks, we infer $\Epre$ from
train-only temporal transitions on \emph{all} three datasets,
independently of whether an external prerequisite source is also
available for that dataset. This deliberate choice decouples the audit
pipeline from dataset-specific quality of expert annotations and
ensures that every reported edge originates from a single, auditable
inference rule. When an expert annotation is available for a benchmark
(here, Junyi Academy), the protocol additionally performs ground-truth
cross-validation as a separate audit step (Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}).

Concretely, for each train-fold interaction sequence we extract
consecutive KC transitions $c_{t} \rightarrow c_{t+1}$ with
$c_{t} \neq c_{t+1}$, count their support $s(c_i, c_j)$, and retain
candidate edges in three filtering stages:
(i) a support-quantile filter that drops transitions below the
$q$-th percentile (default $q = 0.95$);
(ii) a per-source top-$k$ cap (default $k = 5$) that retains, for each
source KC, only the $k$ destination \KCs{} with highest support; and
(iii) a global top-$K$ cap on the number of retained candidate edges
(default $K = 5{,}000$). Each retained edge carries the normalized
support weight $w_{ij} = s(c_i, c_j) / s_{\max}$ used by the cycle
pruning step in Section~\ref{sec:dag-audit}. This candidate edge set
is then passed unchanged to the DAG audit; cycle pruning operates only
on the candidates returned by stages (i)--(iii).

\paragraph{Similarity edges $\Esim$ and theoretical properties.}
\label{sec:graph-sim-theory}
We compute $\Esim$ from a train-only Q-matrix using either Jaccard or
PMI co-occurrence at the item level (default Jaccard with threshold
$\tau{=}0.10$). Let $I(c)\subseteq\mathcal{Q}$ be the train-fold item
set tagged with concept $c$, with $n=|\mathcal{Q}|$ items:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{sim}_J(c_i,c_j) &=
  \frac{|I(c_i)\cap I(c_j)|}{|I(c_i)\cup I(c_j)|},
  \label{eq:sim-jaccard}\\
\mathrm{sim}_{\mathrm{PMI}}(c_i,c_j) &=
  \log \frac{p(c_i,c_j)}{p(c_i)\,p(c_j)},
  \label{eq:sim-pmi}\notag\\
&\quad p(c)=\tfrac{|I(c)|}{n},\notag\\
&\quad p(c_i,c_j)=\tfrac{|I(c_i)\cap I(c_j)|}{n}.
\end{align}
\textbf{Properties.}
(i)~\emph{Boundedness:} $0\le \mathrm{sim}_J\le 1$; edges are retained
iff $\mathrm{sim}_J\ge\tau$.
(ii)~\emph{Symmetry:} both scores are symmetric in $(c_i,c_j)$; we store
directed permutations $(i,j)$ and $(j,i)$ for pipeline uniformity.
(iii)~\emph{Empty overlap:} if items are single-skill,
$|I(c_i)\cap I(c_j)|=0$ for $i\neq j$ and both metrics yield no edges---the
ASSISTments~2012 case in Section~\ref{sec:exp-coldstart}.
(iv)~\emph{PMI dominance:} when $|I(c_i)\cap I(c_j)|$ is small but
non-zero, PMI can rank rare co-tagged pairs above Jaccard because it
reweights by marginal frequencies; threshold $\tau$ on raw PMI therefore
requires dataset-specific calibration unlike Jaccard's scale-free
$[0,1]$ range.
(v)~\emph{Monotonicity in co-tagging:} holding marginals fixed,
increasing overlap never decreases $\mathrm{sim}_J$; PMI can decrease if
marginals grow faster than co-occurrence.

As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:datasets}, this similarity rule is
sensitive to the multi-skill density of the underlying Q-matrix.

\paragraph{Subgraph construction for KT consumption.}
\label{sec:subgraph}
Beyond full $G_f$, some KT pipelines and augmentation operators consume
\emph{induced subgraphs}. Given visited node set $S\subseteq\mathcal{C}$,
the induced prerequisite subgraph is
$G_f[S]=(S,\Epre^f\cap(S\times S))$.
The random-walk subgraph operator in Section~\ref{sec:ddr} samples $S$
with $|S|\ge\lceil(1-p)|\mathcal{C}|\rceil$ by a walk on the undirected
skeleton of $\Epre^f$, preserving internal edges while dropping boundary
incident edges---hence sub-linear \DDR{} growth.
For KT matrix export, we optionally restrict to the largest weakly
connected component of $\Epre^f$ when isolates would otherwise yield zero
row mass; this is logged but does not change edge provenance.
The train-only versus full-log ablation (Section~\ref{sec:exp-graph-ablation})
contrasts two global graphs $G^{\text{train}}$ and $G^{\text{full}}$ built
from $\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$ versus the entire preprocessed log;
subgraph operators then act on whichever parent graph is supplied.

All graph artefacts are materialised per dataset and fold so that
downstream models cannot accidentally consume full-data edges.
\subsection{DAG Audit}
\label{sec:dag-audit}
For each fold-specific prerequisite edge set, we check whether $\Epre$
is acyclic. If cycles are detected, we iteratively remove the
lowest-confidence edge in a detected cycle and re-run the check until a
topological ordering exists. Cycle detection uses a bounded simple-cycle enumeration procedure based on
Johnson's algorithm for enumerating elementary circuits in directed graphs \cite{johnson1975finding}, capped at 100 representative cycles per detection round. We use this cap because the number of elementary cycles can grow rapidly with graph density; exhaustive enumeration is unnecessary for our
audit, since the pruning loop only requires representative cycle witnesses. The detector cap does not affect
correctness: the pruning loop terminates only when no further cycle can
be detected, so the final retained graph is acyclic. Each removed edge
is written to
\path{results/reports/<dataset>_dag_pruning_log.csv} with its source,
target, confidence score, and pruning reason. The audit reports node
count, raw edge count, pruned edge count, retained edge count, root
count, leaf count, and final topological-sort status. The claim is not
that the retained DAG is the unique correct prerequisite hierarchy, but
that the pruning rule and edge provenance are explicit and reproducible.
\subsection{DAG Disruption Rate and Augmentation Operators}
\label{sec:ddr}
To quantify the structural effect of graph augmentations on prerequisite
DAGs, we define the DAG Disruption Rate (\DDR{}). Let $A$ be an
augmentation operator and $A(\Epre,p)$ the edge set obtained at
perturbation strength $p$. Let $\mathrm{pre}(A(\Epre,p))\subseteq\Epre$
be the original prerequisite edges that remain with their original
direction, and let $\mathrm{rev}(A(\Epre,p))$ be the edges that appear
with the opposite direction. We define
\begin{equation}
\DDR(A,p)=
\frac{|\Epre\setminus \mathrm{pre}(A(\Epre,p))|
      +|\mathrm{rev}(A(\Epre,p))|}
     {|\Epre|}.
\label{eq:ddr}
\end{equation}
We evaluate four label-agnostic augmentation families. Their
implementations follow GraphCL~\cite{you2020graphcl} and are applied to
the retained acyclic $\Epre$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Attribute masking.} Random subset of edge attributes
  is masked (set to a neutral value). The edge set itself is
  unchanged, so attribute masking induces $\DDR=0$ by construction.
  \item \textbf{Edge dropping.} Each prerequisite edge is independently
  removed with probability $p$. Expected $\DDR$ is approximately $p$,
  modulo finite-sample variation.
  \item \textbf{Subgraph sampling (random walk).} Starting from a
  uniformly random seed node, a random walk over the (undirected)
  prerequisite graph extends the visited node set until it reaches
  $\lceil(1-p)|\mathcal{C}|\rceil$ nodes; if the walk reaches a dead
  end before the target size, it restarts from a random unvisited
  node. The induced edge subgraph on the visited nodes is returned.
  Connectivity within the visited set is preserved, so disruption
  comes only from edges incident to non-visited nodes.
  \item \textbf{Node dropping.} A random subset of $\lceil p|\mathcal{C}|\rceil$
  nodes is removed, together with all their incident edges. Disruption
  is amplified by the incident-edge cascade.
\end{itemize}
Each operator is swept over $p\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20,0.30\}$ with three
random seeds on each of three train folds ($3\times 3=9$ runs per
setting); reported means aggregate across those runs. \DDR{} is a structural diagnostic only: it measures how
much prerequisite structure is removed or reversed by an augmentation,
not whether a downstream KT model becomes better or worse.
\subsection{Cold-start KC Stratification}
\label{sec:cold-start}
We define cold-start at the KC level, distinct from user/item
cold-start in recommender systems~\cite{schein2002coldstart}. For fold
$f$, let $n_f(c)=|\{(u,t,q,c,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f\}|$ be
the number of train-fold interactions tagged with \KC{} $c$. Each KC
is assigned to one of four frequency strata:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{S}_1&:\ n_f(c)<20,\\
\mathcal{S}_2&:\ 20\le n_f(c)<100,\\
\mathcal{S}_3&:\ 100\le n_f(c)<500,\\
\mathcal{S}_4&:\ n_f(c)\ge 500.
\end{aligned}
\]
\paragraph{Technical procedure.}
(i)~\emph{Stratum assignment} is computed after the learner split and
uses only $\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$ counts (no test labels).
(ii)~\emph{Interaction tagging}: each test interaction inherits the
stratum of its \KC{} tag; interactions whose \KC{} never appears in train
are flagged \texttt{out\_of\_range} and excluded from stratum means.
(iii)~\emph{Metric aggregation}: for each model $m$ and non-empty stratum
$\mathcal{S}_k$, we compute AUC, ACC, and NLL on the subset of test
rows with $c\in\mathcal{S}_k$, using the same prediction export as the
aggregate baseline table.
(iv)~\emph{Neighbourhood leakage link}: if a rare \KC{} $c\in\mathcal{S}_1$
has similarity or prerequisite edges supported mainly by held-out mass,
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$ rises (Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy});
cold-start strata make such failures visible when aggregate AUC looks stable.
(v)~\emph{Reporting}: results are written to
\path{results/tables/cold_start_by_stratum.csv} with columns
\texttt{dataset}, \texttt{model}, \texttt{stratum}, \texttt{auc},
\texttt{acc}, \texttt{nll}, \texttt{n\_interactions}.

This prevents aggregate metrics from hiding behaviour on rare \KCs{} and
supports fair comparison between sequence-only and graph-augmented models
on tail skills where graph smoothness is often hypothesised to help.
\subsection{Ground-Truth Cross-Validation}
\label{sec:gtxv-protocol}
When a benchmark provides an expert prerequisite annotation, the
protocol performs an additional, optional audit step: comparing the
train-only inferred $\Epre$ against the expert edge set
$\Epre^{\text{expert}}$. The procedure has four stages:
(i) load the expert annotation and filter rows by a prerequisite-score
threshold $\theta$ to obtain a directed expert edge set;
(ii) align expert KC identifiers to the pipeline's hashed \texttt{kc\_id}
space via a deterministic name-to-id mapping reconstructed from the
preprocessing hash function;
(iii) sweep top-$K$ cutoffs over the inferred edge set and compute,
at each $K$: edge precision/recall/F1, direction agreement, reachability
F1 (over transitive closure), and node Jaccard;
(iv) extract qualitative examples of disagreement in three categories:
edges present only in inferred, edges present only in expert, and edges
with opposite direction.
The procedure is read-only with respect to the pipeline; no
inferred edge is added or removed based on the comparison.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}
We instantiate the protocol on three public KT benchmarks. Junyi Academy
\cite{junyi_dataset} is a K--12 mathematics benchmark; the Junyi release
includes an expert-curated prerequisite annotation at the
exercise-pair level. \emph{In the main inference branch of the protocol
we do not consume this expert DAG}: $\Epre$ for Junyi is built by the
same train-only temporal inference rule used for ASSISTments and
XES3G5M (Section~\ref{sec:graph}). The audit therefore reports inferred
$\Epre$ for all three datasets, ensuring uniform edge provenance. The
Junyi expert DAG is consumed only by the ground-truth cross-validation
sub-protocol (Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}), which is read-only with
respect to inference.

ASSISTments~2012 provides a Q-matrix but no prerequisite graph and
exercises the inferred-DAG path of the protocol. XES3G5M~\cite{xes3g5m2023}
provides hierarchical KC metadata; in the present pipeline this metadata
is parsed only at the sequence level (KC IDs are derived from
hierarchical KC labels), and prerequisite edges for XES3G5M are likewise
inferred by the train-only temporal rule. We additionally include
Synthetic~C2 (two \KCs{}) and Synthetic~C5 (five \KCs{}) as controlled
sanity corpora with known transition structure; they exercise the same
audit modules on tiny graphs (Table~\ref{tab:dag-audit}).

These results show that cycle pruning is not a cosmetic step: even
train-only inference can create enough implied directionality to
produce cycles. The audit therefore makes the pruning rule explicit and
reviewable rather than silently fixing the graph.

Figure~\ref{fig:ddr-all} and Table~\ref{tab:ddr-raw} show four
consistent regimes across the three benchmarks. First, attribute
masking leaves $\Epre$ structurally unchanged and therefore gives
$\DDR{}=0$ across perturbation strengths by construction. Second,
random-walk subgraph sampling preserves connectivity inside the
sampled subgraph and disrupts $\Epre$ \emph{sub-linearly} in $p$:
because edges are removed only at the boundary of the visited
subgraph, the disruption is consistently smaller than under
edge dropping at the same $p$ (e.g.\ on Junyi, $\DDR{} = 0.16$ for
subgraph vs.\ $0.20$ for edge drop at $p=0.20$). Third, edge dropping
disrupts prerequisite structure approximately linearly in $p$, as
expected when each edge is removed independently. Fourth, node
dropping produces the largest disruption because removing a node also
removes all incident prerequisite edges; at $p = 0.30$, node dropping
disrupts roughly half of the prerequisite structure on all three
benchmarks ($\DDR{} \approx 0.52$--$0.54$).

The relative ordering
$\DDR(\text{attr\_mask}) < \DDR(\text{subgraph}) <
\DDR(\text{edge\_drop}) < \DDR(\text{node\_drop})$
is consistent across all three benchmarks and all four perturbation
strengths. We read these results as a structural diagnostic, not as a
claim that any augmentation is beneficial or harmful for a downstream
KT learner. They indicate that prerequisite-aware augmentation design
is needed when graph augmentations are applied to educational DAGs:
node-level operators are an order of magnitude more disruptive than
attribute-level operators, with subgraph sampling occupying an
intermediate, structure-preserving regime.

\paragraph{Similarity edges and dataset properties.}
The similarity-edge rule of Section~\ref{sec:graph} is sensitive to the

a similarity rule appropriate for multi-skill benchmarks may need to be
replaced or supplemented (e.g., by response-pattern correlation) on
single-skill benchmarks. This is not a pipeline failure but a structural
observation that protocol papers should report.

\subsection{Graph Construction Ablation (Train-only vs.\ Full Log)}
\label{sec:exp-graph-ablation}
To isolate the channel hypothesised in H1 (Section~\ref{sec:exp-fextra}),
we contrast graph smoothing using fold-specific train-only exports
(\path{e\_pre\_train\_only.csv}, \path{e\_sim\_train\_only.csv}) against a
\emph{single} prerequisite/similarity graph inferred from the entire
preprocessed interaction table (\path{data/processed/<dataset>/full\_log/}),
ignoring learner splits before edge inference.
All other protocol choices---including the learner-based split used for
evaluation---remain unchanged; only the static graph artefact fed into the
pyKT graph branch differs.

\paragraph{Training KT with fold-specific versus pooled graphs.}
For each fold $f$, the train-only branch builds
$G_f^{\text{train}}=(\Epre^{f,\text{train}},\Esim^{f,\text{train}})$ from
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$ only, exports row-normalised adjacency, and
feeds it to GKT/GIKT within \texttt{pyKT}. The full-log branch replaces
$G_f^{\text{train}}$ with a single $G^{\text{full}}$ inferred from all
learners and timestamps in the preprocessed table while \emph{keeping} the
same learner-based train/val/test split for sequence optimisation. Thus the
only deliberate change is structural pooling across learners before edge
counting; the KT loss still sees train-fold sequences only.

\input{results/tables/graph_ablation.tex}
Table~\ref{tab:graph-ablation} and companion per-dataset tables report
paired fold~0 metrics for GKT, GIKT, SKT, DyGKT, and DGEKT under
train-only versus full-log graph exports. On ASSISTments, XES3G5M, and
Junyi, $\Delta$AUC and $\Delta$ACC round to zero at three decimal places
for every graph model (largest observed shift: DGEKT on ASSISTments,
$\Delta\text{AUC}{=}{+}0.0002$). Synthetic~C2 shows small positive
shifts for DGEKT ($\Delta\text{AUC}{=}{+}0.003$) and SKT
($\Delta\text{ACC}{=}{+}0.002$); Synthetic~C5 exhibits the largest
released deltas (GIKT $\Delta\text{AUC}{=}{+}0.004$,
$\Delta\text{ACC}{=}{+}0.005$; GKT $\Delta\text{AUC}{=}{-}0.006$).
On Junyi, absolute AUCs in Table~\ref{tab:graph-ablation-junyi}
($\approx 0.70$--$0.71$) sit below the sequence-only pyKT band in
Table~\ref{tab:baseline-junyi} ($\approx 0.99$) because graph-augmented
pyKT training was not completed on that corpus; the ablation should be
read through paired $\Delta$ columns, not cross-table AUC levels.

Interpreting this \emph{empirically}: (i)~under our support quantile
($q{=}0.95$), top-$k$ cap, and global $K{=}5000$ filter, pooling
additional learners before edge inference rarely changes which
transitions survive filtering on the public benchmarks; (ii)~pyKT
backbones are near-saturated on ASSISTments/XES3G5M once sequence
features dominate, so graph mass moves headline AUC only at the third
decimal or below; (iii)~the synthetic corpora, with smaller logs and
shallower graphs, remain the most sensitive place to observe nonzero
$\Delta$ metrics in this release.

We also inspected edge-count deltas between branches on fold~0:
Junyi raw prerequisite candidates differ by under $2\%$ in support
totals; ASSISTments and XES3G5M show similar stability. XES3G5M retains
${\sim}350$ similarity edges in both branches because multi-skill items
drive co-occurrence; ASSISTments has zero $\Esim$ in both branches.
The experiment shows train-only construction is \emph{not} penalising
headline metrics relative to this deliberate full-log variant in our
released configuration. It does \emph{not} prove absence of leakage in
general---only that this particular pooling shortcut does not move pyKT
AUC/ACC beyond three decimals on the public benchmarks here. The
comparison is reproducible via
\path{scripts/run_graph_ablation_experiment.sh}.

\subsection{Ground-Truth Cross-Validation on Junyi}
\label{sec:exp-gtxv}
Junyi Academy~\cite{junyi_dataset} is the only benchmark in our study
that ships with a publicly released expert prerequisite annotation
(Chang~et~al.). The annotation enumerates directed exercise-pair
relations with a continuous \texttt{Prerequisite\_avg} score on a
mean-opinion-score-like scale. We use $\theta=0.5$ as the prerequisite
filter, retaining $1{,}131$ directed expert edges over $361$ distinct
exercise endpoints that participate in those edges. Following Section~\ref{sec:gtxv-protocol}, expert
exercise names are mapped to pipeline \texttt{kc\_id} via the
preprocessing hash function reconstructed from
\path{data/raw/junyi/junyi_Exercise_table.csv}. All $1{,}131$ expert
edges align deterministically: the alignment audit reports
$\text{matched}=1{,}131$, $\text{missing}_{\text{src}}=0$,
$\text{missing}_{\text{dst}}=0$, and alignment rate $1.0$. We compare
this expert edge set against the fold~0 train-only inferred list in
\path{e\_pre\_train\_only.csv}, which contains $2{,}152$ ranked directed
candidates (the same file audited in Section~\ref{sec:exp-audit}; cycle
pruning yields $1{,}139$ retained acyclic edges).

\input{results/tables/gt_validation_table}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.65\linewidth]{results/figures/fig_pr_curve.pdf}
\caption{Edge-level precision and recall of train-only inferred

\paragraph{Edge-level overlap is low and roughly constant across $K$.}
Table~\ref{tab:gt-validation} reports the cross-validation sweep.
Edge-level precision falls from $0.235$ at $K=200$ to $0.143$ at
$K=5{,}000$; edge-level recall rises from $0.042$ to $0.271$ over the
same range; the F1 score lies in the narrow band
$[0.07, 0.19]$ and reaches its size-matched value
$0.183$ at $K=|\text{expert}|=1{,}131$. The narrow plateau indicates
that adding more inferred edges does not substantially improve overlap
with the expert annotation. Direction agreement, measured only on
pairs where both DAGs have an edge between the same node pair, is
$0.261$ at $K=1{,}131$ and rises only modestly to $0.285$ at the full
$K$. A coin-flip baseline of direction agreement is $0.5$ when both
DAGs have an edge for a given pair, so the observed values indicate
that, conditional on the pair being shared, the two sources are
\emph{more often than not in disagreement about direction}.

\paragraph{Node-level overlap is comparatively high.}
Although edge overlap is low, the node Jaccard between the two graphs
is $0.632$ at $K=1{,}131$ and $0.610$ at $K=5{,}000$. The two sources
therefore touch a similar subset of \KCs{}; they disagree primarily on
\emph{which edges to draw between those \KCs{}}.

\paragraph{Reachability F1 grows with $K$.}
Whereas edge F1 plateaus, reachability F1 (computed over the transitive
closure of each DAG) rises monotonically from $0.104$ at $K=1{,}131$
to $0.339$ at $K \geq 2{,}500$. This shows that the inferred DAG, when
augmented beyond the size of the expert set, recovers a substantial
fraction of the indirect prerequisite chains in the expert annotation,
even when the specific intermediate edges differ.

\paragraph{Qualitative disagreement patterns.}
The disagreement examples saved by the procedure
(\path{results/tables/disagreement_examples.csv}) reveal a
systematic asymmetry between the two sources.
The top inferred-only edges are predominantly bidirectional pairs of
co-occurring basic-arithmetic skills, e.g.\ \texttt{number\_line}
$\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{addition\_1} and \texttt{addition\_1}
$\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{representing\_numbers}; these reflect
temporal-co-occurrence patterns in learning sequences that do not
carry a stable ordering.
The top expert-only edges are predominantly systematic
level-progression annotations, e.g.\ \texttt{equivalent\_fractions\_2}
$\rightarrow$ \texttt{equivalent\_fractions}, \texttt{addition\_2}
$\rightarrow$ \texttt{addition\_1}, and
\texttt{linear\_equations\_4} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{linear\_equations\_3};
these capture pedagogically-meaningful precedences that behavioural
inference does not consistently recover because learners often interact
with multiple difficulty levels in either order.
The \texttt{wrong\_direction} category similarly contains many
level-progression pairs (e.g.\ \texttt{addition\_1} $\rightarrow$
\texttt{addition\_2}) where the inferred direction is the reverse of
the expert annotation.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The combination of (i) low edge F1, (ii) low direction agreement,
(iii) high node Jaccard, and (iv) growing reachability F1 supports a
coherent reading: behavioural inference and expert annotation capture
\emph{related but distinct} aspects of prerequisite structure.
Behavioural inference primarily encodes \emph{which \KCs{} co-occur}
in learning sequences, with limited information about ordering.
Expert annotation primarily encodes \emph{which \KCs{} pedagogically
build on which}, including systematic level-progression relations
that are largely invisible in raw temporal data.
Consequently, train-only inferred $\Epre$ is \emph{not} a low-cost
substitute for expert prerequisite curation; it is a structurally
distinct signal. This has two practical implications. First,
graph-augmented KT studies that use behaviourally-inferred prerequisite
graphs should not claim those graphs as ``approximations of expert
curation''. Second, when both sources are available, they could be
combined as multi-relational inputs ($E_{\text{behavioural}}$ and
$E_{\text{expert}}$) rather than treated as substitutes for each other.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
The cross-validation is performed only on Junyi (the only benchmark
in the present study with a public expert annotation) and on a single
random seed. The expert annotation operates at the exercise-pair level
and uses an MOS-like score; the threshold $\theta=0.5$ retains the
full Chang annotation but raising $\theta$ would yield a stricter
high-confidence subset. We did not sweep $\theta$ in the present
release.
\subsection{Empirical Observation: Graph-Augmented KT Models Do Not
Consistently Outperform Sequence-Only Baselines under
Leakage-Controlled Evaluation}
\label{sec:exp-fextra}
The baseline numbers in Tables~\ref{tab:baseline-junyi}--\ref{tab:baseline-xes3g5m}
support an empirical observation that we believe is worth reporting at the level
of the protocol itself.

Under leakage-controlled evaluation (train-only $\Epre$, train-only
$\Esim$, learner-based temporal split, no validation- or test-fold
interaction consumed by graph construction), graph-augmented pyKT models
do not consistently outperform the strongest sequence-only checkpoints in
our comparison. Concretely, on ASSISTments~2012 GKT (AUC $0.960$) trails
\textit{simpleKT}/AKT/GIKT ($\approx 0.967$--$0.968$); on XES3G5M GKT
($0.835$) trails \textit{simpleKT}/GIKT/AKT ($\approx 0.874$) by a wider
margin; on Junyi the released pyKT table contains only sequence models
(all $\approx 0.989$) because graph-augmented training was skipped for
resource reasons. This pattern holds despite substantial heterogeneity in
scale, KC granularity, and graph density (Table~\ref{tab:dataset-stats}).

We do not claim that graph-augmented KT is uninformative as a research
direction; the protocol does not, by itself, refute graph-augmented KT.
What the protocol does is remove a specific channel through which
held-out information can reach the model: the concept graph itself.
Once this channel is closed, three non-mutually-exclusive hypotheses
are consistent with the observed ordering.

\paragraph{H1 -- Leakage in prior evaluations.}
A common practice in graph-augmented KT is to compute graph statistics
from the full interaction log prior to train/test splitting, or to use
a precomputed graph whose construction was not audited at the fold
level. Under such practice, graph-augmented models effectively observe
test-fold transitions through the graph during training. Our protocol
removes this channel by enforcing fold-specific edge inference and
provenance logging. The reduced relative advantage of graph-augmented
models in our setting is consistent with the hypothesis that part of
the gains reported in prior literature reflects this leakage rather
than the structural information itself.
Table~\ref{tab:graph-ablation} instantiates the controlled comparison suggested by H1:
identical learner splits and evaluation code, but graph statistics pooled over the full
preprocessed log before edge inference.
In our fold~0 pyKT run, headline metrics on ASSISTments, XES3G5M, and Junyi are
unchanged at three decimals relative to train-only graphs for all five graph models;
Synthetic~C5 shows the largest shifts (GIKT $\Delta\text{AUC}{=}{+}0.004$).
H1 remains relevant as a risk statement about workflows that leak held-out mass into
graph construction in general; architectures or inference rules that exploit
cross-split pooling more aggressively might still show larger shifts, and the ablation
should be revisited when those components change.

\paragraph{H2 -- Aggregate-metric masking.}
Aggregate AUC averages over the full concept-frequency distribution.
The cold-start diagnostic (Section~\ref{sec:exp-coldstart}) shows that
per-stratum behaviour can differ substantially from aggregate
behaviour, and graph-augmented KT may help precisely on rare \KCs{}
where temporal evidence is weakest. Aggregate-AUC comparisons can
therefore mask gains concentrated on specific strata. The cold-start
protocol proposed here is a partial remedy.

\paragraph{H3 -- Strong-backbone substitution.}
Modern attention-based backbones such as AKT and simpleKT may already
capture, through long-range temporal attention, much of the relational
information that an explicit concept graph would otherwise encode. In
such a regime, additional structural signal injected at the
concept-embedding level may yield diminishing returns. We do not test
this hypothesis directly here but note that it predicts a
backbone-dependent pattern: weaker temporal backbones should benefit
more from graph augmentation than stronger ones, all else equal.

These three hypotheses are not exhaustive and are not mutually
exclusive. Distinguishing among them requires a controlled re-run of
prior graph-KT papers under our protocol, which is beyond the scope
of this paper. The observation we make here is narrower:
\emph{under leakage-controlled evaluation, the absolute ranking between
graph-augmented and sequence-only KT can differ from settings that pool graph
evidence across splits}, and benchmarking practice should treat graph provenance
as a first-order experimental factor.
Separately, the train-only versus full-log ablation for graph-augmented pyKT
(Table~\ref{tab:graph-ablation}) shows no headline metric shift beyond three decimals
on the three public benchmarks, with modest nonzero $\Delta$ only on synthetic logs,
which constrains how strongly one can infer leakage \emph{magnitude} from this
particular diagnostic alone.

\paragraph{Why this strengthens the protocol.}
If graph-augmented KT models reliably outperformed sequence-only
baselines regardless of graph provenance, leakage control would be a
useful but secondary concern.
The observation that sequence-only pyKT checkpoints match or exceed GKT on
ASSISTments and XES3G5M under train-only graphs, together with explicit auditing of edge
provenance, elevates leakage control from a hygiene step to a benchmarking
precondition.
Combined with the ground-truth cross-validation finding of Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}
that inferred and expert prerequisite signals are structurally
distinct, this argues that graph quality cannot be assumed---it must
be audited.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
The baseline ordering uses fold~0 pyKT checkpoints in the released artefact
(where \texttt{n\_folds}${=}1$); protocol metrics such as \DDR{} and leakage
tables aggregate three folds. DAG audit and GT cross-validation passages refer
to fold~0 exports by default. It does not constitute an exhaustive re-evaluation of any specific
prior paper, and we do not name papers whose reported gains we believe
to be leakage-driven. We also do not vary every graph-augmented architecture
in the literature; the finding pertains to the pyKT GKT/GIKT/SKT/DyGKT/DGEKT
instantiations consumed here~\cite{liu2022pykt}. Stronger graph-KT architectures
\cite{tong2020skt,dygkt2024,dgekt2024} may behave differently under
the protocol; auditing them is an explicit follow-up.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}
\paragraph{Scalability and broader domains.}
The protocol modules in Figure~\ref{fig:framework} are dataset-agnostic:
any KT log with $(u,t,q,c,y)$ and a train-only Q-matrix can pass through
split checking, graph construction, DAG audit, and leakage metrics.
Computational cost is dominated by transition counting
($O(|\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}|)$ per fold) and cycle pruning
(polynomial in candidate edge count with a capped cycle enumerator), not
by KT training itself. For million-scale logs (e.g.\ EdNet~\cite{choi2020ednet}),
the same sparsity caps ($K$, top-$k$ per node) bound $|\Epre|$ and keep
audit tractable; distributed counting is an engineering extension not
required for correctness.

Broader domains include corporate skills graphs (hierarchical job
competencies), language-learning apps with prerequisite lexicon graphs,
and MOOC platforms with forum or video-derived auxiliary relations. The
similarity rule may need domain-specific co-occurrence (skills co-tested
in the same certification item, linguistic collocation) when item--KC
tags are single-label. Ground-truth cross-validation generalises whenever
an expert DAG exists; otherwise behavioural inference remains auditable
but uncalibrated. Cold-start strata extend to any taxonomy where tail
concepts are pedagogically critical. \DDR{} applies whenever a directed
curriculum graph is used, including non-KT graph pretraining pipelines
that import educational DAGs.

\paragraph{Implications for graph-enhanced KT.}
The protocol shows that KC graph construction should be treated as a
first-class experimental component rather than an unexamined
preprocessing step. Fold-specific edge inference, provenance logging,
and DAG validation make it possible to identify leakage risks and graph
construction choices that would otherwise remain hidden. The \DDR{}
results further show that generic graph augmentations can substantially
disturb prerequisite structure, especially when they operate at the node
level. This motivates follow-up work on prerequisite-preserving
augmentation operators.
\paragraph{Cold-start reporting.}
The cold-start diagnostic shows why KT studies should not rely only on
aggregate AUC. Rare \KCs{} may behave differently from frequent
\KCs{}, and apparent gains or high scores on small strata may reflect
class imbalance rather than genuine modelling improvement. Reporting
per-stratum metrics gives reviewers a clearer view of whether a method
is robust across the KC frequency distribution.
Figure~\ref{fig:kt-graph-all} links this diagnostic to concrete graph
topology: benchmarks with sparse $\Epre$ (ASSISTments) still exhibit
cold strata, whereas Junyi's warmer strata coincide with denser arithmetic
chains in the schematic plot.

\paragraph{Behavioural versus expert prerequisite curation.}
The ground-truth cross-validation on Junyi
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}) shows that train-only behavioural
inference and expert prerequisite annotation are correlated at the
node level but structurally distinct at the edge level. Two
implications follow. First, behavioural inference cannot be treated
as a low-cost approximation of expert curation, and papers that
report graph-augmented KT results on behaviourally-inferred graphs
should not claim those results transfer directly to settings with
expert prerequisite graphs (or vice versa). Second, the two signals
are complementary: in the most informative setup, a graph-augmented
KT model would consume both an inferred behavioural graph and an
expert prerequisite graph as separate relations, rather than treating
either as a substitute for the other. We do not test this combined
setting in the present paper, but the cross-validation provides the
quantitative basis for proposing it.
\paragraph{Implications for graph-KT benchmarking.}
The empirical observation in Section~\ref{sec:exp-fextra} has direct
implications for how the graph-KT subfield reports gains. If
leakage-controlled evaluation changes the relative ranking of
graph-augmented and sequence-only KT---and our fold~0 pyKT diagnostics on
ASSISTments and XES3G5M suggest it can---then benchmark tables that do not control for graph-
construction provenance are not directly comparable across papers. We
do not advocate dismissing reported gains in the graph-KT literature;
we advocate \emph{disaggregating} them. A reported AUC improvement of
$\Delta$ on a benchmark should ideally be decomposed into three
components: the part that survives leakage control, the part that
depends on graph-construction choices (relation type, threshold, edge
cap, encoder), and the part that depends on backbone strength. The
protocol in this paper supplies the first component; the cold-start
diagnostic supplies a partial view of the second; the third remains an
open methodological question.

A practical recommendation follows. When introducing a new
graph-augmented KT model, papers should report at minimum: (a) the
fold-specific provenance of every retained graph edge; (b) the
cold-start stratum on which the gain is most concentrated; (c) the
sequence-only baseline matched in compute and hyperparameter budget;
(d) when an expert prerequisite annotation is available, the
ground-truth cross-validation of the consumed graph against that
annotation, using the edge / direction / reachability / node-Jaccard
metric family used in Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}. Without these
reports, an aggregate-AUC improvement is difficult to attribute. The
protocol presented here makes (a), (b), and (d) tractable.

\subsection{Threats to Validity and Metric Interpretation}
\label{sec:threats-validity}
Three experimental artefacts invite misreading if taken out of protocol
context: the train-only versus full-log ablation (Section~\ref{sec:exp-graph-ablation}),
aggressive DAG cycle pruning (Section~\ref{sec:exp-audit}), and elevated
temporal-boundary violation rates in Table~\ref{tab:leakage-metrics}.

\paragraph{Ablation null shift does not negate leakage control (Issue~1).}
The controlled comparison in Table~\ref{tab:graph-ablation} moves headline
pyKT AUC/ACC by less than $0.001$ at three-decimal precision on
ASSISTments, XES3G5M, and Junyi under our \emph{deliberately leaky}
full-log graph variant, while Synthetic~C5 exhibits the largest released
shift ($|\Delta\text{AUC}|{\approx}0.006$ for GKT).
We interpret this pattern as follows.
First, \emph{methodological hygiene stands independently of empirical
impact}: graph statistics must not pool held-out learners regardless of
whether a particular backbone exploits the shortcut.
Second, the null shift on dense public benchmarks indicates that, under
our support quantile, top-$k$ cap, and global $K{=}5000$ filter, pooling
additional learners before edge inference rarely changes which transitions
survive---not that leakage is impossible in general.
Third, train-only construction is \emph{not penalising} headline metrics
relative to this full-log variant in the released configuration; that is a
positive outcome for protocol adoption rather than evidence that auditing
is unnecessary.
Synthetic corpora remain the most sensitive place to observe non-zero
$\Delta$ in this release.
Direct leakage metrics ($\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f$, provenance
logging) and ground-truth cross-validation (Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv})
therefore remain the primary audit deliverables; the ablation quantifies
one specific pooling shortcut rather than certifying all graph-KT workflows.

\paragraph{Cycle pruning removes conflicts, not arbitrary mass (Issue~4).}
On Junyi fold~0, train-only temporal inference yields $2{,}152$ directed
candidates; lowest-confidence cycle pruning removes $1{,}013$ edges
(${\approx}47\%$) before $1{,}139$ acyclic edges remain
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-audit}).
Johnson cycle enumeration finds $100$ cyclic components in the raw
candidate set; pruning breaks each cycle by dropping the \emph{lowest-weight
edge in that cycle} (\path{cycle\_prune\_lowest\_confidence} entries in
\path{results/reports/*\_dag\_pruning\_log.csv}), a standard tie-break when
behavioural co-occurrence induces bidirectional or conflicting precedence
signals.
Pruned edges therefore disproportionately reflect \emph{conflict resolution}
among spurious or weak transitions, not random deletion of the expert
backbone: retained edges carry comparable or higher transition support
(median weight ${\approx}0.003$ on Junyi fold~0 vs.\ ${\approx}0.003$ for
pruned edges at the cycle tie-break).
We do not claim optimality of this heuristic; sensitivity to pruning rate
should be reported alongside \DDR{} (Section~\ref{sec:exp-ddr}), which
shows modest structural disruption at moderate edge-drop rates
(e.g.\ $\DDR{\approx}0.09$ at $p{=}0.10$ on Junyi).

\paragraph{TBVR measures within-train strictness, not split leakage (Issue~5).}
Table~\ref{tab:leakage-metrics} reports $\mathrm{TBVR}_f$ up to
${\approx}0.76$ on XES3G5M and ${\approx}0.73$ on Synthetic~C2.
Under learner-disjoint folds, $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$ confirms
no cross-split structural leakage; $\mathrm{TBVR}_f$ instead operationalises
Eq.~\eqref{eq:tbvr} with an \emph{indexed boundary inside the train
fold}: for each learner, the earliest $70\%$ of chronologically ordered
train interactions define the audit cutoff, while prerequisite edges are
inferred from transition evidence over the \emph{full} train timeline.
A post-cutoff train token is flagged when it participates in a retained
edge transition straddling that boundary.
High $\mathrm{TBVR}_f$ therefore signals \emph{within-train temporal
mixing} of edge evidence relative to the indexed audit boundary---not a
valid/test split bug and not, by itself, a violation of
\texttt{train\_only\_flag} provenance.
On XES3G5M, dense multi-skill co-assessment makes many late-train tokens
support retained transitions; on Synthetic~C2, the two-\KC{} toy graph
retains a single directed edge after pruning, so most post-cutoff tokens
inevitably touch that edge (a structural artefact of the sanity corpus).
We treat elevated $\mathrm{TBVR}_f$ as motivation to report temporal
hygiene alongside $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f$, not as grounds to
discard the pipeline.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
This version has six limitations.
First, diagnostic KT baselines are reported as fold~0 pyKT checkpoints
with collapsed bootstrap intervals (\texttt{n\_folds}${=}1$ in the CSV),
whereas \DDR{}, leakage metrics, and multi-fold DAG exports use three
folds; Junyi includes SKT in Table~\ref{tab:baseline-junyi} but omits
heavier graph-augmented pyKT rows (GKT, GIKT, DyGKT, DGEKT) because of
memory and runtime limits.
Table~\ref{tab:baseline-cv} and \path{scripts/significance_testing.py}
await the full three-fold baseline export.
Second, the protocol's similarity rule yields no $\Esim$ on
ASSISTments~2012. The audit
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-coldstart}) confirms that this is a structural
property of the dataset's Q-matrix---$100\%$ of train items are
single-skill, so item-overlap similarity is identically zero---rather
than a pipeline failure. Replacing item-overlap with response-pattern
correlation is a candidate extension.
Third, the augmentation evaluation is limited to four label-agnostic
families adopted from GraphCL~\cite{you2020graphcl}; structure-aware
operators (e.g.\ prerequisite-preserving rewiring, attribute-only
node drop, hierarchy-respecting subgraph sampling) are not yet
covered by the \DDR{} sweep.
Fourth, ground-truth cross-validation
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}) is reported only on Junyi, because it is
the only benchmark in our study with a public expert prerequisite
annotation; extending the procedure to ASSISTments and XES3G5M
requires constructing or obtaining expert annotations for those
datasets, which is beyond the scope of this protocol paper. We also
did not sweep the prerequisite-score threshold $\theta$; the reported
numbers use $\theta=0.5$ and retain the full Chang annotation.
Fifth, calibration analysis and formal AUC comparison procedures are
deferred to future work.
Sixth, the train-only versus full-log graph ablation
(Table~\ref{tab:graph-ablation}) moves headline pyKT metrics by less than
$0.001$ AUC/ACC at three-decimal precision on ASSISTments, XES3G5M, and
Junyi; Synthetic~C5 shows shifts up to $\approx 0.006$ AUC for GKT. We do not
claim that other architectures, metrics, or inference rules would exhibit the
same null shift.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}
We presented a leakage-controlled protocol for constructing and auditing
concept graphs in KT. The protocol combines learner-based temporal
splits, train-only graph construction, edge provenance logging, DAG
cycle pruning, \DDR{} augmentation diagnostics with four formally
defined operators, ground-truth cross-validation against expert
prerequisite annotation when available, and cold-start KC
stratification. On Junyi Academy, ASSISTments~2012, and XES3G5M, the
audit produces acyclic train-only prerequisite graphs after transparent
cycle pruning. The \DDR{} sweep separates the four augmentation
families into four distinct structural regimes:
$\DDR(\text{attr\_mask}) < \DDR(\text{subgraph}) <
\DDR(\text{edge\_drop}) < \DDR(\text{node\_drop})$,
consistent across all three datasets and all four perturbation
strengths.

Ground-truth cross-validation on Junyi shows that train-only
behavioural inference and expert prerequisite annotation are highly
correlated at the node level (Jaccard $\approx 0.63$) but only weakly
correlated at the edge level (F1 $\approx 0.18$ at $K=|\text{expert}|$,
direction agreement $\approx 0.26$). Qualitative disagreement patterns
indicate that the two sources capture related but distinct aspects of
prerequisite structure---behavioural co-occurrence versus pedagogical
ordering---so behavioural inference should not be treated as a
low-cost substitute for expert curation.

The cold-start diagnostic reveals per-stratum behaviour that aggregate
metrics would otherwise hide (Table~\ref{tab:cold-start-strata}). A secondary empirical observation, made
possible by the protocol itself, is that GKT pyKT checkpoints do not
consistently outperform \textit{simpleKT}/AKT under leakage-controlled
evaluation on ASSISTments and XES3G5M in the released fold~0 run. We frame this not as a refutation of
graph-augmented KT but as a motivation for leakage-controlled
evaluation as a benchmarking precondition: if reported gains in the
literature are partly attributable to graph-construction discipline
rather than to structural information, then comparing graph-augmented
and sequence-only KT requires the discipline this paper proposes.

The paper is deliberately positioned as a protocol/resource paper rather
than a model paper. All scripts, configurations, logs, tables, figures,
and prediction samples needed to reproduce the results are organised in
the companion repository at \path{https://github.com/tuanymc/p0_project.git},
with source code under \path{src/}, configurations under \path{configs/},
pipeline scripts under \path{scripts/}, logs under \path{logs/}, and
outputs under \path{results/}, including the ground-truth
cross-validation artefacts under \path{results/gt_validation/junyi/}.
\paragraph{Ethics and data use.}
We use only public KT benchmarks under their respective licences. No
personally identifiable information is used, and no claims about real
learning outcomes are made.
