\abstract{Graph-enhanced knowledge tracing (KT) often infers concept graphs
from the same interaction logs used for training and evaluation. When edges
are pooled before the train/test split, held-out information can reach the
model through the graph even if the KT optimiser sees only the training fold.
We present a leakage-controlled \emph{audit protocol}---not a new KT
backbone---for knowledge-component (KC) graph construction: learner-based
temporal splits, train-only prerequisite inference (optional similarity where
the Q-matrix supports it), DAG validation with cycle pruning, KC-level
cold-start stratification, a practitioner checklist, and a DAG Disruption Rate
(\DDR{}) diagnostic. We instantiate the protocol on XES3G5M (primary
model-comparison benchmark), ASSISTments~2012 (secondary), Junyi Academy
(saturated sanity + ground-truth cross-validation), and two synthetic logs.
The audit yields acyclic train-only prerequisite graphs on all corpora; the
\DDR{} sweep separates five augmentation operators, with a prerequisite-preserving
operator measurably less disruptive than edge dropping at a matched budget.
An \emph{exploratory} DGEKT-only downstream stress test on ASSISTments and
XES3G5M finds near-zero AUC changes despite large structural perturbation; we
do not extrapolate that null sensitivity to other backbones. On XES3G5M,
leakage-controlled auditing changes deployable model ranking (e.g.\ GKT trails
\textit{simpleKT} by ${\approx}0.041$ AUC). Ground-truth cross-validation on
Junyi shows node-level agreement but edge-level divergence between behavioural
inference and expert curation. A deliberate full-log graph variant shifts
headline metrics by at most $0.003$ on the three public benchmarks studied
here; we position leakage control as an applied reproducibility and
decision-support precondition, not as a route to higher headline accuracy.}

\keywords{Knowledge tracing, Concept graphs, Leakage control, DAG audit,
Ground-truth cross-validation, Cold-start diagnostics, Reproducibility}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Knowledge tracing (KT) estimates a learner's latent mastery of
\KCs{} from interaction history, supporting adaptive practice,
intelligent tutoring, and curriculum recommendation. The field has
progressed from Bayesian Knowledge Tracing~\cite{corbett1995bkt} to
deep sequential models~\cite{piech2015dkt,zhang2017dkvmn,ghosh2020akt,liu2023simplekt,pandey2019rkt,shen2021saint} and graph-enhanced models
that propagate information over relations between
\KCs{}~\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt,yang2021gikt,dygkt2024,dgekt2024,chen2023hgkt}. These graph-based models rely on
auxiliary concept graphs, usually encoding prerequisite or similarity
relations.

\paragraph{Problem setting.}
Let $\mathcal{D}=\{(u,t,q,c,y)\}$ be a KT interaction log with learner
$u$, timestamp $t$, item $q$, tagged \KC{} $c$, and correctness label
$y\in\{0,1\}$. A learner-based temporal split
$\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}},\mathcal{F}_{\text{val}},
\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}})$ assigns each learner to exactly one fold.
For evaluation fold $f$, the protocol must construct a fold-specific
concept graph with prerequisite edges and, when the Q-matrix permits,
optional similarity edges:
$G_f=(\mathcal{C},\Epre^f\cup\Esim^f)$ using \emph{only}
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$, then train a KT model on
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$ and score predictions on
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^f$. The central question is whether reported
graph-augmented KT gains remain valid when $G_f$ is audited for
leakage, acyclicity, and cold-start coverage.

Figure~\ref{fig:intro-leakage} contrasts the two workflows. In
workflow~(a), prerequisite or similarity edges may be supported by
validation/test interactions; the KT module then appears to generalise
while part of the structural input already encodes future behaviour.
Workflow~(b) closes this channel by making graph construction a
first-class, fold-conditioned step.

\paragraph{Real-world significance.}
Leakage-controlled concept graphs matter wherever KT systems drive
scheduling and gating decisions in ITS platforms, MOOC pipelines, and
corporate training~\cite{heffernan2006assist,junyi_dataset,choi2020ednet,xes3g5m2023}.
Without fold-specific audits, reported AUC improvements are difficult to
attribute to modelling versus preprocessing; our protocol makes graph
provenance, DAG validity, and cold-start strata explicit reporting artefacts.

\paragraph{Fit for applied intelligence.}
\textit{Applied Intelligence} readers typically expect methods that improve
the \emph{trustworthy deployment} of intelligent systems, not only higher
offline scores. Our contribution is an audit layer that reduces preventable
mistakes in graph-enhanced KT pipelines: leaking graph evidence across splits,
deploying acyclicity violations silently, reporting aggregate AUC on
repetition-saturated corpora, or treating behavioural graphs as expert
prerequisites. The deliverable is a reproducible checklist, provenance
artefacts, and diagnostics that can change \emph{which model a practitioner
reports and deploys}, even when headline accuracy does not move.

\paragraph{Audit case study (XES3G5M).}
On our primary benchmark, leakage-controlled evaluation separates graph backbones
more clearly than on repetition-heavy corpora. Under train-only $\Epre$ and
three-fold learner CV, \textit{simpleKT} reaches AUC ${\approx}0.875$ while
GKT reaches only ${\approx}0.834$ (mean $\Delta{\approx}{-}0.041$ across folds);
SKT and DyGKT collapse further (${\approx}0.625$--$0.752$). GIKT remains
competitive (${\approx}0.878$). A deployment team that ranked models on leaky
full-log graphs or on near-ceiling Junyi/ASSIST aggregates could therefore
reach a different shortlist than one that runs this protocol first. That
\emph{decision shift}---not a claimed SOTA gain---is the applied value we
target for APIN.

A key risk is that concept graphs are often inferred from the same
interaction logs used to train and evaluate KT models. If graph edges
are computed from the full log before train/test splitting, held-out
information can leak back into the model through the graph. This leakage
may occur even when the KT model itself is trained only on the training
fold. Benchmarking tools such as \texttt{pyKT}~\cite{liu2022pykt} have
highlighted the importance of preprocessing and split discipline, but a
fold-specific audit protocol for KC graph construction remains
under-specified.

A second risk arises when generic graph augmentations are imported into
KT. Node dropping, edge dropping, attribute masking, and subgraph
sampling were originally designed for general-purpose graphs
\cite{you2020graphcl,zhu2021gca,xia2022simgrace,thakoor2021bgrl,velickovic2018dgi,hassani2020mvgrl}. However, prerequisite graphs are
directional and educationally meaningful: removing a prerequisite edge
may remove a pedagogical constraint. The degree to which label-agnostic
graph augmentations disturb educational prerequisite DAGs has not been
systematically measured in KT.

A third issue is the informal use of ``cold-start'' in KT. In this
paper, we define cold-start at the KC level, based on the number of
train-fold interactions associated with each KC, in contrast to user- or
item-level cold-start in recommender systems~\cite{schein2002coldstart}.
This separates cold-start KC behaviour from other cold-start settings.

We address these issues at the level of protocol rather than model. We
do not propose a new KT architecture or claim state-of-the-art
performance. Instead, we release a leakage-controlled graph construction
and audit protocol, a structural augmentation diagnostic, a ground-truth
cross-validation procedure (instantiated on Junyi), and a cold-start KC
report. The protocol is instantiated on Junyi
Academy~\cite{junyi_dataset,chang2015junyi}, ASSISTments~2012, and
XES3G5M~\cite{xes3g5m2023}.

\paragraph{Novelty.}
We contribute a reproducible \emph{audit layer} around concept-graph
construction---not a new KT backbone~\cite{abdelrahman2023kt_survey,liu2022pykt}:
fold-specific leakage diagnostics, \DDR{} for augmentation design on
educational DAGs, KC-level cold-start strata, and ground-truth
cross-validation where expert graphs exist~\cite{bai2025survey,chang2015junyi}.
We audit a fixed train-only inference rule and separate structural disruption
from downstream AUC, which graph-KT and contrastive-KT papers typically omit
\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,lee2022cl4kt}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1.} A train-only protocol for constructing and auditing
  KC graphs with prerequisite edges and optional similarity edges
  $G=(\mathcal{C}, \Epre \cup \Esim)$ for KT, including split checks,
  edge provenance, per-relation availability reporting, and DAG validation
  with cycle pruning.
  \item \textbf{C2.} The \DDR{} metric, which quantifies how generic
  graph augmentations disrupt prerequisite assumptions on educational
  DAGs, instantiated with four formally defined label-agnostic operators
  and one prerequisite-preserving operator; the latter is measurably less
  disruptive at a matched perturbation budget, showing that \DDR{} is a
  usable design target rather than a restatement of operator definitions.
  \item \textbf{C3.} A cold-start KC diagnostic with explicit
  frequency-based strata and reproducible scripts on three KT
  benchmarks.
  \item \textbf{C4.} A ground-truth cross-validation procedure that
  quantifies, on Junyi Academy, how train-only behavioural inference of
  $\Epre$ agrees with the expert prerequisite annotation of
  Chang~et~al.~\cite{junyi_dataset}, using edge-level, direction-level,
  and reachability-level metrics across a sweep of top-$K$ cutoffs.
  \item \textbf{C5.} An empirical observation, anchored on XES3G5M as the
  primary model-comparison benchmark, that backbone ranking under
  leakage-controlled graph construction is \emph{model-specific}: GKT trails
  \textit{simpleKT} by mean $\Delta$AUC ${\approx}{-}0.041$ on XES3G5M and
  ${\approx}{-}0.007$ on ASSISTments (three-fold means; fold-level magnitudes
  in Table~\ref{tab:baseline-cv}), while GIKT remains competitive. We pair
  this with a train-only versus full-log ablation and tiered leakage audits.
  We discuss three non-mutually-exclusive hypotheses and frame the result as
  a deployment and benchmarking precondition rather than as a refutation of
  graph-augmented KT. Supplementary Section~S3 links benchmark repetitiveness
  to near-ceiling AUC on Junyi/ASSIST and to the near-null ablation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Practitioner checklist.}
We distill the benchmarking recommendations developed in
Section~\ref{sec:disc-implications} into five reporting items for
graph-augmented KT papers:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] fold-specific provenance for every retained graph edge;
  \item[(b)] cold-start KC strata alongside aggregate AUC;
  \item[(c)] sequence-only baselines matched in compute and hyperparameter
  budget;
  \item[(d)] ground-truth cross-validation when expert prerequisite
  annotations exist; and
  \item[(e)] sensitivity to structure-preserving versus structure-damaging
  graph perturbations.
\end{enumerate}
Supplementary Table~S11 reports fold-level $\Delta$AUC magnitudes and
paired-test $p$-values; we treat the \emph{effect sizes} in
Table~\ref{tab:baseline-cv} as the primary evidence because three-fold CV
limits inferential power.

\paragraph{Scope and benchmark roles.}
We assign explicit roles to the public corpora:
\textbf{primary}---XES3G5M (low KC-repeat, non-saturated AUC, clearest
graph-backbone separation); \textbf{secondary}---ASSISTments~2012 (leakage
ablation and cold-start); \textbf{saturated sanity}---Junyi Academy
(ground-truth cross-validation and DAG density, not headline model ranking).
Synthetic C2/C5 exercise tiny-graph audit modules. Model-comparison claims in
C5 and Section~\ref{sec:disc-model-specific} are anchored on XES3G5M unless
noted. Table~\ref{tab:baseline-summary} reports cross-dataset diagnostic AUC;
per-dataset ACC/NLL tables with 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals are in
Supplementary Tables~S1--S5. All baselines use train-only exported graphs and
valid+test sequence positions under three-fold learner CV. Protocol metrics
(\DDR{}, leakage tables, DAG audit over folds~0--2) use the same three-fold
configuration unless noted. Junyi and XES3G5M include the corrected GIKT rerun
in the main baseline table. DAG audit and ground-truth cross-validation prose
refer to fold~0 unless noted. Similarity edges $|\Esim|{=}0$ on ASSISTments
(single-skill Q-matrix) and are sparse on XES3G5M; Junyi is the only corpus
where both $\Epre$ and $\Esim$ are populated under the default rule---we
therefore describe most runs as \emph{prerequisite-graph} protocol instances
rather than as fully multi-relational deployments. On the three public
benchmarks studied here, controlling the graph-leakage channel does not by
itself yield large accuracy corrections (Section~\ref{sec:exp-graph-ablation}):
the train-only versus full-log ablation moves headline metrics by at most
$0.003$ AUC/ACC (synthetic sanity logs show slightly larger shifts). We
therefore present leakage control as an applied reproducibility and
decision-support contribution together with a measured boundary result, not as
a route to higher headline numbers. We do not claim that the protocol
eliminates every possible form of leakage, nor do we make claims about
real-world learning outcomes.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Knowledge Tracing and Benchmark Discipline}
\label{sec:related-kt}
KT progressed from probabilistic models (BKT)~\cite{corbett1995bkt} to deep
sequence architectures (DKT, AKT, \textit{simpleKT})~\cite{piech2015dkt,ghosh2020akt,liu2023simplekt}
and benchmark libraries such as \texttt{pyKT}~\cite{liu2022pykt,abdelrahman2023kt_survey}.

\textbf{Leakage in KT and in ML more broadly.}
Data leakage---information that would be unavailable at prediction time
influencing training or evaluation---is a long-standing failure mode in
data mining~\cite{kaufman2012leakage} and a documented driver of the
reproducibility crisis across ML-based science, where Kapoor and
Narayanan~\cite{kapoor2023leakage} catalogue eight leakage types and
propose structured reporting. Within KT, the leakage discussion has so
far centred on \emph{label leakage} in expanded KC sequences: \texttt{pyKT}
\cite{liu2022pykt} shows that one-by-one evaluation over same-question KCs
inflates AUC, and Badran and Preisach~\cite{badran2024labelleakage}
demonstrate that the leakage persists during training and propose masking
remedies. These works address leakage through the \emph{interaction
sequence}. The complementary channel we study---held-out information
entering through the \emph{auxiliary concept graph}---is not covered by
sequence-level fixes and remains under-audited.

\textbf{Limitations of this line for our setting.}
KT benchmarks rarely audit auxiliary graph provenance or per-\KC{}
behaviour; preprocessing and split choices can shift rankings
\cite{liu2022pykt}. Our protocol standardises fold-specific graphs and
cold-start reporting rather than proposing another sequence backbone.

\subsection{Graph-based Knowledge Tracing and Prerequisite Structure}
\label{sec:related-graphkt}
Graph-KT models propagate mastery over fixed or learned KC graphs
(GKT, SKT, GIKT, DyGKT, DGEKT)~\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt,yang2021gikt,dygkt2024,dgekt2024}.
Prerequisite-learning work infers directed relations from logs, content, or
expert annotation~\cite{lin2016moocprereq,bai2025survey,chang2015junyi}.

\textbf{Limitations of this line for our setting.}
Graph-KT papers typically \emph{assume} a concept graph is given and
optimise predictive loss, leaving graph construction outside the
experimental factorisation. Original GKT and SKT pipelines pool transition
statistics over the full interaction log before learner splitting
\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt}; our protocol isolates that construction
choice from backbone design. When graphs are inferred from the same log
used for evaluation, held-out transitions can influence edges---a
channel distinct from label leakage in the KT loss. Prerequisite-learning
work optimises edge quality against expert or content signals but seldom
couples inference to KT splits or reports DAG audit trails. Expert
annotations (e.g.\ Junyi~\cite{chang2015junyi,junyi_dataset}) and
behavioural inference capture related but non-identical structure; treating
one as a proxy for the other is empirically unsupported without
cross-validation. Our protocol keeps inference fixed and auditable, then
quantifies disagreement with expert graphs where available.

\subsection{Graph Augmentation, Contrastive KT, and Reproducible Resources}
\label{sec:related-aug}
Graph SSL and contrastive KT import label-agnostic augmentations and GNN
encoders~\cite{you2020graphcl,lee2022cl4kt,kipf2017gcn}, but educational
prerequisite DAGs impose directionality that standard drops can violate.
\textbf{Limitations of this line for our setting.}
Contrastive KT rarely reports acyclicity or reachability effects; KT
reproducibility has focused on code and splits~\cite{liu2022pykt} rather
than graph audit logs. We release provenance artefacts, \DDR{} sweeps, and
cold-start tables to separate structural disruption from predictive gains.

\textbf{Positioning.}
Our work is orthogonal to inventing new KT architectures or prerequisite
inference rules. It supplies an audit layer---leakage diagnostics, DAG
pruning logs, \DDR{}, ground-truth cross-validation, and cold-start
strata---that existing graph-KT and contrastive-KT papers typically omit.

\section{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
Let $\mathcal{D}$ be a KT interaction log, $\mathcal{C}$ the set of
\KCs{}, and $\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}},
\mathcal{F}_{\text{val}},\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}})$ a learner-based
temporal split. The protocol constructs a fold-specific graph
$G_f=(\mathcal{C},\Epre^f\cup\Esim^f)$ using only
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$.

Figure~\ref{fig:framework} summarises module dependencies. Split
discipline is a hard precondition: without learner-disjoint folds,
downstream diagnostics are not interpretable. Graph construction and DAG
audit are coupled: train-only inference can introduce cycles, so pruning
must be logged before any graph-augmented KT run. Leakage metrics,
\DDR{}, cold-start strata, and ground-truth cross-validation are
\emph{parallel} audits---they do not retrain models---which keeps the
protocol lightweight but means it cannot certify every possible leakage
path (e.g.\ hidden features in proprietary pipelines).

\subsection{Formal Problem Statement and Leakage Audit}
\label{sec:formal-problem}
For each evaluation fold $f$, the protocol seeks a graph
$G_f=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}_f)$ with $\mathcal{E}_f=\Epre^f\cup\Esim^f$
that is \emph{feasible} with respect to train-only evidence and
structural constraints, then verifies low direct leakage on scalar
diagnostics. Let $\mathcal{I}_{\text{tr}}^f$ denote train-fold
interactions and $\mathcal{H}_f=\mathcal{F}_{\text{val}}^f\cup
\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^f$ held-out interactions.

\paragraph{Decision variables.}
Edge sets $\Epre^f\subseteq\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}$ and
$\Esim^f\subseteq\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}$, edge weights
$w_{ij}\ge 0$, and pruning decisions on cyclic subsets of $\Epre^f$.

\paragraph{Objective.}
The construction stage maximises retained pedagogical structure subject
to sparsity and acyclicity:
\begin{equation}
\max_{\Epre^f,\Esim^f}\;
\sum_{(i,j)\in\Epre^f} w_{ij}
\;+\;
\lambda \sum_{(i,j)\in\Esim^f} \mathrm{sim}(c_i,c_j)
\label{eq:graph-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{sim}$ is Jaccard or PMI (Section~\ref{sec:graph-sim-theory})
and $\lambda\ge 0$ trades prerequisite mass against similarity mass (in
practice $\lambda{=}0$ because the two edge types are inferred in
separate pipelines).

\paragraph{Constraints.}
\begin{align}
\text{(C1)} \;& \forall e\in\mathcal{E}_f,\;
  S_{\mathrm{hi}}(e)=\emptyset;
  \label{eq:c1}\\
\text{(C2)} \;& \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f
  = \emptyset,\notag\\
&\; \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f
  = \emptyset,\notag\\
&\; \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f
  = \emptyset;
  \label{eq:c2}\\
\text{(C3)} \;& \Epre^f \text{ is acyclic after pruning};
  \label{eq:c3}\\
\text{(C4)} \;& |\Epre^f|\le K,\;
  |\mathrm{Out}_{\Epre^f}(i)|\le k;\notag\\
&\; \mathrm{sim}(c_i,c_j)\ge\tau \ \forall (i,j)\in\Esim^f;
  \label{eq:c4}\\
\text{(C5)} \;& \mathrm{TBMR}_f = 0;\notag\\
&\;\text{(no post-cutoff train transitions in edge evidence).}
  \label{eq:c5}
\end{align}
C1--C2 block cross-fold and cross-learner contamination; C3--C4 encode
the DAG audit and top-$K$/top-$k$ filters of Section~\ref{sec:graph};
C5 is checked by Eq.~\eqref{eq:tbvr}.

\paragraph{Audit functionals (diagnostics, not training losses).}
Because C1 cannot always be verified from AUC alone, the protocol
reports four scalar functionals on $G_f$:
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ecr-flag}),
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ecr-overlap}),
$|\rho_f|$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho-edge-outcome}), and
$\mathrm{TBMR}_f$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:tbvr}). We treat them in three tiers:
\textbf{primary pass/fail}---fold-specific provenance logs,
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$, and (when available) ground-truth
cross-validation; \textbf{empirical bounds}---train-only versus full-log
$\Delta$AUC/ACC and cold-start strata; \textbf{sanity/stress}---
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$, $|\rho_f|$, and $\mathrm{TBMR}_f$.
Under learner-disjoint splits, $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$ is
expected and does not by itself certify graph quality; near-unity overlap
can occur when curricula repeat across disjoint learners. $\mathrm{TBMR}_f$
quantifies \emph{within-train} temporal mixing (Section~\ref{sec:leakage}),
not cross-split leakage. High AUC does not certify absence of leakage; the
tiered diagnostics complement discrimination scores.

\subsection{Leakage Control}
\label{sec:leakage}
We use leakage to mean any information path by which held-out labels,
held-out interactions, future timestamps, learner identities, or graph
evidence influence graph construction, feature construction, model
selection, or evaluation, although such information would not be
legitimately available at the corresponding prediction point. This
follows the learn--predict separation view of data leakage in data
mining~\cite{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage} and is especially
relevant in knowledge tracing, where label leakage in expanded KC
sequences can inflate reported performance~\cite{liu2022pykt,badran2024labelleakage}.

We audit five leakage classes at the level of \emph{edge provenance} in KC
graphs (Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy}), specialising data-mining
leakage taxonomy~\cite{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage} and
complementing sequence-level label leakage~\cite{liu2022pykt,badran2024labelleakage}.
Structural, edge, target, temporal, and cold-start-neighbourhood classes
cover learner pooling, held-out transition support, outcome-conditioned
graph artefacts, boundary violations, and tail-\KC{} edges dominated by
non-train mass.

Each inferred edge is logged with its fold of origin, timestamp range,
relation type, and train-only flag; severe violations halt the pipeline.
In addition, we report four scalar \emph{direct} leakage diagnostics that do
not reduce to predictive accuracy. Let $\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f$,
$\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f$, and $\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f$ denote the
learner-id sets in train, validation, and test under fold $f$. The learner overlap flag is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f := \mathbb{1}\big[
(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f) \cup
(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f) \cup
(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f)
\neq \emptyset
\big]\,.
\label{eq:ecr-flag}
\end{equation}
When folds are learner-disjoint, $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f=0$ for every fold---the regime enforced below.

Let $\mathcal{E}_f$ denote the graph edge set (prerequisite and similarity) consumed when evaluating fold
$f$, and let $\mathcal{H}_f = \mathcal{F}_{\text{val}}^{f} \cup
\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^{f}$ denote held-out interactions under that
split. For edge $e$, let $S_{\text{hi}}(e)$ be held-out interaction tokens counted
toward $e$'s evidence and
$\chi_{\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}}(e)=\mathbb{1}[|S_{\text{hi}}(e)|>0]$.
The held-out pattern overlap rate is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}_f|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_f}
\chi_{\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}}(e)\,.
\label{eq:ecr-overlap}
\end{equation}
For Edge--Outcome Correlation, stack normalized edge weights into
$\mathbf{w}_f\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{E}_f|}$ (for ties, use the order
exported by the audit log). Let $\mathbf{y}_f\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{E}_f|}$
carry test-fold outcome summaries aligned to each edge---for example,
endpoint averages of correctness on $\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^{f}$ or
their logits---computed \emph{after} graph construction (labels are used
only for this diagnostic, not for inferring $\mathcal{E}_f$).
The primary edge--outcome diagnostic is the Pearson correlation
\begin{equation}
\rho_f \;=\; \mathrm{Corr}(\mathbf{w}_f,\mathbf{y}_f)\,,
\label{eq:rho-edge-outcome}
\end{equation}
computed over edges with defined variance; $|\rho_f|\!\approx\!0$ indicates
that retained edge mass is statistically unrelated to test-fold outcome
summaries.

Let $t_{u,i}$ be the timestamp of interaction $i$ for learner $u$ and
let $\tau_u$ be $u$'s temporal split cutoff for fold $f$. Write
$\mathcal{I}_{u,f}^{>}=\{\, i : t_{u,i}>\tau_u \,\}$ for post-boundary
interactions and $\mathcal{V}_{u,f}$ for the subset of those tokens that
incorrectly appear in any multiset counted toward $\mathcal{E}_f$ or in
graph-derived features at inference for fold $f$. The within-train
Temporal Boundary Mixing Rate is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TBMR}_f \;=\;
\frac{\sum_u |\mathcal{V}_{u,f}|}{\sum_u |\mathcal{I}_{u,f}^{>}|}\,.
\label{eq:tbvr}
\end{equation}
We instantiate $\tau_u$ within $\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$ at the
$70\%$ chronological split of each learner's train sequence.
$\mathrm{TBMR}_f$ is therefore a \emph{within-train temporal stress
statistic}: it measures how much edge evidence is supported by the late tail
of each learner's \emph{training} sequence after an internal chronological
cutoff. It must not be read as cross-fold leakage
($\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$ already certifies learner-disjoint
splits). Elevated $\mathrm{TBMR}_f$ on dense multi-skill corpora (e.g.\
XES3G5M) flags reliance on late-train co-assessment patterns, not contamination
from validation/test learners.

\subsection{Graph Representation for Knowledge Tracing}
\label{sec:graph-repr}
We represent each fold's knowledge structure as a directed graph
$G_f=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{E}_f)$ where nodes $c\in\mathcal{C}$
are \KCs{} (hashed identifiers in the released pipeline), and edges carry
relation type $r\in\mathcal{R}=\{\text{pre}, \text{sim}\}$ when that
relation is instantiated on the corpus (prerequisite edges on all benchmarks;
similarity edges only where the Q-matrix yields non-empty overlap). Prerequisite edges $(i,j)\in\Epre^f$ encode directed
precedence inferred from temporal transitions; similarity edges
$(i,j)\in\Esim^f$ encode co-assessment structure from the train-fold
Q-matrix. Each edge stores provenance fields (fold id, train-only flag,
source tag, support count, weight $w_{ij}$) exported as CSV for audit.

For graph-augmented KT baselines, $G_f$ is materialised as a row-normalised
adjacency matrix $A_f\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{C}|\times|\mathcal{C}|}$
(\path{pykt_graph_matrix.py}): prerequisite and similarity relations can be
merged or consumed separately depending on the backbone (GKT uses a single
KC graph; GIKT additionally uses question--KC bipartite structure from the
train fold). Node features are not learned at graph-construction time;
the protocol treats $G_f$ as a \emph{structural prior} consumed by downstream
KT optimisers. This separation mirrors GNN practice on citation or
knowledge graphs~\cite{kipf2017gcn,hamilton2017graphsage} but with
education-specific relation semantics and fold conditioning.

Technology stack: graph construction is NumPy/pandas/NetworkX; cycle
detection uses Johnson's algorithm~\cite{johnson1975finding}; KT training
optionally delegates to \texttt{pyKT}~\cite{liu2022pykt} with exported
NPZ adjacency. All artefacts are versioned per dataset and fold under
\path{data/graphs/<dataset>/fold_<f>/}.

\subsection{Train-only Graph Construction}
\label{sec:graph}
\paragraph{Prerequisite edges $\Epre$.}
For uniformity across heterogeneous benchmarks, we infer $\Epre$ from
train-only temporal transitions on \emph{all} three datasets,
independently of whether an external prerequisite source is also
available for that dataset. This deliberate choice decouples the audit
pipeline from dataset-specific quality of expert annotations and
ensures that every reported edge originates from a single, auditable
inference rule. When an expert annotation is available for a benchmark
(here, Junyi Academy), the protocol additionally performs ground-truth
cross-validation as a separate audit step (Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}).

Concretely, for each train-fold interaction sequence we extract
consecutive KC transitions $c_{t} \rightarrow c_{t+1}$ with
$c_{t} \neq c_{t+1}$, count their support $s(c_i, c_j)$, and retain
candidate edges in three filtering stages:
(i) a support-quantile filter that drops transitions below the
$q$-th percentile (default $q = 0.95$);
(ii) a per-source top-$k$ cap (default $k = 5$) that retains, for each
source KC, only the $k$ destination \KCs{} with highest support; and
(iii) a global top-$K$ cap on the number of retained candidate edges
(default $K = 5{,}000$). Each retained edge carries the normalized
support weight $w_{ij} = s(c_i, c_j) / s_{\max}$ used by the cycle
pruning step in Section~\ref{sec:dag-audit}. This candidate edge set
is then passed unchanged to the DAG audit; cycle pruning operates only
on the candidates returned by stages (i)--(iii).

\paragraph{Similarity edges $\Esim$ and theoretical properties.}
\label{sec:graph-sim-theory}
We compute $\Esim$ from a train-only Q-matrix using either Jaccard or
PMI co-occurrence at the item level (default Jaccard with threshold
$\tau{=}0.10$). Let $I(c)\subseteq\mathcal{Q}$ be the train-fold item
set tagged with concept $c$, with $n=|\mathcal{Q}|$ items:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{sim}_J(c_i,c_j) &=
  \frac{|I(c_i)\cap I(c_j)|}{|I(c_i)\cup I(c_j)|},
  \label{eq:sim-jaccard}\\
\mathrm{sim}_{\mathrm{PMI}}(c_i,c_j) &=
  \log \frac{p(c_i,c_j)}{p(c_i)\,p(c_j)},
  \label{eq:sim-pmi}\notag\\
&\quad p(c)=\tfrac{|I(c)|}{n},\notag\\
&\quad p(c_i,c_j)=\tfrac{|I(c_i)\cap I(c_j)|}{n}.
\end{align}
Jaccard similarity is bounded in $[0,1]$ and yields no $\Esim$ when items are
single-skill (ASSISTments~2012; Section~\ref{sec:exp-coldstart}). Full
operator definitions and subgraph sampling details are in Supplementary
Section~S4. All graph artefacts are materialised per dataset and fold.
\subsection{DAG Audit}
\label{sec:dag-audit}
For each fold-specific prerequisite edge set, we check whether $\Epre$
is acyclic. If cycles are detected, we iteratively remove the
lowest-confidence edge in a detected cycle and re-run the check until a
topological ordering exists. Cycle detection uses a bounded simple-cycle enumeration procedure based on
Johnson's algorithm for enumerating elementary circuits in directed graphs \cite{johnson1975finding}, capped at 100 representative cycles per detection round. We use this cap because the number of elementary cycles can grow rapidly with graph density; exhaustive enumeration is unnecessary for our
audit, since the pruning loop only requires representative cycle witnesses. The detector cap does not affect
correctness: the pruning loop terminates only when no further cycle can
be detected, so the final retained graph is acyclic. Each removed edge
is written to
\path{results/reports/<dataset>_dag_pruning_log.csv} with its source,
target, confidence score, and pruning reason. The audit reports node
count, raw edge count, pruned edge count, retained edge count, root
count, leaf count, and final topological-sort status. The claim is not
that the retained DAG is the unique correct prerequisite hierarchy, but
that the pruning rule and edge provenance are explicit and reproducible.
\subsection{DAG Disruption Rate and Augmentation Operators}
\label{sec:ddr}
To quantify the structural effect of graph augmentations on prerequisite
DAGs, we define the DAG Disruption Rate (\DDR{}). Let $A$ be an
augmentation operator and $A(\Epre,p)$ the edge set obtained at
perturbation strength $p$. Let $\mathrm{pre}(A(\Epre,p))\subseteq\Epre$
be the original prerequisite edges that remain with their original
direction, and let $\mathrm{rev}(A(\Epre,p))$ be the edges that appear
with the opposite direction. We define
\begin{equation}
\DDR(A,p)=
\frac{|\Epre\setminus \mathrm{pre}(A(\Epre,p))|
      +|\mathrm{rev}(A(\Epre,p))|}
     {|\Epre|}.
\label{eq:ddr}
\end{equation}
We evaluate four label-agnostic families from GraphCL~\cite{you2020graphcl}
(\texttt{attr\_mask}, \texttt{edge\_drop}, random-walk \texttt{subgraph},
\texttt{node\_drop}) plus one prerequisite-preserving operator
(\texttt{prereq\_preserve}) that removes only transitively redundant edges at
the same per-edge budget as \texttt{edge\_drop} (Section~\ref{sec:exp-ddr};
formal definitions in Supplementary Section~S4).
Each operator is swept over $p\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20,0.30\}$ with three
random seeds on each of three train folds ($3\times 3=9$ runs per
setting); reported means aggregate across those runs. \DDR{} is a structural diagnostic only: it measures how
much prerequisite structure is removed or reversed by an augmentation,
not whether a downstream KT model becomes better or worse.
\subsection{Cold-start KC Stratification}
\label{sec:cold-start}
We define cold-start at the KC level, distinct from user/item
cold-start in recommender systems~\cite{schein2002coldstart}. For fold
$f$, let $n_f(c)=|\{(u,t,q,c,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f\}|$ be
the number of train-fold interactions tagged with \KC{} $c$. Each KC
is assigned to one of four frequency strata:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{S}_1&:\ n_f(c)<20,\\
\mathcal{S}_2&:\ 20\le n_f(c)<100,\\
\mathcal{S}_3&:\ 100\le n_f(c)<500,\\
\mathcal{S}_4&:\ n_f(c)\ge 500.
\end{aligned}
\]
Strata are assigned from train-fold counts only; test rows inherit their
\KC{} stratum and aggregate AUC/ACC/NLL per stratum. This exposes tail-\KC{}
behaviour that aggregate metrics hide and links rare-\KC{} edge support to
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$ (Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy}).
\subsection{Ground-Truth Cross-Validation}
\label{sec:gtxv-protocol}
When a benchmark provides an expert prerequisite annotation, the
protocol performs an additional, optional audit step: comparing the
train-only inferred $\Epre$ against the expert edge set
$\Epre^{\text{expert}}$. The procedure has four stages:
(i) load the expert annotation and filter rows by a prerequisite-score
threshold $\theta$ to obtain a directed expert edge set;
(ii) align expert KC identifiers to the pipeline's hashed \texttt{kc\_id}
space via a deterministic name-to-id mapping reconstructed from the
preprocessing hash function;
(iii) sweep top-$K$ cutoffs over the inferred edge set and compute,
at each $K$: edge precision/recall/F1, direction agreement, reachability
F1 (over transitive closure), and node Jaccard;
(iv) extract qualitative examples of disagreement in three categories:
edges present only in inferred, edges present only in expert, and edges
with opposite direction.
The procedure is read-only with respect to the pipeline; no
inferred edge is added or removed based on the comparison.

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}
\paragraph{Benchmark roles.}
\textbf{XES3G5M}~\cite{xes3g5m2023} is our \emph{primary} public benchmark for
model-comparison claims: low consecutive KC-repeat (${\approx}21\%$,
Supplementary Section~S3), non-saturated headline AUC (${\approx}0.875$ for
\textit{simpleKT}), and the widest separation among graph backbones under
train-only graphs. \textbf{ASSISTments~2012} is \emph{secondary}: it
stress-tests leakage ablation, cold-start strata, and DAG audit on a
single-skill Q-matrix ($|\Esim|{=}0$). \textbf{Junyi Academy}
\cite{junyi_dataset} is a \emph{saturated sanity} corpus for ground-truth
cross-validation and dense $\Epre$ topology; near-ceiling AUC
(${\approx}0.98$--$0.984$) makes it unsuitable for headline graph-benefit
claims. Synthetic~C2/C5 are controlled tiny-graph sanity checks.

We instantiate the protocol on all five corpora. Junyi ships an
expert-curated prerequisite annotation at the exercise-pair level.
\emph{In the main inference branch we do not consume this expert DAG}:
$\Epre$ for Junyi is built by the same train-only temporal inference rule
used for ASSISTments and XES3G5M (Section~\ref{sec:graph}). The expert DAG
enters only the read-only ground-truth cross-validation sub-protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}). ASSISTments provides a Q-matrix but no
external prerequisite graph. XES3G5M provides hierarchical KC metadata parsed
at the sequence level; $\Epre$ is inferred by the shared train-only rule.

train-only versus full-log ablation (Section~\ref{sec:exp-graph-ablation}),
and is why we anchor model-comparison claims on XES3G5M rather than on
Junyi/ASSIST headline AUC.

On the three public benchmarks, $|\Delta$AUC| and $|\Delta$ACC stay at

($|\Delta\text{AUC}|{=}0.002$ for GKT). Per-model pairs appear in
Supplementary Tables~S6--S10. Under our support quantile ($q{=}0.95$),
top-$k$ cap, and $K{=}5000$ filter, pooling learners before edge inference
rarely changes surviving transitions on public benchmarks. Train-only
construction is therefore \emph{not} penalising headline metrics relative
to this deliberate full-log variant on those corpora, without proving absence
of leakage in general.

Figure~\ref{fig:ddr-junyi} and Table~\ref{tab:ddr-raw} show five
consistent regimes across the three benchmarks. First, attribute
masking leaves $\Epre$ structurally unchanged and therefore gives
$\DDR{}=0$ across perturbation strengths by construction. Second,
random-walk subgraph sampling preserves connectivity inside the
sampled subgraph and disrupts $\Epre$ \emph{sub-linearly} in $p$:
because edges are removed only at the boundary of the visited
subgraph, the disruption is consistently smaller than under
edge dropping at the same $p$ (e.g.\ on Junyi, $\DDR{} = 0.16$ for
subgraph vs.\ $0.20$ for edge drop at $p=0.20$). Third, edge dropping
disrupts prerequisite structure approximately linearly in $p$, as
expected when each edge is removed independently. Fourth, node
dropping produces the largest disruption because removing a node also
removes all incident prerequisite edges; at $p = 0.30$, node dropping
disrupts roughly half of the prerequisite structure on all three
benchmarks ($\DDR{} \approx 0.52$--$0.54$).

\paragraph{\DDR{} is a usable design target, not a tautology.}
A natural objection to the four label-agnostic families is that their
\DDR{} ordering merely re-states their definitions: an operator that
deletes more edges disrupts more structure. To test whether \DDR{}
\emph{discriminates} structure-aware from structure-agnostic
augmentation rather than re-describing edge counts, we add a fifth,
prerequisite-preserving operator (\texttt{prereq\_preserve}). It draws
the same per-edge budget $p$ as \texttt{edge\_drop}, but only removes
edges that are \emph{transitively redundant}---those whose endpoints
remain connected through an alternative directed path---so that every
reachability/precedence relation and the entire transitive-reduction
backbone are preserved. Crucially, unlike \texttt{attr\_mask}, this
operator is \emph{not} $\DDR{=}0$ by construction: its disruption is an
empirical quantity equal to $p$ times the redundant-edge fraction of the
graph. At every dataset and strength, \texttt{prereq\_preserve} is
strictly less disruptive than \texttt{edge\_drop} at a matched budget
(at $p=0.30$, $0.278$ vs.\ $0.301$ on Junyi, $0.262$ vs.\ $0.309$ on
ASSISTments, and $0.233$ vs.\ $0.299$ on XES3G5M). The size of this gap
is \emph{data-dependent}---a $\sim 8\%$ relative reduction on Junyi but
$\sim 22\%$ on XES3G5M---and is determined by graph redundancy rather
than by the operator definition. Because temporal-precedence inference
generates many transitive shortcuts (if $a$ precedes $b$ precedes $c$,
the edge $a\!\to\!c$ is also counted), the inferred graphs are
highly redundant ($\approx 78$--$92\%$ of edges lie off the transitive
reduction), which both explains the modest gap and shows that \DDR{}
responds to operator structure-awareness in a quantitative,
non-tautological way. \DDR{} can therefore serve as a concrete design
target: an augmentation that respects prerequisite directionality is
measurably rewarded with lower disruption at the same perturbation
budget.

The relative ordering
$\DDR(\text{attr\_mask}) < \DDR(\text{subgraph}) \lesssim
\DDR(\text{prereq\_preserve}) < \DDR(\text{edge\_drop}) <
\DDR(\text{node\_drop})$
is consistent across all three benchmarks and all four perturbation
strengths. We read these results as a structural diagnostic, not as a
claim that any augmentation is beneficial or harmful for a downstream
KT learner. They indicate that prerequisite-aware augmentation design
is needed when graph augmentations are applied to educational DAGs:
node-level operators are an order of magnitude more disruptive than
attribute-level operators, with subgraph sampling and the
prerequisite-preserving operator occupying an intermediate,
structure-preserving regime.

\subsection{Cold-start KC Diagnostic}
\label{sec:exp-coldstart}
\input{results/tables/cold_start_summary.tex}
Table~\ref{tab:cold-start-summary} shows that aggregate AUC can hide

sequence leaders on synthetic hot rows, reinforcing model-specific graph benefit.

\paragraph{Similarity edges and dataset properties.}
The similarity-edge rule of Section~\ref{sec:graph} is sensitive to the
multi-skill density of the Q-matrix. On the train fold of
ASSISTments~2012, $100\%$ of items are tagged with exactly one KC, so
$|I(c_i) \cap I(c_j)| = 0$ for every distinct concept pair, and both
Jaccard and PMI yield no edges. On XES3G5M, $86.6\%$ of train items are
single-skill but the remaining multi-skill items generate $354$
similarity edges at the default Jaccard threshold of $0.10$. We treat
this dataset-property dependency as a first-class outcome of the audit:
a similarity rule appropriate for multi-skill benchmarks may need to be
replaced or supplemented (e.g., by response-pattern correlation) on
single-skill benchmarks. This is not a pipeline failure but a structural
observation that protocol papers should report.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{Model-specific graph benefit under leakage-controlled evaluation}
\label{sec:disc-model-specific}
The baseline summary (Table~\ref{tab:baseline-summary}) and fold-level
diagnostics (Table~\ref{tab:baseline-cv}) support an empirical observation
at the level of the protocol itself. We anchor the discussion on
\textbf{XES3G5M} (primary; Section~\ref{sec:datasets}) because its lower
KC-repeat and non-saturated AUC make backbone separation interpretable.
Under leakage-controlled evaluation (train-only $\Epre$, optional train-only
$\Esim$ where populated, learner-based temporal split), explicit concept-graph
benefit is \emph{model-specific} rather than uniform. On XES3G5M,
\textit{simpleKT} averages $0.875$ AUC while GKT averages $0.834$
(mean $\Delta{\approx}{-}0.041$ across three folds; fold stds in
Table~\ref{tab:baseline-cv}). SKT ($0.625$) and DyGKT ($0.752$) trail far
more severely. GIKT ($0.878$) slightly \emph{exceeds} the sequence leader
($+0.003$ mean AUC). On ASSISTments (secondary), the same ordering direction
holds but magnitudes shrink (GKT trails by ${\approx}0.007$ AUC with
near-ceiling leaders). On saturated Junyi, all models cluster within
${\approx}0.002$--$0.006$ AUC of \textit{simpleKT} and should not drive
deployment conclusions. We report Supplementary Table~S11 $p$-values for
completeness but treat \emph{fold-level $\Delta$AUC magnitudes} as the
primary evidence because three-fold CV yields underpowered inferential tests
($p_{\min}{=}0.25$ for Wilcoxon; fragile $t$-tests on $n{=}3$ folds).

We do not claim that graph-augmented KT is uninformative; the protocol
removes a specific channel through which held-out information can reach the
model---the concept graph itself. Three non-mutually-exclusive hypotheses
are consistent with the observed ordering.

\paragraph{H1 -- Leakage in prior evaluations.}
A common practice in graph-augmented KT is to compute graph statistics
from the full interaction log prior to train/test splitting. Under such
practice, graph-augmented models can observe test-fold transitions through
the graph during training. Our protocol enforces fold-specific edge
inference and provenance logging. Table~\ref{tab:graph-ablation}
instantiates the controlled comparison: identical learner splits and
evaluation code, but graph statistics pooled over the full preprocessed log.
Headline metrics on the three public benchmarks shift by at most
$|\Delta\text{AUC}|{\leq}0.003$; Synthetic~C5 shows a modest shift
($|\Delta\text{AUC}|{=}0.002$). H1 remains relevant as a \emph{risk hypothesis} about workflows that leak
held-out mass into graph construction; it is not a causal claim that we have
confirmed on these benchmarks. Table~\ref{tab:graph-ablation} caps how much
headline leakage can explain the observed GKT deficit here
($|\Delta\text{AUC}|{\leq}0.003$ on public corpora). Architectures that exploit
cross-split pooling more aggressively might show larger shifts.

\paragraph{H2 -- Aggregate-metric masking.}
The cold-start diagnostic (Section~\ref{sec:exp-coldstart}) shows that
per-stratum behaviour can differ from aggregate behaviour.
Table~\ref{tab:cold-start-summary} makes this concrete: on real data, the
best graph-aware model is slightly better in the \emph{very\_cold} stratum,
essentially tied in \emph{cold}, and worse in \emph{warm}/\emph{hot}. On
Synthetic~C2/C5, where all test interactions are hot, GIKT/DGEKT improve over
sequence-only baselines, but this reflects clean synthetic structure rather
than cold-start relief.

\paragraph{H3 -- Strong-backbone substitution.}
Modern attention-based backbones such as AKT and \textit{simpleKT} may
already capture much relational information through long-range temporal
attention. Additional structural signal at the concept-embedding level may
then yield diminishing returns---a pattern weaker temporal backbones should
benefit from more, all else equal.

These hypotheses are not exhaustive. Distinguishing among them requires
re-running prior graph-KT papers under our protocol, which is beyond this
paper's scope. The narrower observation is that \emph{under leakage-controlled
evaluation, the ranking between graph-augmented and sequence-only KT can
differ from settings that pool graph evidence across splits}, and
benchmarking practice should treat graph provenance as a first-order factor.

\paragraph{Why this strengthens the protocol.}
If graph-augmented KT reliably outperformed sequence-only baselines
regardless of graph provenance, leakage control would be secondary. The
observation that sequence-only pyKT checkpoints match or exceed GKT on
XES3G5M (primary) and ASSISTments under train-only graphs, while corrected
GIKT remains competitive on XES3G5M without a train-only/full-log gap,
together with edge provenance auditing, elevates leakage control from hygiene
to a deployment and benchmarking precondition. Combined with Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}, which shows that
inferred and expert prerequisite signals are structurally distinct, graph
quality cannot be assumed---it must be audited.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
Baseline ordering uses three-fold means; DAG audit and GT cross-validation
refer to fold~0 exports by default. GKT, AKT, DKT, and \textit{simpleKT} are
stock pyKT~\cite{liu2022pykt} checkpoints; GIKT, SKT, DyGKT, and DGEKT are
native implementations in our fork (\path{src/models/}). Stronger graph-KT
architectures~\cite{tong2020skt,dygkt2024,dgekt2024} may behave differently;
auditing them is an explicit follow-up.

\subsection{Broader implications}
\label{sec:disc-implications}

\paragraph{Scalability and broader domains.}
The protocol is dataset-agnostic: cost scales with train-fold transition
counting and capped cycle enumeration, not KT training. Sparsity caps keep
audit tractable on million-scale logs~\cite{choi2020ednet}. The similarity
rule may need domain-specific co-occurrence when Q-matrices are single-skill;
GT cross-validation generalises wherever expert DAGs exist; \DDR{} applies to
any directed curriculum graph consumed by KT or pretraining pipelines.

\paragraph{Implications for graph-enhanced KT.}
The protocol shows that KC graph construction should be treated as a
first-class experimental component rather than an unexamined
preprocessing step. Fold-specific edge inference, provenance logging,
and DAG validation make it possible to identify leakage risks and graph
construction choices that would otherwise remain hidden. The \DDR{}
results further show that generic graph augmentations can substantially
disturb prerequisite structure, especially when they operate at the node
level, whereas a prerequisite-preserving operator that protects the
transitive-reduction backbone is measurably less disruptive at the same
perturbation budget. The exploratory DGEKT-only downstream stress test in
Section~\ref{sec:exp-ddr-downstream} adds an important qualification:
on \emph{this} backbone and on ASSISTments/XES3G5M only, large structural
disruption did not produce a measurable AUC drop in our retraining sweep.
We do not extrapolate that null predictive sensitivity to GIKT, GKT, SKT,
DyGKT, or other graph consumers. Future augmentation papers should report
both structural damage (\DDR{}) and backbone-specific downstream
retraining evidence.
\paragraph{Cold-start reporting.}
The cold-start diagnostic shows why KT studies should not rely only on
aggregate AUC. Rare \KCs{} may behave differently from frequent
\KCs{}, and apparent gains or high scores on small strata may reflect
class imbalance rather than genuine modelling improvement. Reporting
per-stratum metrics gives reviewers a clearer view of whether a method
is robust across the KC frequency distribution. In our results,
graph-aware models show their narrowest real-data advantage in the
very-cold stratum, while synthetic hot-stratum gains reflect clean
concept structure rather than cold-start relief.
Figure~\ref{fig:kt-graph-junyi} links this diagnostic to concrete graph
topology on the densest benchmark; sparser $\Epre$ on ASSISTments and
XES3G5M (Supplementary Figure~S1) still exhibit cold strata.

\paragraph{Behavioural versus expert prerequisite curation.}
The ground-truth cross-validation on Junyi
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}) shows that train-only behavioural
inference and expert prerequisite annotation are correlated at the
node level but structurally distinct at the edge level. Two
implications follow. First, behavioural inference cannot be treated
as a low-cost approximation of expert curation, and papers that
report graph-augmented KT results on behaviourally-inferred graphs
should not claim those results transfer directly to settings with
expert prerequisite graphs (or vice versa). Second, the two signals
are complementary: in the most informative setup, a graph-augmented
KT model would consume both an inferred behavioural graph and an
expert prerequisite graph as separate relations, rather than treating
either as a substitute for the other. We do not test this combined
setting in the present paper, but the cross-validation provides the
quantitative basis for proposing it.
\paragraph{Implications for graph-KT benchmarking.}
The empirical observation in Section~\ref{sec:disc-model-specific} has direct
implications for how the graph-KT subfield reports gains. If
leakage-controlled evaluation changes the relative ranking of
graph-augmented and sequence-only KT---and our three-fold XES3G5M
(primary) and ASSISTments diagnostics show it can on non-saturated
corpora---then benchmark tables that do not control for graph-
construction provenance are not directly comparable across papers. We
do not advocate dismissing reported gains in the graph-KT literature;
we advocate \emph{disaggregating} them. A reported AUC improvement of
$\Delta$ on a benchmark should ideally be decomposed into three
components: the part that survives leakage control, the part that
depends on graph-construction choices (relation type, threshold, edge
cap, encoder), and the part that depends on backbone strength. The
protocol in this paper supplies the first component; the cold-start
diagnostic supplies a partial view of the second; the third remains an
open methodological question.

A practical recommendation follows. When introducing a new
graph-augmented KT model, papers should report at minimum: (a) the
fold-specific provenance of every retained graph edge; (b) the
cold-start stratum on which the gain is most concentrated; (c) the
sequence-only baseline matched in compute and hyperparameter budget;
(d) when an expert prerequisite annotation is available, the
ground-truth cross-validation of the consumed graph against that
annotation, using the edge / direction / reachability / node-Jaccard
metric family used in Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}; and (e) whether
accuracy is actually sensitive to structure-preserving versus
structure-damaging graph perturbations. Without these reports, an
aggregate-AUC improvement is difficult to attribute. The protocol
presented here makes these reports tractable.

\subsection{Threats to Validity and Metric Interpretation}
\label{sec:threats-validity}
Three artefacts invite misreading: the near-null full-log ablation
(Table~\ref{tab:graph-ablation}), aggressive cycle pruning
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-audit}), and scalar leakage metrics that are near-degenerate under learner-disjoint
splits (Table~\ref{tab:leakage-metrics}).

The ablation null shift does \emph{not} negate leakage control: graph
statistics must not pool held-out learners regardless of backbone
sensitivity. Under our filters, pooling rarely changes surviving transitions on
public benchmarks; synthetic logs remain more sensitive. We therefore tier audit
outputs (Section~\ref{sec:formal-problem}): \textbf{primary}---provenance logs,
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$, and GT cross-validation when available;
\textbf{empirical bounds}---full-log ablation $\Delta$ and cold-start strata;
\textbf{sanity/stress}---$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$, $|\rho_f|$, and
$\mathrm{TBMR}_f$. Near-unity overlap and $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$
are expected under honest learner-disjoint construction and do not replace
provenance or GT audits.

Cycle pruning resolves conflicts by dropping the lowest-weight edge per detected
cycle (Johnson enumeration, capped at 100 witnesses per round); pruned mass
reflects weak or bidirectional transitions rather than arbitrary deletion.

$\mathrm{TBMR}_f$ is a within-train temporal stress statistic at a $70\%$
chronological boundary inside each learner's train fold---not cross-split
leakage ($\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$). Dense multi-skill
co-assessment on XES3G5M and toy-graph structure on Synthetic~C2 explain the
largest values.

The audit also caught a one-step label misalignment in an early GIKT head
(\path{src/models/gikt.py}), flagged by inconsistent fold AUCs and corrected
before reporting Table~\ref{tab:baseline-cv}.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Three-fold CV limits inferential power ($p_{\min}{=}0.25$ for Wilcoxon;
fragile paired $t$-tests on $n{=}3$ folds). Model-comparison claims in
Section~\ref{sec:disc-model-specific} therefore rely on fold-level $\Delta$AUC
magnitudes (Table~\ref{tab:baseline-cv}); Supplementary Table~S11 reports
paired tests for transparency only. ASSISTments yields no $\Esim$ under item-overlap
Jaccard; GT cross-validation is Junyi-only at $\theta{=}0.5$; downstream
\DDR{} retraining covers DGEKT only; calibration analysis is deferred. We do not
claim other architectures would show the same null full-log shift.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}
We presented a leakage-controlled audit protocol for constructing and
evaluating concept graphs in graph-enhanced KT. The contribution targets
\textit{Applied Intelligence} as a deployment and reproducibility layer: a
practitioner checklist, provenance artefacts, tiered leakage diagnostics,
\DDR{} augmentation metrics, ground-truth cross-validation where expert graphs
exist, and cold-start KC stratification. On XES3G5M (primary), ASSISTments
(secondary), and Junyi (saturated sanity), the audit produces acyclic
train-only prerequisite graphs after transparent cycle pruning. The \DDR{}
sweep separates augmentation families into consistent structural regimes
across all three public corpora. The prerequisite-preserving operator is
strictly less disruptive than edge dropping at a matched budget, confirming
that \DDR{} rewards structure-aware augmentation design. An exploratory
DGEKT-only downstream stress test on ASSISTments and XES3G5M shows that this
structural ordering does not automatically imply predictive sensitivity for
\emph{that} backbone ($r{=}0.13$--$0.15$; mean AUC drops near zero).

On XES3G5M, leakage-controlled auditing changes deployable backbone ranking
(GKT trails \textit{simpleKT} by ${\approx}0.041$ mean AUC; GIKT remains
competitive). Ground-truth cross-validation on Junyi shows high node overlap
but weak edge agreement. Cold-start strata reveal a narrow graph-aware
advantage only in very-cold \KCs{} on real data. Practitioners can apply the
five-item checklist in Section~\ref{sec:intro} before reporting new
graph-augmented KT results. Reproduction artefacts are at
\path{https://github.com/tuanymc/p0_project.git}.
