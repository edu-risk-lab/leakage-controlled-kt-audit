\title{Leakage-controlled concept-graph construction and cold-start
diagnostics for graph-enhanced knowledge tracing}

% Double-anonymized: do NOT list authors here. See title_page.tex.

\begin{abstract}
Intelligent tutoring systems and large-scale online practice platforms use
graph-enhanced knowledge tracing to estimate learner mastery and to schedule
practice. In current pipelines, concept graphs are often inferred from the
same interaction logs later used to train and evaluate the tracer. Held-out
learner transitions can therefore enter the model through the graph channel
even when the optimiser sees only the training fold. Across every
configuration tested here, including deliberate 20\% test-fold injection
into a graph-reliant backbone, this channel moves predictive area under
the receiver operating characteristic curve by at most 0.010. The metric
is therefore a poor detector of graph-mediated contamination; an audit
that scores graph provenance independently of accuracy is required.

\textbf{Artificial-intelligence contribution.}
We introduce a leakage-controlled audit protocol for knowledge-component
graphs. The protocol comprises learner-based temporal splits, train-only
prerequisite inference, directed-acyclic-graph validation with cycle
pruning, knowledge-component cold-start stratification, and a structural
diagnostic---the directed-acyclic-graph disruption rate---that ranks
graph-augmentation operators independently of predictive accuracy.

\textbf{Engineering application.}
We apply the protocol to the verification of graph-enhanced tracers in tutoring
software. Experiments on public educational logs (XES3G5M, ASSISTments 2012,
Junyi Academy) and two synthetic logs yield acyclic train-only graphs. Downstream
retraining yields an OLS slope of $0.084$ AUC per unit disruption rate on
graph-reliant GKT/XES3G5M, versus $0.003$ on graph-inert ASSISTments.
Replacing a pooled full-log graph with a train-only
graph changes headline area under the curve by at most 0.003, including the
opened per-source-cap cell on the primary corpus. The protocol is a
CPU-only pre-training gate (graph export plus leakage scalars; Table~\ref{tab:audit-cost}).
The intended
use is to inform graph-provenance decisions at deployment, rather than to
raise headline scores.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Knowledge tracing (KT) estimates a learner's latent mastery of
\KCs{} from interaction history, supporting adaptive practice,
intelligent tutoring, and curriculum recommendation. The field has
progressed from Bayesian Knowledge Tracing~\cite{corbett1995bkt} to
deep sequential models~\cite{piech2015dkt,zhang2017dkvmn,ghosh2020akt,liu2023simplekt,pandey2019rkt,shen2021saint} and graph-enhanced models
that propagate information over relations between
\KCs{}~\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt,yang2021gikt,dygkt2024,dgekt2024,chen2023hgkt}. These graph-based models rely on
auxiliary concept graphs, usually encoding prerequisite or similarity
relations.

We use \emph{data leakage} to mean any situation in which information
that would be unavailable at deployment time influences training or
evaluation---for example, when test-fold interactions support edges in
an auxiliary concept graph even though the KT optimiser trains only on
the training fold~\cite{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage}. We use
\emph{cold-start} in a KT-specific sense at the \KC{} level: a KC is
cold-start when it has too few train-fold interactions to support stable
parameter or neighbourhood estimates, in contrast to user- or item-level
cold-start in recommender systems~\cite{schein2002coldstart}.

Current practice leaves three reproducibility gaps that motivate our
audit. First, concept graphs are often inferred from the same logs used
to train and evaluate KT models; pooling the full log before
learner-based splitting lets held-out transitions enter the graph
channel even when sequence training is fold-clean~\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt,liu2022pykt}.
The broader ML literature documents leakage as a driver of irreproducible
claims~\cite{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage}; within KT,
\texttt{pyKT} and follow-on work address \emph{sequence-level} label
leakage~\cite{liu2022pykt,badran2024labelleakage}, but fold-specific
audit of \emph{graph provenance}---including bounded train-only sampling
when logs are very large---remains under-specified relative to sequence-level
label-leakage remedies~\cite{badran2024labelleakage}. Second, generic graph
augmentations designed for undirected graphs can disturb directional
prerequisite structure without measurement~\cite{you2020graphcl,zhu2021gca}.
Third, ``cold-start'' is used inconsistently in KT reporting, so
aggregate AUC can mask tail-\KC{} behaviour.

Two short examples make the audit concrete. (1)~\textbf{Graph-mediated
leakage (worked illustration).} Suppose a transition $c_i\!\to\!c_j$ has
support $40$ in the train fold but support $60$ once the full log is pooled,
because $20$ of the supporting traversals belong to test-fold learners. Under
a support-quantile cutoff the train-only count may fall below the threshold and be
dropped, while the pooled count clears it and the edge is retained: the graph
then encodes a transition carried mainly by held-out learners never seen by the
optimiser. The fraction of an edge's support that comes from held-out
tokens---here $20/60$---is exactly the \emph{throughput} our audit measures
through evaluation-cohort reachability
($\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}$) and the late-train evidence share
($\mathrm{LTES}$), and it is invisible to a
learner-disjoint split flag. (2)~\textbf{KC cold-start.} If a KC appears only a
handful of times in $\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$ but frequently in
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^f$, graph propagation may rely on unstable
neighbourhood evidence that aggregate metrics hide---and, as
Section~\ref{sec:disc-implications} argues, such sparse-train KCs are precisely
where a single leaked edge has the largest relative throughput.

Formally, let $\mathcal{D}=\{(u,t,q,c,y)\}$ be a KT interaction log
with learner $u$, timestamp $t$, item $q$, tagged \KC{} $c$, and
correctness label $y\in\{0,1\}$. A learner-based temporal split
$\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}},\mathcal{F}_{\text{val}},
\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}})$ assigns each learner to exactly one fold
(we use ratios $(0.7,0.1,0.2)$ in all reported experiments). For
evaluation fold $f$, the protocol must construct a fold-specific concept
graph $G_f=(\mathcal{C},\Epre^f\cup\Esim^f)$ using \emph{only}
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$, or a representative train-only sample
$\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^f_{\text{tr}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^f_{\text{train}}$
drawn entirely within that fold when logs exceed a compute budget
(Section~\ref{sec:graph}), then train a KT model on
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$ and score predictions on
$\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^f$. The central question is whether reported
graph-augmented KT gains remain valid when $G_f$ is audited for leakage,
acyclicity, and cold-start coverage.

Our central claim is a \emph{bounded negative result}. Across every
configuration tested here---train-only versus full-log pooling on three
public benchmarks, and controlled 20\% test-fold injection on
XES3G5M---headline AUC moved by at most $0.010$, including on a
graph-reliant backbone that passes a manipulation check. Because AUC
does not detect this channel under the studied regimes, an audit that
measures graph provenance independently of accuracy is required. We
still report contamination \emph{throughput} (held-out mass in the
consumed edge set) and backbone \emph{reliance} (AUC movement under
near-total graph destruction) as descriptive coordinates; the $2{\times}2$
grid in Supplementary Table~S22 organises those measurements and is
not offered as evidence of a multiplicative interaction.

\paragraph{Implications for reporting and deployment.}
The protocol changes four decisions made before reporting or deploying a
graph-augmented KT model. (1)~\emph{Build the graph fold-clean:}
infer $\Epre$/$\Esim$ only from $\mathcal{F}^f_{\text{train}}$ and export
per-edge provenance, so a reported gain cannot be silently carried by held-out
mass. (2)~\emph{Read the throughput indicators, not the accuracy:} inspect
builder mass and $\mathrm{LTES}$ (Section~\ref{sec:leakage}) to locate
the corpus in the throughput$\times$reliance grid; a high-throughput reading is
a warning even when the learner-disjoint flag remains zero. (3)~\emph{Gate any
downstream claim on a manipulation check:} before attributing accuracy to graph
structure, verify that the backbone uses the graph (destroying it must move
AUC), otherwise treat the graph as a reproducibility artefact only. (4)~\emph{Prefer
structure-preserving augmentation:} when augmenting, use the
prerequisite-preserving operator, which attains lower \DDR{} and incurs the
smallest accuracy loss on graph-reliant backbones (Section~\ref{sec:exp-ddr-downstream}). These
four operational decisions correspond to the decision map in
Table~\ref{tab:audit-actions}; five reporting items for papers are listed in
Section~\ref{sec:disc-implications}.

In workflow~(a) of Figure~\ref{fig:intro-leakage}, prerequisite or similarity
edges may be supported by validation/test interactions, so the KT module
appears to generalise while part of its structural input already encodes future
behaviour; workflow~(b) closes this channel by making graph construction a
first-class, fold-conditioned step.

Leakage-controlled concept graphs matter wherever KT systems drive
scheduling and gating decisions in ITS platforms, MOOC pipelines, and
corporate training~\cite{heffernan2006assist,junyi_dataset,choi2020ednet,xes3g5m2023}.
Without fold-specific audits, reported AUC improvements are difficult to
attribute to modelling versus preprocessing. The protocol supplies provenance
artefacts, DAG validity checks, cold-start strata, and tiered diagnostics that
inform whether and how graph-augmented KT should be deployed, including cases
in which headline accuracy does not change. That last case is the common one
here, and it is the point: a team that constructs graphs from pooled full logs
(the default in the original GKT/SKT pipelines
\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt}) without provenance logs, similarity-edge
feasibility checks, or cold-start strata can reach different reporting and risk
conclusions than one that runs this protocol first, even when the ablation
barely moves headline accuracy
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-graph-ablation}). Backbone-level diagnostic scores on
XES3G5M are reported in Section~\ref{sec:exp-baseline} under attested and
unmatched epoch caps; they are observational and are not used to support any
claim in this paper.

We address the gaps above with an audit protocol rather than a new sequential
tracer. The artificial-intelligence contribution is a leakage-controlled graph
construction and audit protocol, a structural augmentation diagnostic
(\DDR{}), ground-truth cross-validation (instantiated on Junyi
Academy~\cite{junyi_dataset,chang2015junyi}), and cold-start KC
reporting, evaluated on ASSISTments~2012 and
XES3G5M~\cite{xes3g5m2023} alongside two synthetic sanity logs. The
engineering application is verification of graph-enhanced knowledge tracing
inside intelligent tutoring pipelines. The contribution is a reproducible
audit layer around concept-graph
construction---not another sequence model~\cite{abdelrahman2023kt_survey,liu2022pykt}---that
separates structural disruption from downstream AUC, a separation that graph-KT and
contrastive-KT papers typically omit~\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,lee2022cl4kt}.

Each contribution below answers a specific limitation (L) observed in
current graph-KT practice; residual risks are stated explicitly rather
than deferred to the Discussion alone.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1 (addresses L1: graph leakage).} A train-only protocol
  for constructing and auditing KC graphs with prerequisite edges and
  optional similarity edges $G=(\mathcal{C}, \Epre \cup \Esim)$ for KT,
  including split checks, edge provenance, per-relation availability
  reporting, and DAG validation with cycle pruning.
  \emph{Residual risk:} the protocol audits declared graph provenance and
  tiered diagnostics; it does not certify every possible leakage path in
  proprietary feature pipelines.
  \item \textbf{C2 (addresses L2: augmentation disrupts DAGs).} The
  \DDR{} metric, which quantifies how generic graph augmentations disrupt
  prerequisite assumptions on educational DAGs, instantiated with four
  formally defined label-agnostic operators and one prerequisite-preserving
  operator; the latter is measurably less disruptive at a matched
  perturbation budget, showing that \DDR{} discriminates augmentation
  operators at the structural level rather than restating edge counts alone.
  \emph{Downstream evidence:} under an anchored multi-seed retraining sweep, \DDR{}
  predicts accuracy loss on a graph-reliant backbone that passes a
  manipulation check (GKT on XES3G5M: OLS slope $\DDRslopeXEScore$ AUC per unit
  \DDR{}, cluster-bootstrap 95\% CI \DDRslopeXEScoreci, $n{=}\DDRnXEScore$ on
  the $p{\le}0.3$ core; Pearson $r{\approx}0.95$ is secondary and scale-free;
  \texttt{prereq\_preserve}
  incurs the smallest AUC loss at matched budget), while remaining a purely structural
  audit on graph-inert backbones (DGEKT, and GKT on ASSISTments), where the
  corresponding slope is $\DDRslopeASSISTcore$ (CI \DDRslopeASSISTcoreci) and total
  graph destruction moves AUC by ${\le}0.003$ (Section~\ref{sec:exp-ddr-downstream}).
  \emph{Residual risk:} downstream sensitivity is thus backbone- and
  dataset-conditional; we do not claim \DDR{} predicts accuracy for graph-inert
  consumers.
  \item \textbf{C3 (addresses L3: leakage risk cannot be sized before
  training).} A \emph{leakage exposure bound} that predicts how far pooling can
  move headline accuracy, computed from a CPU-only builder census plus the
  single reliance probe of C2, with no retraining:
  $|\Delta\text{AUC}_{\text{leak}}|\le s_{m,D}\cdot\delta_{\theta}$, where
  $s_{m,D}$ is the \DDR{} slope of C2 and $\delta_{\theta}$ is the structural
  delta that pooling introduces at filter setting $\theta$
  (Section~\ref{sec:exposure}). Measuring $\delta_{\theta}$ by the weight mass
  that pooling moves, rather than by leaked edge count, both tightens the bound
  and explains the near-null result: opening the filter admits many leaked edges
  but little weight, so the channel is throttled by edge weight rather than by
  edge count. Predictions for cells not yet trained are registered in advance
  rather than fitted after the fact.
  \emph{Residual risk:} the bound is informative only where it exceeds the
  training noise floor and where the reliance probe of C2 passes; on graph-inert
  backbones it collapses below resolution and makes no testable claim.
  $s_{m,D}$ is measured by edge \emph{removal} while pooling \emph{adds} edges,
  so the transfer between the two perturbation modes is assumed, not shown.
  \item \textbf{C4 (addresses L4: cold-start reporting).} A cold-start KC
  diagnostic with explicit frequency-based strata and reproducible scripts
  on three KT benchmarks.
  \emph{Residual risk:} stratum thresholds are fixed; very-cold counts can
  be small on real corpora and should not be over-interpreted.
  \item \textbf{C5 (addresses L5: expert vs.\ inferred graphs).} A
  ground-truth cross-validation procedure that quantifies, on Junyi
  Academy, how train-only behavioural inference of $\Epre$ agrees with the
  expert prerequisite annotation of Chang~et~al.~\cite{chang2015junyi},
  using edge-level, direction-level, and reachability-level metrics across
  a sweep of top-$K$ cutoffs.
  \emph{Residual risk:} GT cross-validation is Junyi-only at a single
  expert threshold ($\theta{=}0.5$).
\end{itemize}
Section~\ref{sec:disc-implications} distills five reporting items for
graph-augmented KT papers: fold provenance, cold-start strata, matched sequence
baselines, ground-truth cross-validation when available, and
structure-preserving versus damaging perturbation sensitivity.

The public corpora carry explicit roles. XES3G5M is \textbf{primary} (low
KC-repeat and non-saturated AUC, so backbone differences are not compressed by
ceiling effects); ASSISTments~2012 is \textbf{secondary} (leakage ablation and
cold-start); Junyi Academy is a \textbf{saturated sanity} corpus (ground-truth
cross-validation and DAG density, not headline model ranking). Synthetic C2/C5
exercise tiny-graph audit modules. All baselines use train-only exported graphs
and valid+test sequence positions under three-fold learner CV; protocol metrics
use the same split configuration, and DAG audit and ground-truth
cross-validation refer to fold~0 unless noted. Similarity edges are empty on
ASSISTments (single-skill Q-matrix) and sparse on XES3G5M, so Junyi is the only
corpus where both $\Epre$ and $\Esim$ are populated under the default rule.
We therefore present leakage control as an engineering verification
contribution together with a measured boundary result, not as a route to
higher headline scores. We do not claim that the protocol eliminates
every possible form of leakage, nor do we make claims about real-world
learning outcomes.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
KT progressed from BKT~\cite{corbett1995bkt} to deep sequence models
(DKT, AKT, \textit{simpleKT})~\cite{piech2015dkt,ghosh2020akt,liu2023simplekt}
and libraries such as \texttt{pyKT}~\cite{liu2022pykt,abdelrahman2023kt_survey}.
Within KT, leakage work has centred on \emph{label leakage} in expanded KC
sequences~\cite{liu2022pykt,badran2024labelleakage}; the broader ML literature
catalogues additional leakage types~\cite{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage}.
The complementary channel we study---held-out information entering through the
\emph{auxiliary concept graph}---is not closed by sequence-level remedies and
remains under-audited in KT benchmarks.

\paragraph{Positioning against reproducibility and graph-audit practice.}
KT benchmark libraries~\cite{liu2022pykt,abdelrahman2023kt_survey} and
label-leakage audits~\cite{badran2024labelleakage} standardise sequence splits
and expanded-label checks; broader reproducibility work catalogues leakage
modes beyond a single train/test divide~\cite{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage}.
Our incremental contribution is not another split routine but a
\emph{fold-conditioned graph provenance layer}: exported edge lists, DAG repair
logs, tiered scalar diagnostics, and practitioner reporting items that
sequence-level KT fixes do not supply. Concretely, relative to
\texttt{pyKT}~\cite{liu2022pykt} split discipline alone, the audit layer adds:
(i)~fold-specific exported edge artefacts (\path{e\_pre\_train\_only.csv},
\path{e\_sim\_train\_only.csv}) with per-edge provenance;
(ii)~logged DAG cycle pruning rather than silent repair;
(iii)~tiered leakage functionals plus a controlled injection stress test
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-injection}) that shows $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f$
can stay zero while builder-level mass shifts;
(iv)~\DDR{} structural diagnostics decoupled from AUC;
(v)~ground-truth cross-validation where expert graphs exist; and
(vi)~KC cold-start strata tied to train-fold counts. We complement---rather
than replace---\texttt{pyKT}-style label audits by auditing the auxiliary graph
channel those audits omit.

Graph-KT models (GKT, SKT, GIKT, DyGKT, DGEKT)~\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt,yang2021gikt,dygkt2024,dgekt2024}
propagate mastery over KC graphs, and recent surveys organise the family by
graph form and propagation
mechanism~\cite{xu2026gbktsurvey,abdelrahman2023kt_survey}; memory-augmented
graph variants extend it to forgetting dynamics~\cite{abdelrahman2023dgmn}.
Prerequisite-learning work infers directed relations from logs, content, or
experts~\cite{lin2016moocprereq,bai2025survey,chang2015junyi}, and a growing
line treats the prerequisite structure itself as a latent object to be
recovered rather than supplied: PSI-KT infers a shared prerequisite graph
jointly with learner traits under a Bayesian
formulation~\cite{zhou2024psikt}, and prerequisite structure discovery makes
the knowledge structure a learnable parameter evaluated by how well it drives
content recommendation~\cite{annabi2023prereq}.
This literature is our closest neighbour and also the sharpest contrast. It
asks whether an inferred graph is \emph{accurate}; we ask, prior to that,
whether it is \emph{admissible}---which interactions were allowed to support
each edge. The two questions are independent: a graph can recover the expert
structure well and still have been built from held-out learners. Graph-KT
papers typically treat the graph as given and optimise predictive loss, and
the original GKT and SKT pipelines pool transition statistics over the full log
before learner splitting~\cite{nakagawa2019gkt,tong2020skt}; our protocol
isolates that provenance choice. Expert annotations (e.g.\ Junyi) and
behavioural inference capture related but non-identical structure; we quantify
disagreement rather than treating one as a proxy for the other.

Graph SSL and contrastive KT import label-agnostic augmentations~\cite{you2020graphcl,lee2022cl4kt,kipf2017gcn},
but educational prerequisite DAGs impose directionality that standard drops can
violate. KT reproducibility has focused on code and splits~\cite{liu2022pykt}
rather than graph audit logs. Our work is orthogonal to new KT architectures: it
supplies an audit layer---leakage diagnostics, DAG pruning logs, \DDR{},
ground-truth cross-validation, and cold-start strata---that graph-KT and
contrastive-KT papers typically omit.

\section{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Formal Problem Statement and Leakage Audit}
\label{sec:formal-problem}
For each evaluation fold $f$, the protocol seeks a graph
$G_f=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}_f)$ with $\mathcal{E}_f=\Epre^f\cup\Esim^f$
that is \emph{feasible} with respect to train-only evidence and
structural constraints, then verifies low direct leakage on scalar
diagnostics. Let $\mathcal{I}_{\text{tr}}^f$ denote train-fold
interactions and $\mathcal{H}_f=\mathcal{F}_{\text{val}}^f\cup
\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^f$ held-out interactions.

\paragraph{Decision variables.}
Edge sets $\Epre^f\subseteq\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}$ and
$\Esim^f\subseteq\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}$, edge weights
$w_{ij}\ge 0$, and pruning decisions on cyclic subsets of $\Epre^f$.

\paragraph{Design intent (not a deployed solver).}
The released code does \emph{not} solve a joint numerical graph programme.
Prerequisite and similarity edges are inferred in separate greedy pipelines with
$\lambda{=}0$ in practice; sparsity enters through support quantiles, top-$k$
per-node caps, and a global edge budget~$K$ (Section~\ref{sec:graph}), followed
by deterministic cycle pruning. The conceptual objective
and feasibility constraints~(F1)--(F4) are
stated formally in the supplementary material; treat
them as design documentation, not as an optimiser executed end-to-end.

\paragraph{Constraints (summary).}
F1--F2 block cross-fold and cross-learner contamination; F3--F4 encode
the DAG audit and top-$K$/top-$k$ filters of Section~\ref{sec:graph}.
(These feasibility labels are distinct from the contribution labels
C1--C5 of Section~\ref{sec:intro}.)
We deliberately do \emph{not} impose $\mathrm{LTES}_f{=}0$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:ltes}) as a feasibility constraint: temporal-transition
inference necessarily draws evidence from each learner's full train
timeline, so a zero rate would be attainable only by discarding
late-train evidence. $\mathrm{LTES}_f$ is instead reported as a
within-train stress diagnostic in the sanity/stress tier below; the
released runs accordingly show non-zero values on dense corpora
(Table~\ref{tab:leakage-metrics}).

\paragraph{Audit functionals (diagnostics, not training losses).}
Because F1 cannot always be verified from AUC alone, the protocol
reports a \emph{pass/fail} structural check together with
\emph{throughput} indicators on $G_f$ (Table~\ref{tab:leakage-metrics}).
The pass/fail flag is $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ecr-flag}):
under learner-disjoint splits it is $0$ by construction and functions as a
unit test of the split, not as a graded diagnostic.
Throughput is read from the distribution of per-edge held-out shares
$\pi_e=n_{\mathrm{hi}}(e)/(n_{\mathrm{tr}}(e)+n_{\mathrm{hi}}(e))$, with
$p90(\pi_e)$ as the primary indicator, together with the late-train evidence
share ($\mathrm{LTES}_f$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ltes})---a within-train mixing
statistic, not a train/test leak rate.
The remaining scalars---$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:ecr-overlap}) and $|\rho_f|$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho-edge-outcome})---are
sanity/stress checks: overlap is saturated on every public corpus here, and
$|\rho_f|$ stays weak.
We treat them in three tiers:
\textbf{primary pass/fail}---fold-specific provenance logs,
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$, and (when available) ground-truth
cross-validation; \textbf{empirical bounds}---train-only versus full-log
$\Delta$AUC/ACC and cold-start strata; \textbf{sanity/stress}---
$p90(\pi_e)$, LTES, $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$, and $|\rho_f|$.
Under learner-disjoint splits, $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$ is
expected and does not by itself certify graph quality; near-unity overlap
can occur when curricula repeat across disjoint learners---a binary indicator
that masks how \emph{concentrated} held-out mass is on each edge.
Even when $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f{\approx}1$, most edges keep $\pi_e\ll1$ on
dense logs, whereas tail-\KC{} neighbourhoods---the same locus as the cold-start
strata in Section~\ref{sec:cold-start}---are where a few edges can reach
$\pi_e>0.5$. High AUC does not certify absence of leakage; the
tiered diagnostics complement discrimination scores.

\subsection{Leakage Control}
\label{sec:leakage}
As introduced in Section~\ref{sec:intro}, leakage here means any
information path by which held-out labels, held-out interactions, future
timestamps, learner identities, or graph evidence influence graph
construction, feature construction, model selection, or evaluation although
such information would not be legitimately available at the corresponding
prediction point. Section~\ref{sec:intro} gives the intuitive definition;
here we specialise it to \emph{edge provenance} in KC graphs
(Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy}), following the learn--predict separation
view in data mining~\cite{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage} and
complementing sequence-level label leakage in KT~\cite{liu2022pykt,badran2024labelleakage}.

We audit five leakage classes at the level of edge provenance
(Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy}): structural, edge, target, temporal, and
cold-start-neighbourhood classes cover learner pooling, held-out transition
support, outcome-conditioned graph artefacts, boundary violations, and
tail-\KC{} edges dominated by non-train mass.

Each inferred edge is logged with its fold of origin, timestamp range,
relation type, and train-only flag; severe violations halt the pipeline.
In addition, we report scalar \emph{direct} diagnostics that do
not reduce to predictive accuracy, grouped as a pass/fail check and as
throughput indicators (Table~\ref{tab:leakage-metrics}). Let $\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f$,
$\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f$, and $\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f$ denote the
learner-id sets in train, validation, and test under fold $f$. Both scalars
below measure \emph{evaluation-cohort reachability} ($\mathrm{ECR}$): how far
evidence belonging to the evaluation cohort reaches into the graph the model
consumes. The superscript distinguishes the binary structural check from the
graded per-edge rate. The learner overlap flag is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f := \mathbb{1}\big[
(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f) \cup
(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f) \cup
(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f \cap \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f)
\neq \emptyset
\big]\,.
\label{eq:ecr-flag}
\end{equation}
When folds are learner-disjoint, $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f=0$ for every fold---the regime enforced below.

Let $\mathcal{E}_f$ denote the graph edge set (prerequisite and similarity) consumed when evaluating fold
$f$, and let $\mathcal{H}_f = \mathcal{F}_{\text{val}}^{f} \cup
\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^{f}$ denote held-out interactions under that
split. For edge $e$, let $S_{\text{hi}}(e)$ be held-out interaction tokens counted
toward $e$'s evidence and
$\chi_{\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}}(e)=\mathbb{1}[|S_{\text{hi}}(e)|>0]$.
The held-out pattern overlap rate is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}_f|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_f}
\chi_{\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}}(e)\,.
\label{eq:ecr-overlap}
\end{equation}
For the edge--outcome diagnostic, stack normalized edge weights into
$\mathbf{w}_f\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{E}_f|}$ (for ties, use the order
exported by the audit log). Let $\mathbf{y}_f\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{E}_f|}$
carry test-fold outcome summaries aligned to each edge---for example,
endpoint averages of correctness on $\mathcal{F}_{\text{test}}^{f}$ or
their logits---computed \emph{after} graph construction (labels are used
only for this diagnostic, not for inferring $\mathcal{E}_f$).
The primary edge--outcome diagnostic is the Pearson correlation
\begin{equation}
\rho_f \;=\; \mathrm{Corr}(\mathbf{w}_f,\mathbf{y}_f)\,,
\label{eq:rho-edge-outcome}
\end{equation}
computed over edges with defined variance; we report the magnitude
$|\rho_f|$ in Table~\ref{tab:leakage-metrics}. Values
$|\rho_f|\!\approx\!0$ indicate that retained edge mass is statistically
unrelated to test-fold outcome summaries; on the public benchmarks in
Table~\ref{tab:leakage-metrics}, $|\rho|$ ranges from ${\approx}0.09$
(XES3G5M) to ${\approx}0.22$ (Junyi Academy).

Let $t_{u,i}$ be the timestamp of interaction $i$ for learner $u$ and
let $\tau_u$ be $u$'s temporal split cutoff for fold $f$. Write
$\mathcal{I}_{u,f}^{>}=\{\, i : t_{u,i}>\tau_u \,\}$ for post-boundary
interactions and $\mathcal{V}_{u,f}$ for the subset of those tokens that
incorrectly appear in any multiset counted toward $\mathcal{E}_f$ or in
graph-derived features at inference for fold $f$. The late-train evidence
share is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{LTES}_f \;=\;
\frac{\sum_u |\mathcal{V}_{u,f}|}{\sum_u |\mathcal{I}_{u,f}^{>}|}\,.
\label{eq:ltes}
\end{equation}
We instantiate $\tau_u$ within $\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f$ at the
$70\%$ chronological split of each learner's train sequence.
$\mathrm{LTES}_f$ is therefore a \emph{within-train temporal stress
statistic}: it measures how much edge evidence is supported by the late tail
of each learner's \emph{training} sequence after an internal chronological
cutoff. The name is deliberate: the quantity is an evidence share, not a leak
rate.\footnote{Released code and CSV artefacts carry this column under its
earlier name \texttt{tbvr}/\texttt{tbmr}; the definition is unchanged.}
It must not be read as cross-fold leakage
($\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$ already certifies learner-disjoint
splits). Elevated LTES on dense multi-skill corpora (e.g.\
XES3G5M) flags reliance on late-train co-assessment patterns, not contamination
from validation/test learners.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:leak-audit} collects the pass/fail flag and the remaining
scalars in one pass over a fold. $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f$
is a hard gate that halts the pipeline. Throughput of held-out mass into the
consumed edge set is read primarily from $p90(\pi_e)$
(Table~\ref{tab:leakage-metrics}), computed from the same per-edge held-out
token sets that enter $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$; LTES
($\mathrm{LTES}_f$) and $|\rho_f|$ are within-train / association checks,
none of which reduces to predictive accuracy.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Edge-provenance leakage audit for fold $f$}
\label{alg:leak-audit}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require learner sets $\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f,\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f,\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f$;
  consumed edges $\mathcal{E}_f$; held-out interactions $\mathcal{H}_f$;
  edge weights $\mathbf{w}_f$; outcome summaries $\mathbf{y}_f$
\Ensure $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f,\ \mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f,\ |\rho_f|,\ \mathrm{LTES}_f$
\State $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f \gets \mathbb{1}\big[(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f\cap\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f)\cup(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{tr}}_f\cap\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f)\cup(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{va}}_f\cap\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{te}}_f)\neq\emptyset\big]$
\If{$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f = 1$} \State \textbf{halt}: structural leakage \EndIf
\State $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f \gets \dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{E}_f|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_f}\mathbb{1}\big[|S_{\text{hi}}(e)|>0\big]$ \Comment{held-out tokens supporting $e$}
\State $\rho_f \gets \mathrm{Corr}(\mathbf{w}_f,\mathbf{y}_f)$ \Comment{report $|\rho_f|$ in tables}
\State $\mathrm{LTES}_f \gets \dfrac{\sum_u |\mathcal{V}_{u,f}|}{\sum_u |\mathcal{I}^{>}_{u,f}|}$ \Comment{within-train $70\%$ chronological cutoff}
\State \Return $(\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f,\ \mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f,\ |\rho_f|,\ \mathrm{LTES}_f)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Graph Representation for Knowledge Tracing}
\label{sec:graph-repr}
We represent each fold's knowledge structure as a directed graph
$G_f=(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{E}_f)$ where nodes $c\in\mathcal{C}$
are \KCs{} (hashed identifiers in the released pipeline), and edges carry
relation type $r\in\mathcal{R}=\{\text{pre}, \text{sim}\}$ when that
relation is instantiated on the corpus (prerequisite edges on all benchmarks;
similarity edges only where the Q-matrix yields non-empty overlap). Prerequisite edges $(i,j)\in\Epre^f$ encode directed
precedence inferred from temporal transitions; similarity edges
$(i,j)\in\Esim^f$ encode co-assessment structure from the train-fold
Q-matrix. Each edge stores provenance fields (fold id, train-only flag,
source tag, support count, weight $w_{ij}$) exported as CSV for audit.

\paragraph{Scalability limits.}
Even when train folds contain tens of millions of interactions (Junyi:
${\approx}25.9$\,M rows; Table~\ref{tab:dataset-stats}), the exported graph
remains bounded: nodes are at most the distinct \KC{} vocabulary
$|\mathcal{C}|$; prerequisite edges are capped by a global top-$K$ filter
(default $K{=}5{,}000$) and a per-source out-degree cap (default $k{=}5$)
before DAG pruning (Section~\ref{sec:graph}). Transition counting scales
linearly in train-fold sequence length; cycle detection operates on the
sparse candidate set, not the full $|\mathcal{C}|^2$ adjacency. Table~\ref{tab:graph-scalability}
reports observed node and edge counts after pruning on fold~0; density stays
well below unity on all public benchmarks. We write $|\mathcal{C}_{\text{vocab}}|$ for the
preprocessed KC vocabulary (Table~\ref{tab:dataset-stats}) and $|V_G|$ for nodes
incident to at least one retained prerequisite edge after DAG audit; $|V_G|\leq|\mathcal{C}_{\text{vocab}}|$.

For graph-augmented KT baselines, $G_f$ is materialised as a row-normalised
adjacency matrix $A_f\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{C}|\times|\mathcal{C}|}$
(\path{pykt_graph_matrix.py}): prerequisite and similarity relations can be
merged or consumed separately depending on the backbone (GKT uses a single
KC graph; GIKT additionally uses question--KC bipartite structure from the
train fold). Node features are not learned at graph-construction time;
the protocol treats $G_f$ as a \emph{structural prior} consumed by downstream
KT optimisers. This separation mirrors GNN practice on citation or
knowledge graphs~\cite{kipf2017gcn,hamilton2017graphsage} but with
education-specific relation semantics and fold conditioning.

Technology stack: graph construction is NumPy/pandas/NetworkX; cycle
detection uses Johnson's algorithm~\cite{johnson1975finding}; KT training
optionally delegates to \texttt{pyKT}~\cite{liu2022pykt} with exported
NPZ adjacency. All artefacts are versioned per dataset and fold under
\path{data/graphs/<dataset>/fold_<f>/}.

\subsection{Train-only Graph Construction}
\label{sec:graph}
When $|\mathcal{F}^f_{\text{train}}|$ is very large, the protocol allows
an optional \emph{within-train} sampling step to bound edge-inference cost
without opening a leakage channel (Figure~\ref{fig:intro-leakage}).
We draw $\tilde{\mathcal{I}}^f_{\text{tr}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^f_{\text{train}}$
such that (i)~all sampled interactions come from train learners only;
(ii)~per-\KC{} coverage is preserved by stratifying on KC frequency bins so
rare \KCs{} are not systematically dropped; and (iii)~temporal order is
preserved within each sampled learner sequence. All retained edges still
carry fold provenance and train-only flags. \emph{All reported experiments
in this paper use the full train fold} (sampling disabled); Junyi
(${\approx}26$\,M train interactions) remains tractable under the
top-$K$ and per-source top-$k$ filters below.

\paragraph{Prerequisite edges $\Epre$.}
For uniformity across heterogeneous benchmarks, we infer $\Epre$ from
train-only temporal transitions on \emph{all} three datasets,
independently of whether an external prerequisite source is also
available for that dataset. This deliberate choice decouples the audit
pipeline from dataset-specific quality of expert annotations and
ensures that every reported edge originates from a single, auditable
inference rule. When an expert annotation is available for a benchmark
(here, Junyi Academy), the protocol additionally performs ground-truth
cross-validation as a separate audit step (Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}).

Concretely, for each train-fold interaction sequence we extract
consecutive KC transitions $c_{t} \rightarrow c_{t+1}$ with
$c_{t} \neq c_{t+1}$, count their support $s(c_i, c_j)$, and retain
candidate edges in three filtering stages:
(i) a support-quantile filter that drops transitions below the
$q$-th percentile (default $q = 0.95$);
(ii) a per-source top-$k$ cap (default $k = 5$) that retains, for each
source KC, only the $k$ destination \KCs{} with highest support; and
(iii) a global top-$K$ cap on the number of retained candidate edges
(default $K = 5{,}000$). Each retained edge carries the normalized
support weight $w_{ij} = s(c_i, c_j) / s_{\max}$ used by the cycle
pruning step in Section~\ref{sec:dag-audit}. This candidate edge set
is then passed unchanged to the DAG audit; cycle pruning operates only
on the candidates returned by stages (i)--(iii).
Supplementary Figure~S5 diagrams these
stages; Supplementary Figure~S4(a) situates the block inside the wider
data pipeline.

\paragraph{Similarity edges $\Esim$ and theoretical properties.}
\label{sec:graph-sim-theory}
We compute $\Esim$ from a train-only Q-matrix using either Jaccard or
PMI co-occurrence at the item level (default Jaccard with threshold
$\tau{=}0.10$). Let $I(c)\subseteq\mathcal{Q}$ be the train-fold item
set tagged with concept $c$, with $n=|\mathcal{Q}|$ items:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{sim}_J(c_i,c_j) &=
  \frac{|I(c_i)\cap I(c_j)|}{|I(c_i)\cup I(c_j)|},
  \label{eq:sim-jaccard}\\
\mathrm{sim}_{\mathrm{PMI}}(c_i,c_j) &=
  \log \frac{p(c_i,c_j)}{p(c_i)\,p(c_j)},
  \label{eq:sim-pmi}\notag\\
&\quad p(c)=\tfrac{|I(c)|}{n},\notag\\
&\quad p(c_i,c_j)=\tfrac{|I(c_i)\cap I(c_j)|}{n}.
\end{align}
Jaccard similarity is bounded in $[0,1]$ and yields no $\Esim$ when items are
single-skill (ASSISTments~2012; Section~\ref{sec:exp-coldstart}). Full
operator definitions and subgraph sampling details are in Supplementary
Section~S4. All graph artefacts are materialised per dataset and fold.
\subsection{DAG Audit}
\label{sec:dag-audit}
For each fold-specific prerequisite edge set, we check whether $\Epre$
is acyclic. If cycles are detected, we iteratively remove the
lowest-confidence edge in a detected cycle and re-run the check until a
topological ordering exists. Cycle detection uses a bounded simple-cycle enumeration procedure based on
Johnson's algorithm for enumerating elementary circuits in directed graphs \cite{johnson1975finding}, capped at 100 representative cycles per detection round. We use this cap because the number of elementary cycles can grow rapidly with graph density; exhaustive enumeration is unnecessary for our
audit, since the pruning loop only requires representative cycle witnesses. The detector cap does not affect
correctness: the pruning loop terminates only when no further cycle can
be detected, so the final retained graph is acyclic. Each removed edge
is written to
\path{<dataset>\_dag\_pruning\_log.csv} (repository reports/) with its source,
target, confidence score, and pruning reason. The audit reports node
count, raw edge count, pruned edge count, retained edge count, root
count, leaf count, and final topological-sort status. The claim is not
that the retained DAG is the unique correct prerequisite hierarchy, but
that the pruning rule and edge provenance are explicit and reproducible.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:graph-build} states the construction and audit steps
together. Counting and filtering touch only $\mathcal{F}^f_{\text{train}}$,
so no held-out interaction can support a retained edge; the cycle-pruning loop
terminates only when no further cycle is detected, guaranteeing an acyclic
$\Epre^f$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Train-only prerequisite graph construction with DAG audit}
\label{alg:graph-build}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require train fold $\mathcal{F}^f_{\text{train}}$; quantile $q$; per-source cap
  $k$; global cap $K$
\Ensure acyclic edge set $\Epre^f$ with provenance
\State $s \gets \emptyset$ \Comment{support counter over KC transitions}
\For{each learner sequence $(c_1,\dots,c_T)\in\mathcal{F}^f_{\text{train}}$}
  \For{$t \gets 1$ to $T-1$ \textbf{with} $c_t \neq c_{t+1}$}
    \State $s[(c_t,c_{t+1})] \gets s[(c_t,c_{t+1})] + 1$
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State $\theta \gets$ $q$-th percentile of $\{s[\cdot]\}$ \Comment{(i) support-quantile filter}
\State $C \gets \{(i,j) : s[(i,j)] \ge \theta\}$
\State $C \gets \bigcup_{i} \textsc{TopK}_{j}(C_i, k)$ by support \Comment{(ii) per-source top-$k$}
\State $C \gets \textsc{TopK}(C, K)$ by support \Comment{(iii) global top-$K$}
\For{$(i,j)\in C$}
  \State $w_{ij} \gets s[(i,j)]/\max_{(a,b)\in C} s[(a,b)]$; tag $(f,\textit{train-only})$
\EndFor
\State $\Epre^f \gets C$
\While{$\Epre^f$ contains a cycle} \Comment{DAG audit}
  \State $Z \gets \textsc{JohnsonCycles}(\Epre^f,\ \text{cap}=100)$
  \State $e^\star \gets \arg\min_{e\in\bigcup Z} w_e$; remove $e^\star$; log $e^\star$
\EndWhile
\State \Return $\Epre^f$ with topological order
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{DAG Disruption Rate and Augmentation Operators}
\label{sec:ddr}
To quantify the structural effect of graph augmentations on prerequisite
DAGs, we define the DAG Disruption Rate (\DDR{}). Let $A$ be an
augmentation operator and $A(\Epre,p)$ the edge set obtained at
perturbation strength $p$. Let $\mathrm{pre}(A(\Epre,p))\subseteq\Epre$
be the original prerequisite edges that remain with their original
direction, and let $\mathrm{rev}(A(\Epre,p))$ be the edges that appear
with the opposite direction. We define
\begin{equation}
\DDR(A,p)=
\frac{|\Epre\setminus \mathrm{pre}(A(\Epre,p))|
      +|\mathrm{rev}(A(\Epre,p))|}
     {|\Epre|}.
\label{eq:ddr}
\end{equation}
We evaluate four label-agnostic families from GraphCL~\cite{you2020graphcl}
(\texttt{attr\_mask}, \texttt{edge\_drop}, random-walk \texttt{subgraph},
\texttt{node\_drop}) plus one prerequisite-preserving operator
(\texttt{prereq\_preserve}) that removes only transitively redundant edges at
the same per-edge budget as \texttt{edge\_drop} (Section~\ref{sec:exp-ddr};
formal definitions in Supplementary Section~S4).
Each operator is swept over $p\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20,0.30\}$ with three
random seeds on each of three train folds ($3\times 3=9$ runs per
setting); reported means aggregate across those runs. \DDR{} is a structural diagnostic only: it measures how
much prerequisite structure is removed or reversed by an augmentation,
not whether a downstream KT model becomes better or worse.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:ddr} states the computation. An edge counts as disrupted if
it is removed outright or re-appears with reversed direction, so \DDR{}
captures both deletion and direction flips of prerequisite precedence.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{DAG Disruption Rate (\DDR{})}
\label{alg:ddr}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require prerequisite edges $\Epre$; augmentation operator $A$; strength $p$
\Ensure $\DDR(A,p)\in[0,1]$
\State $E' \gets A(\Epre, p)$ \Comment{apply operator at strength $p$}
\State $\mathrm{pre} \gets \{(i,j)\in\Epre : (i,j)\in E'\}$ \Comment{kept, same direction}
\State $\mathrm{rev} \gets \{(i,j)\in\Epre : (j,i)\in E'\ \textbf{and}\ (i,j)\notin E'\}$ \Comment{reversed}
\State \Return $\big(|\Epre\setminus\mathrm{pre}| + |\mathrm{rev}|\big)\,/\,|\Epre|$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Leakage Exposure Bound}
\label{sec:exposure}
The audit of Section~\ref{sec:leakage} is all-or-nothing: a team either runs it
or does not, and learns the accuracy consequence of pooling only after training
both arms. This subsection turns the two quantities the protocol already
measures into an \emph{a priori} estimate of that consequence, so a team can
size the risk before spending the compute.

\paragraph{The two inputs.}
The first is \emph{backbone reliance}
$s_{m,D}$: the OLS slope of AUC loss on \DDR{} for backbone $m$ on corpus $D$
(Section~\ref{sec:ddr}; measured in Section~\ref{sec:exp-ddr-downstream}). It
answers how much accuracy a unit of prerequisite disruption costs. The second is
the \emph{pooling delta} $\delta_\theta$: how far the graph built from pooled
logs sits from the train-only graph at filter setting
$\theta=(q,k,K,\tau)$. This is a property of the builder alone, read off the
census (Table~\ref{tab:m4-qk-census}) at no training cost. Writing
$\Epre^{\mathrm{to}}$ and $\Epre^{\mathrm{fl}}$ for the train-only and full-log
prerequisite sets, the direct reading counts leaked edges,
\begin{equation}
\delta^{\mathrm{cnt}}_\theta
  = \frac{|\Epre^{\mathrm{fl}}\setminus\Epre^{\mathrm{to}}|}
         {|\Epre^{\mathrm{to}}|},
\label{eq:delta-count}
\end{equation}
which shares the normaliser $|\Epre|$ with \DDR{}, so the product
$s_{m,D}\cdot\delta_\theta$ is in AUC units.

\paragraph{Weight mass is the better currency.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:delta-count} treats every leaked edge alike, but a
graph-consuming backbone propagates over a \emph{row-normalised} adjacency: what
reaches the model is each concept's outgoing weight distribution, not its edge
count. We therefore also read the delta as the weight mass that pooling moves,
\begin{equation}
\delta^{\mathrm{w}}_\theta
  = \frac{\sum_{(i,j)}\big|w^{\mathrm{fl}}_{ij}-w^{\mathrm{to}}_{ij}\big|}
         {\sum_{(i,j)\in\Epre^{\mathrm{to}}} w^{\mathrm{to}}_{ij}},
\label{eq:delta-weight}
\end{equation}
summing over the union of the two edge sets. Both are computed by
\path{scripts/leakage_exposure.py} from exported edge CSVs in seconds.

\paragraph{The bound.}
For a backbone whose reliance probe passes,
\begin{equation}
\big|\Delta\text{AUC}_{\text{leak}}(m,D,\theta)\big|
  \;\le\; \max\big(s_{m,D}\cdot\delta_\theta,\ \sigma\big),
\label{eq:exposure-bound}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the training noise floor at the attested budget. The
$\max$ is not cosmetic. When reliance is weak, $s_{m,D}\cdot\delta_\theta$
falls below the smallest difference the experiment can resolve, and the bound
stops making a testable claim rather than becoming a tight one; Section~%
\ref{sec:threats-validity} reports where this happens. Equation~%
\eqref{eq:exposure-bound} therefore inherits the manipulation-check gate that
already governs \DDR{}: it is a statement about graph-reliant backbones, and on
graph-inert ones it is silent by construction.

\paragraph{Cost and standing assumption.}
Applying the bound to a new filter setting costs one CPU census and no
training, because $s_{m,D}$ is measured once per backbone--corpus pair and is
exactly the probe Section~\ref{sec:ddr} already requires. The standing
assumption is that this slope transfers across perturbation modes: $s_{m,D}$ is
estimated by \emph{removing} and reversing edges, whereas pooling \emph{adds}
them. We state this as an assumption rather than a result, and revisit it in
Section~\ref{sec:threats-validity}.

\subsection{Cold-start KC Stratification}
\label{sec:cold-start}
We define cold-start at the KC level, distinct from user/item
cold-start in recommender systems~\cite{schein2002coldstart}. For fold
$f$, let $n_f(c)=|\{(u,t,q,c,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}^f\}|$ be
the number of train-fold interactions tagged with \KC{} $c$. Each KC
is assigned to one of four frequency strata:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{S}_1&:\ n_f(c)<20,\\
\mathcal{S}_2&:\ 20\le n_f(c)<100,\\
\mathcal{S}_3&:\ 100\le n_f(c)<500,\\
\mathcal{S}_4&:\ n_f(c)\ge 500.
\end{aligned}
\]
Strata are assigned from train-fold counts only; test rows inherit their
\KC{} stratum and aggregate AUC/ACC/NLL per stratum. KCs with zero train-fold
interactions are absent from the frequency table and therefore excluded from
stratum aggregates unless they appear under \emph{out of range} when a held-out
\KC{} cannot be mapped to the four canonical bins. We use \emph{cold-start}
throughout in this \emph{frequency-stratum} sense (few train interactions),
distinct from zero-interaction or zero-shot cold-start splits in recommender-style
KT protocols. This exposes tail-\KC{}
behaviour that aggregate metrics hide and links rare-\KC{} edge support to
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$ (Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy}).
\subsection{Ground-Truth Cross-Validation}
\label{sec:gtxv-protocol}
When a benchmark provides an expert prerequisite annotation, the
protocol performs an additional, optional audit step: comparing the
train-only inferred $\Epre$ against the expert edge set
$\Epre^{\text{expert}}$. The procedure has four stages:
(i) load the expert annotation and filter rows by a prerequisite-score
threshold $\theta$ to obtain a directed expert edge set;
(ii) align expert KC identifiers to the pipeline's hashed \texttt{kc\_id}
space via a deterministic name-to-id mapping reconstructed from the
preprocessing hash function;
(iii) sweep top-$K$ cutoffs over the inferred edge set and compute,
at each $K$: edge precision/recall/F1, direction agreement, reachability
F1 (over transitive closure), and node Jaccard;
(iv) extract qualitative examples of disagreement in three categories:
edges present only in inferred, edges present only in expert, and edges
with opposite direction.
The procedure is read-only with respect to the pipeline; no
inferred edge is added or removed based on the comparison.

\subsection{Controlled test-fold edge injection}
\label{sec:exp-injection}
This experiment occupies the high-throughput row of
Supplementary Table~S22: we raise contamination
\emph{throughput} on purpose and observe which backbones move. We
deliberately contaminate the XES3G5M fold-0 train-only prerequisite builder
with $5\%$ and $20\%$ of test-fold interactions (Supplementary Table~S17).
Candidate-edge count ($|\Epre|$: $1162{\to}1378{\to}1417$; counted
\emph{before} DAG pruning, whereas Table~\ref{tab:dag-audit} reports the
$701$ edges retained \emph{after} pruning on the clean fold) and
$\mathrm{LTES}_f$ ($0.754{\to}0.776{\to}0.779$) increase monotonically with
the injection rate, while $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f$ remains $0$
because learner-id sets stay disjoint: flag-level checks alone cannot detect
this contamination channel, which motivates the tiered audit.
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$ is already saturated at $1.0$ on this
transition-dense corpus (Section~\ref{sec:formal-problem}), so it carries no
additional signal here. Retraining on the injected graphs uses the \emph{reduced}
graph-backbone budgets in Table~S15 (10 epochs, smaller batches; Supplementary
Table~S18), not the 30-epoch sequence cap---so absolute AUC levels are not
directly comparable to headline Table~\ref{tab:baseline-summary} rows. The
qualitative pattern we emphasise is \emph{decoupling}: builder-side indicators
move while fold-0 test AUC remains nearly unchanged for all three backbones
($|\Delta\text{AUC}|{\leq}0.010$ at $20\%$ injection). \textit{simpleKT} moves by ${+}0.0004$ ($0.8744{\to}0.8748$);
GIKT by ${+}0.0002$ ($0.8776{\to}0.8778$); GKT \emph{dips} slightly by
${-}0.010$ ($0.8346{\to}0.8243$). Metric-level contamination is detectable in
builder mass and $\mathrm{LTES}_f$ before leakage would be inferred
from AUC alone.

The qualitative pattern is \emph{decoupling}: provenance and builder-mass
diagnostics move when AUC does not, so AUC is a poor detector of this channel
under the studied regimes. Supplementary Table~S22 lays the two axes out as a
descriptive grid, crossing contamination throughput against backbone reliance;
no cell exceeds $|\Delta\text{AUC}|{\approx}0.010$, and the realistic operating
point sits in the low-throughput row.

\subsection{DAG Disruption Rate}
\label{sec:exp-ddr}
Supplementary Figure~S2 plots \DDR{} versus
perturbation strength $p$ on all three benchmarks; the main text retains the
tabular summary only.

\input{ddr_raw.tex}
Supplementary Figure~S2 and Table~\ref{tab:ddr-raw} show five
consistent regimes across the three benchmarks. First, attribute
masking leaves $\Epre$ structurally unchanged and therefore gives
$\DDR{}=0$ across perturbation strengths by construction. Second,
random-walk subgraph sampling preserves connectivity inside the
sampled subgraph and disrupts $\Epre$ \emph{sub-linearly} in $p$:
because edges are removed only at the boundary of the visited
subgraph, the disruption is consistently smaller than under
edge dropping at the same $p$ (e.g.\ on Junyi, $\DDR{} = 0.16$ for
subgraph vs.\ $0.20$ for edge drop at $p=0.20$). Third, edge dropping
disrupts prerequisite structure approximately linearly in $p$, as
expected when each edge is removed independently. Fourth, node
dropping produces the largest disruption because removing a node also
removes all incident prerequisite edges; at $p = 0.30$, node dropping
disrupts roughly half of the prerequisite structure on all three
benchmarks ($\DDR{} \approx 0.52$--$0.54$).

\paragraph{\DDR{} is an operator-level structural diagnostic, not a tautology.}
A natural objection to the four label-agnostic families is that their
\DDR{} ordering merely re-states their definitions: an operator that
deletes more edges disrupts more structure. To test whether \DDR{}
\emph{discriminates} structure-aware from structure-agnostic
augmentation rather than re-describing edge counts, we add a fifth,
prerequisite-preserving operator (\texttt{prereq\_preserve}). It draws
the same per-edge budget $p$ as \texttt{edge\_drop}, but only removes
edges that are \emph{transitively redundant}---those whose endpoints
remain connected through an alternative directed path---so that every
reachability/precedence relation and the entire transitive-reduction
backbone are preserved. Crucially, unlike \texttt{attr\_mask}, this
operator is \emph{not} $\DDR{=}0$ by construction: its disruption is an
empirical quantity equal to $p$ times the redundant-edge fraction of the
graph. At every dataset and strength, \texttt{prereq\_preserve} is
strictly less disruptive than \texttt{edge\_drop} at a matched budget
(at $p=0.30$, $0.278$ vs.\ $0.301$ on Junyi, $0.262$ vs.\ $0.309$ on
ASSISTments, and $0.233$ vs.\ $0.299$ on XES3G5M). The size of this gap
is \emph{data-dependent}---a $\sim 8\%$ relative reduction on Junyi but
$\sim 22\%$ on XES3G5M---and is determined by graph redundancy rather
than by the operator definition. Because temporal-precedence inference
generates many transitive shortcuts (if $a$ precedes $b$ precedes $c$,
the edge $a\!\to\!c$ is also counted), the inferred graphs are
highly redundant ($\approx 78$--$92\%$ of edges lie off the transitive
reduction), which both explains the modest gap and shows that \DDR{}
responds to operator structure-awareness in a quantitative,
non-tautological way. \DDR{} can therefore serve as a concrete design
target: an augmentation that respects prerequisite directionality attains
measurably lower disruption at the same perturbation
budget. On a graph-reliant backbone this structural difference also appears
downstream---\texttt{prereq\_preserve} incurs the smallest test-AUC loss at a matched
budget on GKT/XES3G5M---so the design target is validated in accuracy, not
only in structure, whenever the backbone actually consumes the graph
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-ddr-downstream}).

The relative ordering
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\DDR(\mathrm{attr\_mask}) < \DDR(\mathrm{subgraph}) \\
&\quad\lesssim \DDR(\mathrm{prereq\_preserve}) < \DDR(\mathrm{edge\_drop}) \\
&\quad < \DDR(\mathrm{node\_drop})
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
is consistent across all three benchmarks and all four perturbation
strengths. We read these results as a structural diagnostic, not as a
claim that any augmentation is beneficial or harmful for a downstream
KT learner. They indicate that prerequisite-aware augmentation design
is needed when graph augmentations are applied to educational DAGs:
node-level operators are an order of magnitude more disruptive than
attribute-level operators, with subgraph sampling and the
prerequisite-preserving operator occupying an intermediate,
structure-preserving regime.

\subsection{\DDR{} Downstream Stress Test}
\label{sec:exp-ddr-downstream}
\input{ddr_downstream.tex}
\input{ddr_downstream_gkt.tex}
We retrain graph-consuming backbones on perturbed graphs and ask whether
structural disruption (\DDR{}) predicts accuracy loss. Because a null
\DDR{}$\to$AUC relationship is ambiguous---it can mean either ``\DDR{} does not
predict AUC'' or ``this backbone ignores the graph''---we gate every
interpretation on a \emph{manipulation check}: an anchor at $p{=}0.90$ that
destroys almost the entire graph (\DDR{}${\approx}0.9$--$1.0$). A backbone whose
AUC does not move even under total destruction is graph-inert, and its
\DDR{}$\to$AUC correlation carries no downstream meaning.

\paragraph{Low-reliance anchors (DGEKT everywhere; GKT on ASSISTments).}
Table~\ref{tab:ddr-downstream} reports the original DGEKT sweep on
ASSISTments~2012 and XES3G5M. Despite large structural disruption
(\texttt{node\_drop} mean \DDR{} $0.493$--$0.555$ at $p{=}0.30$), DGEKT AUC
changes stay near zero and pooled \DDR{}--AUC-drop correlations are weak
($r{=}0.13$--$0.15$). GKT on ASSISTments behaves the same way
(Table~\ref{tab:ddr-downstream-gkt}, second block): destroying the graph
entirely at the manipulation-check anchor (\texttt{node\_drop} $p{=}0.90$,
\DDR{}${=}0.995$) moves AUC by only $0.0021$ ($0.9630{\to}0.9610$). These cells
\emph{fail} the manipulation check; we read them as \emph{low-reliance anchors}
(reliance ${\approx}0$), not as evidence about \DDR{}. This is exactly the
low-reliance column of Supplementary Table~S22:
where the backbone does not consume the graph, no structural metric can shift
accuracy, and \DDR{}'s value is confined to structure and reproducibility.

\paragraph{Graph-reliant backbone (GKT on XES3G5M): \DDR{} slope, not Pearson $r$.}
The same protocol on GKT/XES3G5M (Table~\ref{tab:ddr-downstream-gkt}, first
block; seeds $\{42,17,1234\}$, six fold$\times$seed clusters) tells the opposite story. GKT \emph{passes} the
manipulation check decisively: near-total destruction drops AUC from the
pooled baseline $0.8346$ to $0.7636$ (\texttt{edge\_drop} $p{=}0.90$, ${-}0.071$) and
$0.7468$ (\texttt{node\_drop} $p{=}0.90$, ${-}0.088$), so this backbone
demonstrably reads the prerequisite graph. Within the interpretable regime,
the OLS slope of AUC-drop on \DDR{} is $\DDRslopeXEScore$ AUC per unit \DDR{}
(cluster-bootstrap 95\% CI \DDRslopeXEScoreci; $n{=}\DDRnXEScore$ on the
$p{\le}0.3$ core). Pearson $r{=}0.95$ on that core is a secondary, scale-free
summary: the same coefficient can be large when the practical effect is
negligible, as on ASSISTments/GKT (core slope $\DDRslopeASSISTcore$, CI
\DDRslopeASSISTcoreci, despite $r{=}0.93$). Including the $p{=}0.90$ anchors
raises the XES3G5M/GKT slope only to $\DDRslopeXESfull$ (CI \DDRslopeXESfullci,
$n{=}\DDRnXESfull$); we do not treat that inflated Pearson $r{=}0.99$ as a
headline number. Moreover, at a \emph{matched} perturbation budget the
operator ordering carries through to accuracy: at $p{=}0.30$,
\texttt{prereq\_preserve} costs $0.0098$ AUC, \texttt{edge\_drop} $0.0118$, and
\texttt{node\_drop} $0.0423$, and the same
$\texttt{prereq\_preserve}<\texttt{edge\_drop}<\texttt{node\_drop}$ ordering
holds at $p{=}0.10$ and $p{=}0.20$. The structure-preserving operator that is
least disruptive by \DDR{} (Section~\ref{sec:exp-ddr}) is also the one that
incurs the smallest accuracy loss on a backbone that uses the graph.

This is the positive control the earlier DGEKT-only sweep lacked: on a
graph-reliant backbone, \DDR{} is not merely a structural audit but a
\emph{downstream-predictive} one, and \texttt{prereq\_preserve}'s structural
advantage translates into a measurable accuracy advantage. Because reliance is
backbone- and dataset-specific (GKT is graph-reliant on XES3G5M yet inert on
ASSISTments), \DDR{}'s downstream relevance is likewise conditional: it becomes
a design target precisely in the high-reliance regime that the manipulation
check identifies. Extending the same anchored protocol to further graph-reliant
backbones (e.g.\ GIKT under the same \DDR{}$\to$AUC protocol) is the natural
follow-up (Section~\ref{sec:future-work}).

\paragraph{Similarity edges and dataset properties.}
The similarity-edge rule of Section~\ref{sec:graph} is sensitive to the
multi-skill density of the Q-matrix. On the train fold of
ASSISTments~2012, $100\%$ of items are tagged with exactly one KC, so
$|I(c_i) \cap I(c_j)| = 0$ for every distinct concept pair, and both
Jaccard and PMI yield no edges. On XES3G5M, $86.6\%$ of train items are
single-skill but the remaining multi-skill items generate $354$
similarity edges at the default Jaccard threshold of $0.10$. We treat
this dataset-property dependency as a first-class outcome of the audit:
a similarity rule appropriate for multi-skill benchmarks may need to be
replaced or supplemented (e.g., by response-pattern correlation) on
single-skill benchmarks. This is not a pipeline failure but a structural
observation that protocol papers should report.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{Model-specific graph benefit under leakage-controlled evaluation}
\label{sec:disc-model-specific}
The baseline summary (Table~\ref{tab:baseline-summary}) and fold-level
diagnostics (Table~\ref{tab:baseline-cv}) support a \emph{bounded}
benchmarking observation rather than a universal ranking reversal. We anchor
the discussion on \textbf{XES3G5M} (primary; Section~\ref{sec:datasets})
because its lower KC-repeat and non-saturated AUC make backbone separation
interpretable. Under leakage-controlled evaluation (train-only $\Epre$,
optional train-only $\Esim$ where populated, learner-based temporal split),
explicit concept-graph benefit is \emph{model-specific} within the
\textbf{primary trio} (\textit{simpleKT}, pyKT GKT, native GIKT) rather than
uniform. On XES3G5M, \textit{simpleKT} averages $0.875$ AUC while GKT averages
$0.834$ under the released budget (mean $\Delta{\approx}{-}0.041$; 95\% CI
\GKTdeltaci{}, Table~S16; fold stds in Table~\ref{tab:baseline-cv};
extended-training sensitivity in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}/Table~S21).
GIKT ($0.878$) slightly \emph{exceeds}
the sequence leader ($+0.003$ mean AUC; interval \GIKTdeltaci{}, tied at
three decimals on all folds). Native SKT ($0.625$)
and DyGKT ($0.752$) are reported as \emph{fork wiring diagnostics} only: their
large deficits may reflect under-tuned native stacks rather than inherent
backbone limits and are not used to argue that graph-augmented KT fails. On
ASSISTments (secondary), the same ordering direction holds but magnitudes shrink
(GKT trails by ${\approx}0.007$ AUC with near-ceiling leaders). On saturated
Junyi, all models cluster within ${\approx}0.002$--$0.006$ AUC of
\textit{simpleKT} and should not drive deployment conclusions. Because the
ablation of Section~\ref{sec:exp-graph-ablation} leaves headline metrics
essentially unmoved, the protocol's primary \emph{provenance} contribution is
reproducibility and risk disclosure, not a demonstrated systematic flip of
backbone rankings caused by leakage control alone.

We do not claim that graph-augmented KT is uninformative; the protocol
removes a specific channel through which held-out information can reach the
model---the concept graph itself. Three non-mutually-exclusive hypotheses
are consistent with the observed ordering.

\paragraph{H1 -- Leakage in prior evaluations.}
A common practice in graph-augmented KT is to compute graph statistics
from the full interaction log prior to train/test splitting. Under such
practice, graph-augmented models can observe test-fold transitions through
the graph during training. Our protocol enforces fold-specific edge
inference and provenance logging. Table~\ref{tab:graph-ablation}
instantiates the controlled comparison---identical learner splits and
evaluation code, but graph statistics pooled over the full preprocessed
log---and its near-null result
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-graph-ablation}) caps how much of the observed GKT
deficit headline leakage can explain. H1 therefore remains relevant as a
\emph{risk hypothesis} about workflows that leak held-out mass into graph
construction, not as a causal claim we have confirmed here. Architectures that
exploit cross-split pooling more aggressively might show larger shifts.

\paragraph{H2 -- Aggregate-metric masking.}
The cold-start diagnostic (Section~\ref{sec:exp-coldstart}) shows that
per-stratum behaviour can differ from aggregate behaviour.
Table~\ref{tab:cold-start-summary} makes this concrete: stratum-level
$\Delta_{\max}$ is small and inconsistent in sign (ASSISTments
\emph{very\_cold} $-0.007{\pm}0.012$; XES3G5M $+0.006{\pm}0.006$),
essentially tied in \emph{cold}, and worse in \emph{warm}/\emph{hot}.
Junyi \emph{very\_cold} and \emph{cold} are omitted for the same reliability
reasons as in Section~\ref{sec:exp-coldstart} ($n{=}11$/$43$ on fold~0;
uncapped-prediction GPU rerun). On
Synthetic~C2/C5, where all test interactions are hot, GIKT/DGEKT improve over
sequence-only baselines, but this reflects clean synthetic structure rather
than cold-start relief.

\paragraph{H3 -- Strong-backbone substitution.}
Modern attention-based backbones such as AKT and \textit{simpleKT} may
already capture much relational information through long-range temporal
attention. Additional structural signal at the concept-embedding level may
then yield diminishing returns---a pattern weaker temporal backbones should
benefit from more, all else equal.

These hypotheses are not exhaustive. Distinguishing among them requires
re-running prior graph-KT papers under our protocol, which is beyond this
paper's scope. The narrower observation is that \emph{under leakage-controlled
evaluation on XES3G5M, backbone ordering within the primary trio can differ
from near-ceiling aggregates on other corpora}, and benchmarking practice
should treat graph provenance and training-budget disclosure as first-order
reporting factors---while acknowledging that provenance control alone barely
moved headline AUC on the public benchmarks studied here.

\paragraph{Why this strengthens the protocol.}
If graph-augmented KT reliably outperformed sequence-only baselines
regardless of graph provenance, leakage control would be secondary. The
combination of (i)~model-specific trio ordering on XES3G5M under train-only
graphs, (ii)~a near-null train-only/full-log ablation on public benchmarks,
(iii)~controlled injection showing scalar flags can miss graph-builder
contamination (Tables~S17--S18), and (iv)~edge provenance plus GT
cross-validation on Junyi, supports treating graph auditing as a reporting
requirement rather than an optional preprocessing check. Combined with Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}, which shows
that inferred and expert prerequisite signals are structurally distinct, graph
quality cannot be assumed---it must be audited.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
Baseline ordering uses three-fold means; DAG audit and GT cross-validation
refer to fold~0 exports by default. GKT, AKT, DKT, and \textit{simpleKT} are
stock pyKT~\cite{liu2022pykt} checkpoints; GIKT, SKT, DyGKT, and DGEKT are
native implementations in our fork (\path{src/models/}). Epoch caps differ in
the primary release (30 for sequence checkpoints vs.\ 10 for graph backbones;
Table~S15). Extended-training configuration sensitivity for GKT is consolidated
in Section~\ref{sec:limitations} (Table~S21).
The GKT--\textit{simpleKT} gap on XES3G5M remains \emph{observational} under
attested budgets, not proof that graph signal is useless after exhaustive tuning.
The severe SKT/DyGKT deficits in Tables~\ref{tab:baseline-summary}
and~\ref{tab:baseline-cv} are flagged as likely implementation-budget artefacts
and excluded from primary ranking claims. Stronger graph-KT architectures~\cite{tong2020skt,dygkt2024,dgekt2024}
may behave differently; re-auditing them under the same protocol is explicit
follow-up (Section~\ref{sec:future-work}).

\subsection{Broader implications}
\label{sec:disc-implications}

\paragraph{Scalability and broader domains.}
The protocol is dataset-agnostic: cost scales with train-fold transition
counting and capped cycle enumeration, not KT training. Sparsity caps keep
audit tractable on million-scale logs~\cite{choi2020ednet}. The similarity
rule may need domain-specific co-occurrence when Q-matrices are single-skill;
GT cross-validation generalises wherever expert DAGs exist; \DDR{} applies to
any directed curriculum graph consumed by KT or pretraining pipelines.

\paragraph{Implications for graph-enhanced KT.}
KC graph construction deserves treatment as a first-class experimental
component rather than an unexamined preprocessing step: fold-specific edge
inference, provenance logging, and DAG validation surface leakage risks that
would otherwise stay hidden. The downstream stress test of
Section~\ref{sec:exp-ddr-downstream} makes the augmentation story
\emph{conditional} rather than universal. Where the manipulation check confirms
the backbone reads the graph, structural damage translates into predictive
damage and \texttt{prereq\_preserve} is the safest operator at matched budget;
where it fails, the graph is effectively unused and \DDR{} remains a
structural and reproducibility audit. Future augmentation papers should
therefore report both, rather than assuming the first case.

\paragraph{Cold-start reporting.}
Rare \KCs{} can behave unlike frequent ones, and apparent gains on small strata
may reflect class imbalance rather than modelling improvement, so per-stratum
reporting shows whether a method is robust across the KC frequency
distribution. In our results graph-aware models show \emph{no consistent}
real-data advantage by stratum (Section~\ref{sec:exp-coldstart}); synthetic
hot-stratum gains reflect clean concept structure, not cold-start relief.
Very-cold counts stay small, so stratum-level AUC carries high variance and
must be read alongside $n$ and fold-level dispersion.

\paragraph{Leakage risk concentrates at cold-start KCs.}
Leakage control and cold-start stratification are usually treated as separate
checks, but throughput links them directly. \emph{Throughput}
is the share of an edge's evidence that comes from held-out mass, and this share
is largest exactly where the train-fold neighbourhood is thinnest. A hot KC with
hundreds of train transitions barely moves when a few held-out traversals leak
into its edges; a very-cold KC supported by only a handful of train transitions
can have its incident edges---and hence its graph-propagated representation---
dominated by a single leaked transition. Concretely, in the worked example of
Section~\ref{sec:intro} the same $20$ leaked traversals that change an edge's
support from $40$ to $60$ ($33\%$ throughput) would, on a KC with train support
$4$, raise it from $4$ to $24$ ($83\%$ throughput): leakage throughput scales
inversely with train-fold KC frequency. This is why the leakage taxonomy in
Table~\ref{tab:leakage-taxonomy} lists a dedicated \emph{cold-start
neighbourhood} class audited by $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}$ together with
the strata: cold-start KCs are the highest-throughput entry points for
graph-mediated leakage, so per-stratum provenance---not just aggregate
provenance---is the conservative report. Equivalently, a near-null
\emph{aggregate} ablation can still mask a larger, throughput-driven shift
confined to the very-cold stratum; disaggregating the ablation by stratum is
the natural next measurement.

\paragraph{Pipeline integration and operational cost.}
The audit is a CPU-only pre-training gate, not an online inference tax.
In an ITS pipeline it runs after the train-fold graph export and
\emph{before} KT training: a continuous-integration job consumes the
fold CSVs, writes provenance logs, and emits the pass/fail flag plus
$p90(\pi_e)$. The job \emph{fails} if $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{\neq}0$
(split regression) or if a model that \emph{requires} $\Esim$ is scheduled
while $|E_{\mathrm{sim}}|{=}0$ (the ASSISTments vignette above). It
\emph{warns} if the builder binds at $k{\ge}20$ with $q{\le}0.80$ without a
train-only versus full-log row (Tables~\ref{tab:m4-qk-census},~\ref{tab:m4-phase-b-auc}), or if LTES
jumps under a graph-construction change while headline AUC does not.
The data steward owns the logs; the modelling owner owns the fail/warn
policy. Table~\ref{tab:audit-cost} reports measured wall-clock and peak RSS
for this gate on the three public corpora (fold~0, then $3\times$ as the
protocol default): $1.6$\,min / $0.6$\,GiB on ASSISTments, $3.2$\,min /
$1.2$\,GiB on XES3G5M, and $18$\,min / ${\approx}5$\,GiB on Junyi
($25.9$\,M interactions). A three-fold continuous-integration job is therefore
about $5$\,min, $10$\,min, and $54$\,min respectively---acceptable as a
nightly or pre-training gate, not as a per-request check.
No GPU is required; the cost is dominated by linear
transition counting and the per-learner leakage scan on the train fold, after which DAG pruning is cheap relative to even one GKT epoch.

\paragraph{Behavioural versus expert prerequisite curation.}
The ground-truth cross-validation on Junyi (Section~\ref{sec:exp-gtxv}) finds
train-only behavioural inference and expert annotation correlated at the node
level but structurally distinct at the edge level. Behavioural inference is
therefore not a low-cost approximation of expert curation, and results obtained
on one should not be claimed to transfer to the other. The more productive
reading is that the two are complementary relations for a single model to
consume---a setting this paper does not test but does supply the quantitative
basis for proposing.

\paragraph{Implications for graph-KT benchmarking.}
Benchmark tables that omit graph-construction provenance, cold-start strata,
and training-budget disclosure are hard to compare across papers even at
similar aggregate AUC. We do not advocate dismissing reported gains in this
literature; we advocate \emph{disaggregating} them into the part that survives
leakage control, the part that depends on graph-construction choices, and the
part that depends on backbone strength. This protocol supplies the first, the
cold-start diagnostic a partial view of the second, and the third remains open.
Concretely, a new graph-augmented KT model should report at minimum: (a)~the
fold-specific provenance of every retained edge; (b)~the cold-start stratum
where the gain concentrates; (c)~a sequence-only baseline matched in compute
and hyperparameter budget; (d)~ground-truth cross-validation against an expert
annotation where one exists; and (e)~whether accuracy is sensitive to
structure-preserving versus structure-damaging perturbations. Without these, an
aggregate-AUC improvement is difficult to attribute.

\subsection{Threats to Validity and Metric Interpretation}
\label{sec:threats-validity}
Three artefacts invite misreading: the near-null full-log ablation
(Table~\ref{tab:graph-ablation}), aggressive cycle pruning
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-audit}), and scalar leakage metrics that are near-degenerate under learner-disjoint
splits (Table~\ref{tab:leakage-metrics}).

The ablation null shift does \emph{not} negate leakage control: graph
statistics must not pool held-out learners regardless of backbone
sensitivity. Under our filters, pooling rarely changes surviving transitions on
public benchmarks; synthetic logs remain more sensitive. We therefore tier audit
outputs (Section~\ref{sec:formal-problem}): \textbf{primary}---provenance logs,
$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$, and GT cross-validation when available;
\textbf{empirical bounds}---full-log ablation $\Delta$ and cold-start strata;
\textbf{sanity/stress}---$\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{overlap}}_f$, $|\rho_f|$, and
$\mathrm{LTES}_f$. Near-unity overlap and $\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$
are expected under honest learner-disjoint construction and do not replace
provenance or GT audits.

Cycle pruning resolves conflicts by dropping the lowest-weight edge per detected
cycle (Johnson enumeration, capped at 100 witnesses per round); pruned mass
reflects weak or bidirectional transitions rather than arbitrary deletion.

$\mathrm{LTES}_f$ is a within-train temporal stress statistic at a $70\%$
chronological boundary inside each learner's train fold---not cross-split
leakage ($\mathrm{ECR}^{\mathrm{flag}}_f{=}0$). Dense multi-skill
co-assessment on XES3G5M and toy-graph structure on Synthetic~C2 explain the
largest values.

The audit also identified a one-step label misalignment in an early GIKT head
(\path{src/models/gikt.py}), flagged by inconsistent fold AUCs and corrected
before reporting Table~\ref{tab:baseline-cv}.

\paragraph{Limitations.}\label{sec:limitations}
Three-fold CV limits inferential power of fold-level tests ($p_{\min}{=}0.25$ for
Wilcoxon\footnote{With three learner-disjoint folds, the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test has minimum attainable $p{=}0.250$.}).
Primary XES3G5M ranking claims rely on fold-level $\Delta$AUC
magnitudes (Table~\ref{tab:baseline-cv}) and Table~S16 intervals
(\path{scripts/bootstrap_auc_ci.py}; paired-$t$ over three folds by default,
matching fold-level $\Delta$AUC; optional \texttt{--learner-bootstrap} on
exported parquets). The GKT--\textit{simpleKT}
gap is \emph{observational} under attested maximum epoch budgets (30 vs.\ 10; Table~S15).
Due to the substantially greater computational cost of GKT, the
primary GKT configuration used a maximum of 10 epochs, whereas
\textit{simpleKT} used a maximum of 30 epochs. A targeted seed-42
three-fold check evaluated an extended GKT configuration with a
maximum of 30 epochs; its batch size, hidden dimension, and maximum sequence
length also differed from the primary configuration (batch~4 vs.\ batch~32,
and smaller hidden/sequence caps). The observed mean AUC was
$0.003451$ higher under the extended-training configuration, but the
approximate 95\% CI $[-0.004,0.011]$ included zero. Because multiple
hyperparameters changed together, this result should be interpreted as exploratory
configuration sensitivity rather than an isolated epoch effect. The
study does not provide a fully compute-matched comparison. Nine-fold GKT30
artefacts at batch~32 yield a pooled $\Delta$AUC of
${\approx}{-}0.038$ versus \textit{simpleKT} under 30-epoch budgets---directionally
consistent with the primary $-0.041$ gap but still not batch-matched.
Native SKT/DyGKT runs
are wiring diagnostics, not tuned-backbone benchmarks. Controlled injection
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-injection}, Tables~S17--S18) demonstrates metric-level
sensitivity. ASSISTments yields no $\Esim$ under item-overlap Jaccard; GT
cross-validation is Junyi-only at $\theta{=}0.5$; pyKT GKT on Junyi Academy
is omitted because dense multi-million interaction graphs make the stock GKT
wrapper impractical under our compute budget (Tables~S1, \ref{tab:baseline-summary});
downstream \DDR{} retraining covers DGEKT and GKT (the latter with a
manipulation-check anchor), with GKT/XES3G5M reported across three seeds
($\{42,17,1234\}$, nine folds).
The $|{\Delta}\mathrm{AUC}|{\le}0.003$ train-only versus full-log bound holds
on the published public-benchmark ablation and on the three XES3G5M GKT fold-0
cells in Table~\ref{tab:m4-phase-b-auc} (default; open $q$ with $k{=}5$;
$k{=}20$). Builder leak jumps when $k$ is lifted
(Table~\ref{tab:m4-qk-census}); $k{=}\infty$ and additional folds were not
trained once that stop rule held.

\paragraph{Scope of the $2{\times}2$ grid.}
Two limits bound the reading of Supplementary Table~S22.
First, the high-throughput row is reached only by \emph{controlled} injection;
we have not exhibited a natural workflow (absent deliberate contamination) that
raises throughput on these corpora. Closing this gap would require a realistic
mechanism that lifts throughput without hand injection---a looser but commonly
used support filter, a denser-graph corpus, or a re-built legacy graph-KT
pipeline. Second, the reliance column currently rests on a manipulation check
for GKT and GIKT versus \textit{simpleKT} and DGEKT (${\approx}0$). No cell
in the grid shows concentrated AUC harm: the largest injection shift is
$|\Delta\text{AUC}|{\approx}0.010$. The grid is therefore a descriptive
organiser, not a tested throughput$\times$reliance interaction. A matched
\DDR{}$\to$AUC sweep on additional graph-reliant backbones at both throughput
levels would be required before treating that interaction as a claim.

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:future-work}
Three extensions follow naturally from the audit boundary established here.
\textbf{Continuous and online graph updating.} Deployed tutoring systems
accumulate new interactions over time; $G_f$ should be refreshable from
post-deployment logs under explicit temporal cutoffs so that graph updates
do not re-pool held-out or future learners. An online variant would extend
the tiered leakage diagnostics ($\mathrm{ECR}$, $\mathrm{LTES}$) to sliding windows
and report when structural drift warrants retraining versus graph-only
patches.
\textbf{Feedback-aware graph construction.} Behavioural prerequisite
inference currently weights co-occurrence and temporal precedence. Future
work can condition edge retention on learning gain, hint usage, or
remediation outcomes while keeping the train-only provenance contract;
the edge--outcome diagnostic $|\rho|$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho-edge-outcome})
provides a starting point for detecting target-conditional artefacts.
\textbf{Broader backbone and operator coverage.} Re-auditing additional
graph consumers under the same protocol, and testing hierarchy-respecting
augmentations beyond \DDR{}, would clarify whether structural diagnostics
transfer across encoders. A fully compute-matched GKT vs.\ \textit{simpleKT}
comparison (matched maximum epochs \emph{and} batch size on the same folds)
remains open; exploratory GKT30 runs at batch~32 (Table~S21 context) narrow
but do not close this gap.
\textbf{\DDR{} downstream coverage.} The anchored retraining sweep now covers
two graph consumers (DGEKT and GKT), including a completed three-seed GKT
replication on XES3G5M, and establishes the manipulation-check protocol that
separates graph-reliant from graph-inert backbones. Remaining steps are to add a
second graph-reliant backbone at both throughput levels (e.g.\ GIKT), and to pair
the edge-level \DDR{} correlation with the reachability-disruption variant so that
\emph{which} structural property a backbone consumes can be identified, not just
that it consumes one.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}
We presented a leakage-controlled audit protocol for constructing and
evaluating concept graphs in graph-enhanced KT. The protocol comprises a
reporting checklist, provenance artefacts, tiered leakage diagnostics,
\DDR{} augmentation metrics, ground-truth cross-validation where expert graphs
exist, and cold-start KC stratification. On XES3G5M (primary), ASSISTments (secondary), and Junyi (saturated sanity),
the audit produces acyclic train-only prerequisite graphs after transparent
cycle pruning. An anchored downstream retraining test makes the predictive
reading conditional: on a graph-reliant backbone that passes a manipulation
check (GKT on XES3G5M; three seeds) \DDR{} predicts AUC loss (OLS slope
$\DDRslopeXEScore$ AUC per unit \DDR{}, cluster-bootstrap 95\% CI
\DDRslopeXEScoreci, $n{=}\DDRnXEScore$ on $p{\le}0.3$) and the
prerequisite-preserving operator incurs the smallest accuracy loss at matched
budget, whereas on graph-inert cells (DGEKT; GKT on ASSISTments) total
graph destruction moves AUC by ${\le}0.003$.

Train-only versus full-log ablation moves headline AUC by at most $0.003$
on the three public benchmarks, and by at most $0.0015$ on the opened-channel
GKT fold-0 cells (Table~\ref{tab:m4-phase-b-auc}). Under the verified injection sweep, fold-0
AUC remains nearly unchanged (Table~S18) even while builder mass rises
(Table~S17): the largest observed shift is $|\Delta\text{AUC}|{\approx}0.010$.
These results support a bounded negative reading: under the studied
operating regimes, the graph channel is not a reliable accuracy alarm, so
provenance audit is required independently of headline scores. Diagnostic
backbone ordering under unmatched training budgets is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:exp-baseline} and is not a contribution of this work. The
five reporting items in Section~\ref{sec:disc-implications} can be applied
before reporting new graph-augmented KT results. Section~\ref{sec:future-work}
outlines continuous graph updating and feedback-aware construction as next
steps.
Reproduction artefacts are provided as an anonymized supplementary
archive for review.
